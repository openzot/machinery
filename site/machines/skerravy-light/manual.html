Standing Orders
for the Optic Driving & Fog-Signal Machinery

Skerravy Rock Light — Station No. 41 — Established 1868, electrified 1911

Overview

The light of Skerravy is not kept by electricity alone. The great drum lens is turned, as it has been since 1868, by an iron weight of two hundredweight descending the north wall of the tower against a stud chain; the keeper’s hand at the winding crank, not the dynamo, decides whether the character of the light stands through the night. To this the Board has added an electric cell carousel — four carbon-filament lamps on a revolving carrier, which will pass to a fresh cell within three seconds of a filament parting, if the selector stands at AUTO — and a reed fog signal sounded by air from the receivers charged off the main gear train.

Your duties are three, and in this order: that the optic makes its four flashes in forty-five seconds and does not stop; that a light burns behind the lens at every moment of darkness; and that in thick weather the trumpet speaks. Every alarm on the lower strip is a promise that one of these three duties is about to fail unless you move. An alarm ignored is a casualty logged against the station — a vessel reporting no character, a dark light, or a fog casualty off the rock — and the incident cannot be struck out by any keeper.

Controls

WINDING CRANK
The iron crank on the left frame, wooden griped. Hold it down — with the palm or the space bar — and the pawl winds the weight up its full fall of thirty-six feet in about five seconds. The ratchet will be felt clicking. Winding may be done at any time, running or stopped, and should be done whenever the WEIGHT LOW flag threatens.
DRIVE CLUTCH
A lever with two positions. OUT disengages the gear train; the optic coasts to rest and the compressor charges nothing. IN throws the train into gear; the governor carries the optic to its set speed and the weight begins its descent. Press the lever (or press Enter upon it) to move between positions.
GOVERNOR
Ivory knob, marked in seconds per revolution from 30 to 60. The plate figure for Skerravy’s character — Fl. (4) 45s — is 45. Turn with the arrow keys; faster revolutions (lower figures) spend the weight sooner.
OPTIC CELL SELECTOR
Five-position rotary plate above the cell windows: cells I, II, III, IV and AUTO. At AUTO the carousel passes to the next live cell by itself when a filament parts. At any numeral the carousel holds to that cell only — choose it deliberately, and never leave a burned cell selected. Move between positions with the arrow keys.
FOG AIR COCK
Three-position quadrant lever: CHARGE opens the receivers to the belt-driven compressor (which turns only while the train turns); HOLD shuts the receiver valve entirely; BLOW admits air to the reed trumpet, which speaks one blast of 1½ seconds in every four. Below 25 lb/in² the trumpet cannot speak.
FAULT TEST SWITCHES
A covered tray beneath the alarm strip, stencilled KEEP CLOSED. Inside are three switches — GOV. SLIP, LAMP BURN, AIR LEAK — by which the District Engineer simulates the principal casualties. Each raises the true alarms and must be cleared by the true recovery given under Faults and recovery.
BELL SILENCE
Brass push. Stops the hand bell while the flags stand; it does not clear any alarm. The bell rings again of itself when a fresh alarm falls.

Indicators: seven drop-flag annunciators along the lower strip, a ruby jewel beside the nameplate which glows while an alarm stands unacknowledged, a green-hearted jewel showing the train turning, Bourdon dials for air pressure and lamp volts, a six-figure revolution counter, and the sea-visibility scale, whose pointer is set by the lookouts on the rock.

Normal operation

  1. Inspect. See that no cell window shows a burned filament, that the revolution counter agrees with the log, and put the FOG AIR COCK to HOLD.
  2. Wind. Hold the WINDING CRANK until the weight stands at the head of its well — thirty-six feet of fall showing.
  3. Set the character. Leave the GOVERNOR at 45 seconds per revolution.
  4. Engage. Put the DRIVE CLUTCH to IN. The gears take up quietly; the turning jewel lights.
  5. Charge. Once the train turns, set the FOG AIR COCK to CHARGE and watch the receivers rise to at least 170 lb/in², then return the cock to HOLD for fine weather.
  6. Select. Put the OPTIC CELL SELECTOR to AUTO and leave it there.
  7. Watch one revolution. Four flashes should pass the seaward face at even intervals — about eleven seconds apart — and the beam should sweep the mimic.
  8. In thick weather, when the visibility pointer reaches FOG or DENSE, put the FOG AIR COCK to BLOW and keep the receivers above 130 lb/in² by returning occasionally to CHARGE.
  9. Before the WEIGHT LOW flag falls, hold the crank and bring the weight to the top again. A careful keeper winds at the half.

Alarms

Flag Meaning What the keeper does
WEIGHT LOW Fewer than nine feet of fall remain. Hold the WINDING CRANK until the weight is at the top.
OPTIC STOPPED The clutch stands in but the weight is spent and the optic is at rest. Wind at once; the optic will pick up its speed as the weight takes the load.
GOVERNOR OVERSPEED The optic is turning above thirteen-tenths of her set speed. Throw the DRIVE CLUTCH to OUT and see Faults and recovery, governor slip.
LAMP FAILED A filament has parted; the flag stands latched until accepted. See the fresh cell alight, choose it if the carousel has not, then accept with BELL SILENCE.
NO SPARE CELL All four filaments are gone. Show the station DARK at the masts and send for the boat; nothing else avails.
AIR PRESSURE LOW The receivers stand below 130 lb/in². Engage the train and charge; in fog weigh the duty against the reserve.
FOG SILENT Dense weather is on the rock and the trumpet is not speaking. Put the cock to BLOW; if the gauge will not support it, charge first and say so in the log.

A flag falls when its condition arises and lifts when the condition clears. The ruby jewel burns while anything stands unacknowledged; the BELL SILENCE push quiets the bell only. If OPTIC STOPPED stands three-quarters of a minute, or the light stands dark, or the trumpet silent past two minutes in fog, the incident is logged ashore and the Superintendent will have the papers.

Faults and recovery

Governor slip

You see: the optic races, flashes crowding together, GOVERNOR OVERSPEED fallen, the weight sinking at twice its rate.

  1. Throw the DRIVE CLUTCH to OUT; let her coast to rest.
  2. Open the FAULT TEST SWITCHES tray and switch GOV. SLIP off; the governor is reseated.
  3. Confirm the GOVERNOR knob still reads 45.
  4. Re-engage the clutch and wind the weight up full.

Filament burnout

You see: a brief darkness behind the lens, LAMP FAILED fallen; at AUTO the carousel passes to the next cell within three seconds and the flag lifts.

  1. Confirm a fresh cell window glowing and the volts dial standing near 14.
  2. If the selector held a numeral, turn it to AUTO or to a live cell yourself.
  3. Open the FAULT TEST SWITCHES tray and switch LAMP BURN off when the burned cell has been changed ashore.

Air main leak

You see: the receivers falling fast while the cock stands off HOLD, slowly even at HOLD — the main weeps past the valve — then AIR PRESSURE LOW, and in fog FOG SILENT.

  1. Put the FOG AIR COCK to HOLD at once; only the weep remains against the receivers.
  2. See the train turning, then give short spells of CHARGE until the gauge reads 170 or better.
  3. In fog work between BLOW and CHARGE, watching that the trumpet keeps speaking above 25 lb/in².
  4. When the joint is caulked, switch AIR LEAK off in the FAULT TEST SWITCHES tray.

Specifications

Optic Drum lens, 500 mm, four panels, focus 12 ft
Character Fl. (4) 45s — four flashes each revolution
Revolution period Adjustable 30–60 s; plate figure 45 s
Driving weight 224 lb cast iron; fall 36 ft on 1⅜ in stud chain
Weight endurance Nominal eight minutes per full fall at 45 s
Winding rate Full fall in five seconds at the crank
Lamps Four carbon-filament cells, 13.8 V 60 A, rated 1,200 hours
Changeover AUTO pass to next live cell within 3 s
Air receivers Two of 9 cu ft, 200 lb/in² working, charged off the main train at 14 lb/in² per minute
Fog signal Reed trumpet; blast 1½ s in every 4 s; cut-off below 25 lb/in²
Alarm bell 8 in hand bell, geared striker, silenced at the push
Makers J. Cuningham & Sons, Engineers, Leith, 1924 — machine No. 7
