MET OFFICE · ENGINEERING BRANCH · INSTRUCTIONAL PAMPHLET

MANUAL M.61

HYDROGEN SHED & SOUNDING POSITION No. 1
TARSKERRY UPPER-AIR STATION

Clyde Valley Electrolytic Co. of Motherwell, machine No. 61/0489, to the order of the Meteorological Office, London. For the instruction of observer candidates. Two shillings and sixpence. Nothing in this pamphlet relieves an observer of the standing orders on naked flame, and on hydrogen.

Overview

The shed makes its own lifting gas and sends up the day's radiosonde. A banked electrolytic generator splits water into hydrogen and oxygen; the hydrogen collects above the seal of a floating-bell holder until it is wanted; a twelve-hundred-gram balloon is seated on the brass nozzle and inflated against a counterweighted balance to her free lift; and at the sounding window the release key is turned and the balloon rises through the roof hatch, carrying the sonde whose signals you follow on the theodolite until she bursts.

Three things are always true in this shed. The gas is made only as fast as the stack can be cooled. The balloon goes nowhere without her free lift, measured on the balance and not guessed. And the window waits for nobody: the times are fixed by the forecast office, a sounding missed is a sounding lost, and the log remembers.

This rig is fitted for fast-time ascent so that a full flight may be followed in a few minutes of instruction. Do not expect the altimeter of a real ascent to move so briskly; do expect the same habits from yourself.

Controls

GENERATOR SWITCH
A brass knife switch on the slate panel. Blade down-out is OFF; seated home between the clips is RUN. It will refuse to hold in while the purge interlock lamp shows LOCKED OUT, or while the stack is still hotter than 75 °C after a thermal trip.
FIELD RHEOSTAT
The large crank knob sets demanded stack current from 0 to 480 amperes. Turn upwards for more current. Watch the STACK CURRENT meter and nothing else while you turn it.
CELL BANK SELECTOR
Three positions: BANKS OUT, ONE BANK, TWO BANKS. Each bank engaged nearly doubles the gas made for a given current. TWO BANKS is the working position; BANKS OUT must be selected before switching off for any prolonged stop.
COOLING WATER COCK
A quarter-turn lever: SHUT, ½, OPEN. The little sight glass beside it shows the flow. The stack is cooled by this water alone; OPEN before you close the generator switch, every time.
PURGE VALVE
A red mushroom button. Hold it pressed for five seconds to sweep air-diluted gas from the holder and lines to atmosphere. It is the only key that will clear the LOCKED OUT interlock once the explosive-mixture lamp has dropped.
HOLDER VENT LEVER
Move right to TO AIR to waste holder gas through the roof vent; left to SEALED for all normal running. Use it to bring a full holder down before it lifts its safety.
FILLING VALVE
The long handled lever on the quadrant beside the nozzle stand. SHUT is at rest; over to FILLING admits holder gas to the balloon. Never leave it at FILLING unattended.
NECK CLAMP
The T-screw that grips the balloon neck onto the nozzle. Screw down CLAMPED before opening the filling valve; a neck that is free is a neck that slips.
NEW BALLOON
The brass plunger. Press to seat a fresh twelve-hundred-gram balloon on the nozzle. It does nothing while a balloon is already mounted or in flight.
BALANCE CRANK
Sets the target index on the free-lift dial between 700 and 900 grams. The forecast office states the free lift required; set it before filling begins.
RELEASE KEY
Under a red guard. Lift the guard, and when the WINDOW card shows open and the balance needle stands at or beyond the target index, turn the barrel. The hatch opens and she is away. The interlock refuses the key outside the window, or below target lift, and tells you which.
TRACKING CRANK
Swings the theodolite crosswire ±60 degrees to keep the rising sonde on the vertical hair. The ON TARGET counter records your share of the flight followed truly.
ALARM FLAG RESET
Lifts the annunciator flags — but only those whose conditions have cleared. A flag that will not stay up is telling the truth; answer the condition first.
WORKS TEST SWITCHES
A covered plate of three switches used to demonstrate faults on this training rig: CROSSOVER, WATER LOSS and NECK SLIP. They reproduce oxygen crossover, cooling water failure, and a balloon neck slipping off the nozzle respectively. Leave them all down-out after demonstration.
SOUND CUT
CUT silences the gas hiss entirely. Put to LIVE, the shed may be heard: the stack bubbles quietly, and the fill and purge speak for themselves. Sound follows a touch of the controls, never otherwise.

Normal operation

  1. Walk the shed. Confirm no naked flame, no oil, no loose cordage near the hatch. Works test switches down-out; sound cut to taste.
  2. Open the COOLING WATER COCK to OPEN and see water standing in the sight glass.
  3. Set the CELL BANK SELECTOR to TWO BANKS. Leave the FIELD RHEOSTAT at zero.
  4. Close the GENERATOR SWITCH. The stack current meter reads zero still; that is correct.
  5. Raise the FIELD RHEOSTAT in steps of about eighty amperes, pausing at each step, until the meter stands at 400–480 A. The STACK temperature will settle in the forties.
  6. Watch the gas holder fill. Her bell carriage climbs the scale; the draught gauge breathes a little above four millimetres. If the HOLDER OVERFULL flag drops, open the HOLDER VENT LEVER until she is below two-and-a-quarter cubic metres, then seal again.
  7. Press NEW BALLOON to seat a fresh balloon. Set the BALANCE CRANK to the ordered free lift — eight hundred grams is the usual figure.
  8. Screw the NECK CLAMP down CLAMPED. Verify by eye that the neck cannot walk off the nozzle.
  9. Pull the FILLING VALVE to FILLING. The balance needle climbs as she swells. Top up the holder meanwhile if the bell falls low; a slow fill is better than a starved one.
  10. When the needle reaches the target index shut the FILLING VALVE. She now hangs at her free lift, straining gently upward.
  11. At the window — the card will show WINDOW OPEN — lift the red guard and turn the RELEASE KEY. The hatch leaves swing clear and she rises. Close the guard at once.
  12. Follow her on the TRACKING CRANK, keeping the sonde's dot on the vertical hair. Note altitude and range at each whole kilometre if logging.
  13. At burst, read RESULT. GOOD means the flight was followed throughout; enter it, the burst height and the mean bearing in the log.
  14. Shut down: rheostat to zero, CELL BANK SELECTOR to BANKS OUT, GENERATOR SWITCH OFF, and last of all the COOLING WATER COCK to SHUT.

Alarms

EXPLOSIVE MIXTURE
Gas purity has fallen below ninety-five and a half per cent. The generator locks itself out at once. Do not try the switch. Stop, seal everything, and purge as under Faults.
STACK OVERHEAT
The stack has passed eighty-two degrees Celsius. Reduce current or prove the cooling water. Ignored, the thermal trip fires at one hundred and eight degrees and the shift loses a quarter hour waiting for the stack to cool below seventy-five.
WATER MAINS OFF
The cooling main has lost pressure — on this rig, by demonstration switch or by failure. The stack heats uncooled within the minute. Take current off at once and find your water.
NECK SLIPPED
The balloon neck is free of the nozzle and lift is leaving her. Shut the filling valve before anything else; then re-clamp as under Faults.
HOLDER OVERFULL
The bell stands at the top of her travel. The safety wastes gas from here on. Open the HOLDER VENT LEVER deliberately and bring her down; a lifted safety is a loud neighbour.
SOUNDING MISSED
A window closed without a launch. The forecast office is informed by the log, whether or not the excuse is a good one. Acknowledge with ALARM FLAG RESET and make certain the next window finds you ready.

Faults and recovery

Oxygen crossover

FAULT: oxygen crossover — the cell membrane leaks and oxygen passes into the product gas. What you see: the purity falls steadily, the EXPLOSIVE MIXTURE flag drops, the LOCKED OUT lamp lights and the generator stops itself. On this rig the condition can be demonstrated from the WORKS TEST SWITCHES.

  1. Leave the GENERATOR SWITCH alone; it is already out by interlock.
  2. Open the HOLDER VENT LEVER to TO AIR.
  3. Hold the PURGE VALVE pressed a full five seconds — watch the second hand, not your opinion.
  4. Release, seal the vent, and only then try the GENERATOR SWITCH. She will hold in once purity is above ninety-eight and a half.
  5. If the crossover was a demonstration, put the test switch down-out; if it was real, the machine stays on ONE BANK and low current until the fitter has seen it.

Cooling water failure

FAULT: cooling water failure — the stack cooling main drains. What you see: the WATER MAINS OFF flag drops immediately, the sight glass empties, and with the generator on line the stack temperature climbs fast towards the thermal trip. Demonstrable from the WORKS TEST SWITCHES.

  1. Pull the FIELD RHEOSTAT smartly to zero. Current off is worth more than water on late.
  2. Restore the water: on this rig, put the WATER LOSS test switch down-out, then work the COOLING WATER COCK to OPEN and see the glass stand.
  3. If the stack tripped on temperature, wait for the needle to fall below seventy-five before closing the GENERATOR SWITCH again.
  4. Bring the current back in steps and watch the temperature settle before resuming the fill.

Balloon neck slips off the nozzle

FAULT: balloon neck slips off the nozzle — the clamp was never made up, or the test switch pulled it. What you see: the NECK SLIPPED flag drops and the balance needle falls away even with the filling valve open; the balloon slackens on the stand. Demonstrable from the WORKS TEST SWITCHES.

  1. Shut the FILLING VALVE first — gas straight to shed air profits nobody.
  2. Put the NECK SLIP test switch down-out if she was demonstrated.
  3. Screw the NECK CLAMP fully CLAMPED and pull the neck by hand to be sure.
  4. Re-open the FILLING VALVE and nurse her back to the target index. There is usually time if the holder was kept up; there is not, if it was not.
  5. If she emptied altogether, press NEW BALLOON and begin the fill again, and let the log show the second balloon.

Specifications

Ratings and limits — Machine No. 61/0489
Generator type Filter-press electrolytic, two cell banks, series/parallel
Supply 230 V DC from the station charger
Stack current, working 400–480 A, both banks
Gas output at 480 A approximately 1·7 cubic metres per hour per bank engaged
Gas purity, new 99·7 per cent hydrogen
Purity, alarm limit 95½ per cent — explosive mixture lockout
Stack temperature, working 35–55 °C with cooling water OPEN
Stack temperature, trip 108 °C; restart permitted below 75 °C
Holder capacity 2·5 m³ floating bell, water-sealed
Holder draught 4 mm water gauge at zero make, 30 mm at full make
Balloon 1200 g latex, nominal free lift 800 g (limits 700–900 g)
Filling time, typical 90–150 seconds from a held holder at TWO BANKS
Sounding windows every ten minutes of rig time, open ninety seconds
Ascent, simulated fast-time climb to burst at 34 km
Theodolite follow ±60 degrees of bearing on the tracking crank; on-target within 6 degrees
