NORTHERN SUPPLY BOARD · WORKS DEPARTMENT

Corriedhu Grid Supply Point

OPERATING INSTRUCTION MI/CGP/2 · ISSUE 3 · AUG 1994 · PANEL REF CGP/2 · FOR OPERATION OF THE 132/33 kV CONTROL & INDICATION PANEL

Overview

Corriedhu Grid Supply Point takes bulk power from the 132 kV network by two circuits, Teindland and Cairnbeath, and feeds it into the local 33 kV system through two 45 MVA transformers fitted with on-load tap changers. The 33 kV busbar is run split: Bus A feeds feeders F1 to F3 from TX1, Bus B feeds feeders F4 to F6 from TX2, and the bus section between them is left open. This panel is the control point for every circuit breaker on the site, for both tap changers, and for the alarm annunciator. It was assembled by the Board's Works Department around Calderbank Switchgear gearwork, with telemetry RTU 7 retrofitted in 1993.

The mimic diagram at the centre of the panel is worked live: red breaker symbols are closed, green are open, solid lines are alive and dashed lines are dead. Read the mimic before you touch anything, and leave it telling the truth after you have finished.

Controls

All switches are operable from the desk by hand and from the keyboard when focused: a control switch CLOSES on ENTER, SPACE or RIGHT ARROW, and TRIPS on LEFT ARROW (or right-click); an OLTC rocker RAISES on ENTER or UP ARROW and LOWERS on DOWN ARROW; rotary selectors turn one position per click or arrow press.

Normal operation

  1. Panel cold: every breaker open, mimic dashed, taps reading +0. Press LAMPS TEST PUSHBUTTON and prove every window and lamp.
  2. Turn the SYNCHRO-CHECK SELECTOR to ON. The synchroscope is now live whenever an incoming circuit stands either side of an open breaker.
  3. Command the TEINDLAND CONTROL SWITCH to CLOSE. With the bus dead the synchro-check admits the close at once; the 132 kV busbar goes solid and the voltmeter should read 140–142 kV.
  4. Command the TX1 CONTROL SWITCH to CLOSE. Watch HV close, then LV a second behind it; Bus A comes alive and the transformer begins to hum.
  5. Command the TX2 CONTROL SWITCH to CLOSE. Bus B comes alive. Leave CAIRNBEATH open as the reserve circuit.
  6. Turn the VOLTAGE REGULATOR SELECTOR to AUTO. Within two or three tap movements the halves should settle at 32.5–33.5 kV.
  7. Close FEEDER 1 through FEEDER 6 in order, watching each half's loading stay inside about 40 MVA (the ammeters read roughly 700 A at full rated current).
  8. Standing watch: winding temperatures settling below 80°C, no windows lit, RTU scanning. At the evening peak expect 55–65 MW total demand shared evenly across the two units.

Alarms

Window Means Do
TEINDLAND LINE Teindland breaker open on protection or out of service. Confirm reserve; reclose per section 5, fault 3.
TX1 GAS Buchholz gas in the TX1 conservator: stage 1 alarm, escalating if ignored. Section 5, fault 1. Do not reset and walk away.
TX1 WDG TEMP TX1 winding hot-spot above 115°C. Reduce loading; a trip follows at 140°C.
TX OVERLOAD A unit above 105% continuous rating. Shift or shed load; mind the remaining unit.
33KV U/V A bus half below 90% for four seconds. Trim taps or find the lost source; below 45% the feeders release themselves.
TX2 OLTC JAM Tapchanger drive seized mid-movement. Section 5, fault 2.
SYNC BLOCK A close was attempted outside the synchronism window, or with the check off. Wait for the scope and try again properly.
SUPPLY FAIL Both 33 kV halves dead after having been in service. Rebuild per section 4; find why before reclosing everything.

Faults and recovery

MAINTENANCE FLAP: the FAULT TEST BUTTONS exist so the duty operator can prove the panel. They put real conditions on real plant. Recoveries follow.

1. Transformer gas accumulation (TX1)

You see: TX1 GAS flashing almost as soon as the relay test is pressed Ignored, gas keeps rising and the Buchholz surge contact operates within about half a minute: TX1 trips, Bus A dies, and its feeders drop off on undervoltage release. The whole site then hangs on TX2, which will overload at teatime demand and eventually trip on winding temperature.

Recover: ACCEPT the alarm. Open the TX1 CONTROL SWITCH to TRIP (both breakers). Close the 33kV BUS SECTION CONTROL SWITCH to backfeed Bus A from TX2 — the parallel interlock allows this because only one half is live. Re-close only the feeders that must run, and shed the rest so TX2 stays under about 42 MVA. With TX1 proven isolated (both breakers open), open the maintenance flap and press GAS RELAY RESET; the window extinguishes on ALARM RESET PUSHBUTTON once the condition has cleared. TX1 may then be returned to service and the section opened again.

2. Tap changer mechanism jam (TX2)

You see: TX2 OLTC JAM lit immediately; further raise or lower commands, automatic or local, are refused and logged. Bus B voltage drifts away from 33 kV because the AUTO regulator can no longer trim TX2.

Recover: take the VOLTAGE REGULATOR SELECTOR to MANUAL (or OFF) so the auto sequence stops flogging a seized drive. Hold Bus B voltage as best you can with local control where available, then press OLTC OVERRIDE on the maintenance flap — the drive resets and the window clears on the next ALARM RESET PUSHBUTTON. Return the selector to AUTO. The override is deliberately refused while the selector stands at AUTO.

3. Incomer line trip (Teindland)

You see: TEINDLAND LINE flashing, the Teindland breaker green on the mimic, the 132 kV busbar dead if Cairnbeath was standing open. Everything downstream collapses unless the reserve circuit is brought in.

Recover: confirm SYNCHRO-CHECK SELECTOR at ON — the check refuses any incomer close without it and raises SYNC BLOCK. If the bus is being carried by Cairnbeath, watch the synchroscope: command TEINDLAND CONTROL SWITCH to CLOSE only as the rotor passes twelve o'clock, inside the ±18° window; outside it the sync-check blocks you and says so. On a dead bus the close is admitted at once. The window extinguishes when the circuit restores; RESET clears it.
