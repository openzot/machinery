BEP-3 REACTOR — OPERATING MANUAL

Control Console BEP3/CON/58, Panel 2 of 4 · Issued by the Instrument Workshop · 1958

Overview

BEP-3 is a 5 MW(thermal) heavy-water moderated and cooled research reactor. This console puts the whole of the reactor under one operator’s hands: the electrical supplies that feed it, the nucleonic instruments that watch it, and the four control rods that govern it. The console was designed and built in the Establishment’s own Instrument Workshop; it is arranged left to right in the order you use it — supplies first, instruments centre, rods to the right — so that a start-up follows the desk from one end to the other.

The reactor is never made critical quickly. The rule of this panel is printed on every trainee’s notebook: watch the PERIOD meter, not the pointer. A doubling time of twenty seconds is brisk; eight seconds trips the machine and earns you an interview with the Superintendent. The moderator temperature does the fine arithmetic for you once the plant is hot: as the fuel heats, reactivity falls away, and the regulating rod exists only to trim what physics leaves over.

Three defects account for most lost time on this console: a slipping shim-rod drive clutch, tripping of the coolant blower breaker, and slow drift of the ion chamber. Each has a test switch on the works-test plate under its hinged cover and a recovery procedure in Section 5. Learn them before you need them.

Controls

  • CONTROL KEY SWITCH — two-position key switch, OFF / RUN. RUN energises the control bus; the ivory jewel lights. With the key OFF nothing else on the desk will answer.
  • MODE SELECTOR — three-position rotary: OFF / MANUAL / AUTO. MANUAL permits rod motion from the rockers; AUTO hands the regulating rod to the servo, which trims power towards the POWER DEMAND setting.
  • BLOWER SELECTOR — three-position rotary: OFF / LOW / HIGH. Drives the D2O circulators; LOW gives 62 % flow, HIGH the full 100 . Allow two minutes after changing before trusting the reading.
  • ROD DRIVE SUPPLY — guarded toggle under a striped flip-up cover. Energises the rod drive motors and picks up the safety-rod magnet. No rod moves without it.
  • SHIM A ROD CONTROL / SHIM B ROD CONTROL / REGULATING ROD CONTROL — spring-return rockers: RAISE held drives the rod out, LOWER drives it in. Release and the rod stops where it is. The regulating rocker is deliberately slower than the shims.
  • POWER DEMAND — knurled knob, 10 to 110 % of full power. In AUTO the servo trims the regulating rod to hold this figure. Drag the knob or use the arrow keys.
  • DETECTOR CALIBRATE — push button, hold three seconds. Runs the chamber check and re-zeros the LOG POWER channel after drift.
  • DRIVE BREAKER RESET — recloses the shim-drive clutch breaker after a slip.
  • BLOWER RESET — recloses the circulator overload breaker after a trip.
  • COUNT-RATE SET — toggle feeding the count-rate loudspeaker above the selectors. Clicks follow neutron population; switch off before it maddens you.
  • MAINTENANCE TEST SWITCHES — hinged cover, KEEP DOWN IN SERVICE. Beneath are three test switches which inject, for drill: a shim drive clutch slip, a blower breaker trip, and ion chamber drift.
  • SCRAM BAR — the crimson bar across the desk edge. One movement drops every rod home by gravity and unlatches nothing. There is no disgrace in using it; there is disgrace in hesitating.
  • TRIP RESET — clears the scram latch after a trip, but only when every rod reads zero and fuel temperature is below 200 °C. It will refuse otherwise, and it is right to.
  • ALARM ACCEPT — steadies the flashing annunciators; the window stays lit while its condition persists.
  • LAMPS TEST — hold to light every annunciator window and jewel at once.

Normal operation

  1. Console cold: verify all rods read 0 %, MODE SELECTOR at OFF, BLOWER SELECTOR at OFF, ROD DRIVE SUPPLY covered and down.
  2. Turn CONTROL KEY SWITCH to RUN; the ivory CONTROL SUPPLY jewel lights.
  3. Raise the guard and switch ROD DRIVE SUPPLY on. The SAFETY rod drum winds out to 100 % as the magnet picks it up.
  4. Set BLOWER SELECTOR to HIGH and confirm D²O FLOW reaches 95 % or better before any rod moves further.
  5. Set MODE SELECTOR to MANUAL.
  6. Hold RAISE on SHIM A ROD CONTROL in short bursts to about 60 %. After each burst read the PERIOD meter: keep it beyond twenty seconds. If it falls below fifteen, stop and let the instruments settle.
  7. Repeat with SHIM B ROD CONTROL. Power will now be climbing on the LOG POWER meter; the count-rate loudspeaker, if switched in, quickens.
  8. Near the working point, take fine trim with the REGULATING ROD CONTROL and bring power to the neighbourhood of your intended figure.
  9. Set POWER DEMAND to the required output (80 % is typical), then turn MODE SELECTOR to AUTO. The CRITICAL jewel burns steady and the servo walks the regulating rod to hold demand.
  10. Log the chart, the fuel temperature and the flow. The run is now normal: fuel temperature settles near 350 °C at full flow, and no annunciator windows are lit.

Alarms

Window / lamp Means Action
REACTOR SCRAM (red) All rods have dropped; the latch is set. Find the cause before resetting. See Section 5 and TRIP RESET conditions.
SHORT PERIOD Indicated period below 15 s while power is significant. Trip follows at 8 s. Insert the last rod moved; do not withdraw anything until the needle calms.
HIGH FLUX (red) Thermal power above rated 5 MW. Trim in the regulating rod, or a shim if the trim is insufficient. Trip at 118 %.
HIGH FUEL TEMPERATURE Fuel above 380 °C. Trip at 420 °C. Check blower selector and flow first; then reduce power.
LOW COOLANT FLOW D²O flow below 45 % with the plant demanding cooling. Restore the blower. If power exceeds 1 MW the reactor trips itself after eight seconds; beat it there.
ROD DRIVE FAULT A shim drive clutch has slipped; that rod ignores its rocker. Press DRIVE BREAKER RESET. Run on the remaining rods meanwhile.
FLUX MONITOR FAULT The ion chamber has drifted more than 22 %; LOG POWER and PERIOD are not trustworthy. Hold DETECTOR CALIBRATE for three seconds. Until then, steer by fuel temperature.
BLOWER BREAKER The circulator overload has tripped; flow decays regardless of selector. Reduce power, press BLOWER RESET, restore the selector.
CONTROL SUPPLY (white jewel) Control bus energised. If dark, the key is OFF or the bus has failed; nothing else will work.
CRITICAL (green jewel) Net reactivity balanced and power above source range. —

Faults and recovery

1. Shim rod drive failure

You see: SHIM A freezes wherever it stood; its rocker clicks but the drum does not move. The ROD DRIVE FAULT window flashes and, once accepted, burns steady. Injected for drill by the first works-test switch.

Recovery: (a) cease operating SHIM A; repeated rocking only heats the clutch. (b) Hold power steady using SHIM B and the regulating rod — remember SHIM A’s worth is still in the core, so you have less pull-up margin. (c) Press DRIVE BREAKER RESET; the recloser snaps the clutch back in and the window extinguishes. (d) Prove the repair with one short RAISE and return the rod to its band.

Coolant blower trip

You see: the BLOWER BREAKER window lights whatever the selector position; D²O FLOW runs down to zero over about twenty seconds. If the reactor is above 1 MW, LOW COOLANT FLOW joins it and the eight-second clock starts towards a fuel-temperature trip. Injected for drill by the second works-test switch.

Recovery: (a) cut power hard — insert both shims, or use the SCRAM BAR without ceremony if flow is already below half. (b) Press BLOWER RESET; the breaker recloses. (c) Restore BLOWER SELECTOR to HIGH and watch flow recover. (d) After a scram, reset the trip when the rods are seated and restart per Section 3.

Ion chamber drift

You see: LOG POWER creeps upward against a steady plant — several percent a minute — and the PERIOD needle wanders though nothing was touched. When the error passes about 22 %, FLUX MONITOR FAULT announces that you are effectively blind. Injected for drill by the third works-test switch.

Recovery: (a) distrust the meters at once; steer by fuel temperature and flow, which are independent of the chamber. (b) Hold DETECTOR CALIBRATE for a full three seconds — the amber ring fills as the check runs — and release. The channel re-zeros and both windows clear. (c) Compare LOG POWER with the fuel-temperature balance before resuming rod motion.
