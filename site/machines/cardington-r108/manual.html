Overview

The R.108 keeps her place in the air by arithmetic alone. Lift is stored in three thousand cubic feet of hydrogen held in fore, midship and stern cells; weight is carried as water ballast in two tanks beneath the control car and as petrol in the wing walkways. The difference between the two — the static condition of the ship — decides whether she rises, settles, or holds her height, and it is the flight engineer's whole business to keep that difference at nothing.

This Board gathers the business into one panel. The sight glasses show the water and petrol remaining; the wheels and lever move that water about or throw it away; the selector and guarded vent lever let gas go when the ship is light and must be made heavy; the inclinometer, balance scale and hull section show what the ship is doing while you do it. Water, once dropped, may be recovered at the next mooring only if a friendly rain squall obliges. Gas, once valved, is gone for good. Bear both facts in mind in that order.

Controls

FORE BALLAST DUMP VALVE / AFT BALLAST DUMP VALVE
Six-turn hand wheels, one above each tank's sight glass. Turning a wheel open drops water from that tank at up to about 330 lb a minute at six turns. Turn anticlockwise to the stop to shut. Each turn is one sixth of full flow; a quarter turn is enough to arrest a gentle rise.
TRIM PUMP LEVER
Three positions: FORE, HOLD, AFT. The pump moves water between the tanks without losing an ounce — ninety pounds a minute — so the nose may be raised or lowered at no cost in static condition. HOLD leaves the water where it lies.
GAS CELL SELECTOR
Four-position rotary knob choosing which cells are led to the vent stack: SHUT, BOW CELLS, MIDSHIP CELLS, STERN CELLS. Turning the selector alone lets nothing escape.
GAS VENT LEVER
Crimson lever under a hinged guard. Lift the guard before the lever will answer. Latching the lever with the selector open valves about five per cent of the chosen cell's contents a minute — roughly seven hundred pounds of lift. Close the lever the moment the balance crosses zero.
ELEVATOR HANDWHEEL
Wheel on the forward rim of the attitude bay, twenty-five degrees either side of amidships. The elevators bite in proportion to air speed and hold the nose against momentary imbalance; they are a courtesy to the trim, not a substitute for it. Double-click returns the wheel to amidships.
ENGINE TELEGRAPH
SLOW, HALF, FULL. The crimson pointer is your order; the blued pointer is the mechanics' answer, arriving some six seconds later by way of the telegraph wires and a man with a ladder. Petrol burns fastest at FULL.
ALARM BELL CUT
Silences the alarm bell for the alarms showing when pressed. A new alarm rings again regardless. The bell never acknowledges a fault — only your having seen it.
LAMPS TEST
Press to drop every shutter and light the pilot jewel. Any shutter that will not drop in test will not drop in earnest either; report it.
MAINTENANCE TESTS
Hinged tray below the annunciators. The INJECT column puts each service fault onto the board for drill; the REPAIR column holds the remedy for each and answers only while its fault is present. See Faults and recovery.
COLD RESET
Returns the Board and the simulated ship to the standing patrol condition: fifteen hundred feet, even keel, tanks at four-fifths, petrol full, engines answering HALF, every shutter raised. For use after a grounding or at the end of a drill.

Normal operation

  1. Press LAMPS TEST. Every shutter drops and the ruby pilot jewel lights; raise nothing until you have seen all seven shutters fall.
  2. Read the balance scale. At the standing patrol condition the pointer sits within a hand's breadth of zero and the drum reads between plus and minus two hundred pounds.
  3. Order the engines HALF on the ENGINE TELEGRAPH and wait for the blued answer pointer to follow the crimson one.
  4. Confirm the cell pressure gauges both sit above twenty-five millimetres and the superheat reads within five degrees of its reference.
  5. Watch the balance through the first ten minutes. Warming sunshine makes the ship light; a cloud shadow makes her heavy. Correct small differences with the TRIM PUMP LEVER for keel and a quarter turn of a dump valve for height.
  6. If the ship runs persistently heavy and the tanks cannot be spared, lead a cell group to the vent with the GAS CELL SELECTOR, lift the guard, and latch the GAS VENT LEVER while watching the balance. Unlatch at zero.
  7. Keep the inclinometer inside its red marks. Use the ELEVATOR HANDWHEEL for gusts; use the TRIM PUMP LEVER for truth.
  8. Before handing over: balance at zero, both glasses above half, petrol sufficient for the return leg, no shutter down, bell cut released.

Alarms

Shutter Meaning Action
STATIC HEAVY The ship weighs more than her lift by over seven hundred pounds; she is settling. Drop water — quarter turn at a time — or valve a cell group if the tanks are low.
STATIC LIGHT Lift exceeds weight by over seven hundred pounds; she is rising against the mooring wires' limit. Valve gas with the selector, guard and vent lever; do not hoard water you may need at night.
TRIM LIMIT Pitch has passed five and a half degrees. Bring the ELEVATOR HANDWHEEL towards amidships and send water the short way with the TRIM PUMP LEVER. Sustained neglect strains the hull.
GAS PRESSURE A cell gauge has fallen below twenty-two millimetres — a leaking valve, or worse. Read both gauges. If fore: suspect the cell (see Faults). If after venting: shut the vent and let the cells settle.
BALLAST LOW Fewer than nine hundred pounds of water remain between both tanks. Stop dumping. Valve gas instead, and make for the mast before the next sunset makes her heavier still.
PUMP TRIP The trim pump motor has overloaded and is out of circuit. Trim by asymmetric dumping until the breaker can be reset (see Faults).
GROUND CONTACT The ship has been flown into the ground, or held past the hull's pitch limit long enough to strain her. She is done for this watch. Nothing will answer but COLD RESET, and afterwards the Board Secretary's report.

The ruby pilot jewel beside the maker's plate shows the Board alive. In the LAMPS TEST it burns bright white; if it will not light, the Board is dead and so, shortly, might you be — find the fuses before finding altitude.

Faults and recovery

a. Gas cell puncture

You see: the FORE cell pressure gauge sagging towards the red, the GAS PRESSURE shutter down, the bow cell drawn pale in the hull section, and the ship growing heavy minute by minute as lift leaks into the envelope.

To clear: open the MAINTENANCE TESTS tray and press PATCH CELL. The rigger's patch party re-seats the tail of the fore cell; pressure climbs back as the neighbouring cells share their gas, and the shutter rises. Until patched, feed the heaviness with ballast you can spare.

b. Forward dump valve passing

You see: the FORE sight glass falling although its wheel is shut, the bow rising, and presently the STATIC LIGHT shutter. A passing seat is losing about a hundred and fifty pounds of water a minute.

To clear: press RESEAT VALVE in the maintenance tray; the lapping is ground in and the leak stops. Meanwhile hold the trim with the pump, sending water aft to keep the inclinometer honest, and be ready to valve gas if the lightness outruns you.

c. Trim pump overload

You see: the PUMP TRIP shutter down at once; the TRIM PUMP LEVER moves the quadrant but no water changes tanks; the balance drifts as it pleases.

To clear: let the motor cool a moment, then press RESET PUMP BREAKER in the tray. Until reset, trim by dropping from whichever tank opposes the nose — clumsy, but the R.101's log shows worse done calmly.
