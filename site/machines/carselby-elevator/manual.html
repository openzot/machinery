Overview

This panel runs the leg house: the receiving pit, the bucket elevator (“the leg”), the track scale, the rotary distributor that feeds the six annex bins, the continuous-flow grain dryer, and the cyclone that keeps the dust down. Grain is augered in at the pit, lifted ninety feet by steel cups on a belt, weighed on the head, dried to grade if it needs it, and turned out into whichever annex bin you have chosen.

A leg house is a friendly place until it isn't. Grain dust in the air will burn like gunpowder given half a chance, a dry bearing will seize a full-speed belt in half a minute, and a boot left to flood will stall a motor that costs the co-op a day's ration of patience. Read this booklet, mind the annunciator, and the plant will lift six thousand bushels an hour from now until threshing supper.

The panel is arranged as the building stands: pit and boot at the foot of the centre drawing, the leg rising through it, scale and distributor at the head, bins down the right-hand side, dryer and dust plant at the far right. Everything below the brass rail is maintenance men's business and is guarded or keyed accordingly.

Controls

MAIN BREAKER
Red lever in the black slot, top left. Pull up for ON, push down for OFF. Energises the panel, the leg motor starter and the dryer fuel solenoid. The SUPPLY jewel lights when it is in.
PIT GATE
Brass quadrant lever under the pit grate. Eleven notches; each notch feeds twelve bushels a minute to the boot. Notch ZERO before starting the leg, notch EIGHT is the most you may run continuously, ten floods a working leg. Drag with the finger or use the arrow keys.
LEG MOTOR START
Green mushroom in the cast start station. Latches the starter unless the breaker is out, the bearing is seized, or the motor is still cooling from an overload trip.
LEG MOTOR STOP
Large red mushroom. Drops the starter at once. Use it before touching anything on the head or the take-up.
DISTRIBUTOR SELECTOR
The semicircular dial at the head. Press a numbered stud 1–6 and the electric spout runs round to that bin, ringing the position bell as it passes each station. While the spout is travelling, or parked off stud, all grain returns down the return spout into the boot - so choose your bin before you feed.
CYCLONE FAN
Three-position selector, OFF / LOW / HIGH, driving the dust cyclone above the leg. HIGH holds the dust under the alarm line at any feed rate you can legitimately open; LOW only suffices for light trimming; OFF lets the dust build within the minute. Leave her on HIGH whenever the leg turns.
BURNER VALVE
Black knurled knob beside the discharge thermometer. Sets the dryer fire in five-percent steps. Fifty percent suits wheat at a full hundred bushels a minute; less flow, less valve.
BURNER IGNITER
Amber button marked HOLD. Press and hold while the valve is open a crack and the FLAME EYE jewel will flush amber when the fire catches. Release and trim to grade.
FLAME EYE CLEAN
Slate button marked HOLD beneath the igniter. Holds the photocell wiper across the scanner window for a second and a half. Cures a fouled flame scanner (FLAME FAIL).
ALARM ACCEPT
Steadies every flashing annunciator window and stops the horn. It accepts nothing away: the window stays lit amber until the condition itself clears.
HORN CUT
Silences the horn for the alarms now standing. A new alarm rings again from the first stroke.
LAMPS TEST
Hold down: every jewel, window and bin-full lamp lights. Prove the lamps before you believe a dark board.
PLANT RESET
Keyed button on the bottom strip. Clears latched trips (overload, scorch fuel lockout, blown vent flag, seized-bearing latch) and returns the maintenance test switches to OUT. It cures nothing mechanical - only lets you try again once the iron has cooled.
BELT TAKE-UP
Hand crank in the repairs bay. Hold to wind; each turn takes stretch out of the belt and lifts the TENSION needle. The crank is interlocked against a turning leg: stop the motor first. Good tension is the green sector, eighty and up.
BOOT KNOCKER
Cast mallet pusher. One stroke frees bridged grain in the boot. Three strokes mean you should have shut the gate instead.
OIL CUP
Slate HOLD button by the repairs bay. Works lubricant into the head-shaft bearing. Hold it with the leg stopped until the bearing column falls back through the amber band.
MAINTENANCE TESTS
The guarded flap on the right of the bottom strip. Under it, three switches: TEST BELT SLIP, TEST HOT BEARING and TEST FLAME SCAN. Throwing one simulates that defect so a man can rehearse his own alarm drill. Throw OUT (and press PLANT RESET) when the lesson is over.

Normal operation

From a cold, dead board to grain running to bin:

  1. Walk the plant. Pit clear, boot door shut, spout free, nothing on the belt from yesterday. Prove the lamps with LAMPS TEST.
  2. PIT GATE to zero. MAIN BREAKER ON - the SUPPLY jewel lights.
  3. CYCLONE FAN to HIGH. Dust is the enemy; start the fan before the leg.
  4. DISTRIBUTOR SELECTOR to the bin wanted. Wait for the position bell; the chosen stud shows gold when the spout is seated.
  5. Press LEG MOTOR START. Watch the LEG SPEED needle come up onto its green band near 520 feet a minute and the ammeter settle around fifteen amps.
  6. Open the PIT GATE two notches and watch FEED on the flow plate. Ease out a notch at a time toward eight, keeping an eye on the boot glass and the LEG SPEED needle - a sagging needle means the leg is being fed faster than she lifts.
  7. With grain flowing, light the dryer if the moisture calls for it: BURNER VALVE to twenty percent, press and hold BURNER IGNITER until the FLAME EYE jewel burns amber, then release and trim the valve until DISCHARGE reads between 380 and 480 degrees.
  8. Settle in: fan HIGH, gate eight or under, discharge steady, dust needle flat near the bottom of its dial, tension needle in the green, bearing column in the black. The scale register will be turning honestly.
  9. To change bins: ease the gate to four, press the new stud, wait for the bell, bring the gate back out.
  10. To shut down: gate to zero, burner valve closed, let the leg run thirty seconds to clear itself, LEG MOTOR STOP, fan HIGH another two minutes, breaker OUT. Log the register reading in the book.

Alarms

Window / lamp Means Do this
HIGH BOOT More than thirty bushels lying in the boot; feed is beating lift. Ease the PIT GATE back two notches and watch the boot glass fall. If it keeps rising, STOP the leg before she chokes.
LEG SLIP Belt tension low; cups are riding slack and spilling their grain. Gate to zero, LEG MOTOR STOP, wind the BELT TAKE-UP until the tension needle stands in its green sector, restart.
OVERLOAD Motor drew more than thirty-six amps too long - a flooded boot, a slipping belt, or both. Starter has dropped and locked out about twenty seconds. Gate to zero, let the motor cool, drain the boot by restarting gently at low feed, then work back to rate.
BIN FULL The selected bin is within twelve bushels of its 3,500-bushel capacity; grain is spilling back. Choose another bin on the DISTRIBUTOR SELECTOR, or close the gate and call the annex man.
DUST HIGH Dust past three-tenths ounce per thousand cubic feet. This is the one that burns elevators. CYCLONE FAN to HIGH at once and find what is making dust - a travelling spout, an overflowing bin, a gate opened too far. Do not resume feeding until the needle falls.
BEARING HEAT Head-shaft bearing past 85°C. Stop the leg, hold OIL CUP until the column drops back through amber, accept the alarm, restart. Past 140°C the shaft seizes and only PLANT RESET after cooling will answer the start button.
FLAME FAIL The scanner cannot see flame although the burner circuit wants fire; the fuel solenoid is held shut. Hold FLAME EYE CLEAN for its wipe, reopen the valve, relight with the IGNITER.
SCORCH Discharge past 620°F; eighteen seconds of it trips the fuel lockout. Close the BURNER VALVE, check grain flow, find why fire beat grain - usually a gate shut under a lit burner.
VENT BLOWN Dust ignited. The roof vent has gone, everything trips, and the elevator man writes a letter he does not enjoy writing. Fans on, everybody out of the dust, find the ignition source. PLANT RESET only answers once dust is back under the line and the hot metal cooled.

Jewels: SUPPLY (white) proves the breaker; LEG RUN (green) proves the starter pulled up; TRIP (red) repeats overload, seizure or vent; FLAME EYE (amber) proves fire at the dryer gun.

Faults and recovery

leg belt slip
You see: LEG SLIP flashing, speed sagging under load, tension needle sliding out of its green sector, boot climbing though the gate has not moved.
Cause: the take-up has paid out or the belt has stretched - or somebody has thrown TEST BELT SLIP to drill you.
Clear it: PIT GATE to zero; LEG MOTOR STOP; hold BELT TAKE-UP (wind the crank) until tension stands above ninety-six; ALARM ACCEPT; throw the test switch OUT if she was thrown IN; restart per Normal operation. The defect will not clear while the leg turns - the interlock keeps the crank honest.
hot head-shaft bearing
You see: BEARING HEAT at 85°C and the bearing column climbing through amber toward red; smoke wisps over the head past 120°C.
Cause: the cup is dry - springtime greasing skipped, or TEST HOT BEARING thrown for drill.
Clear it: PIT GATE to zero first - a stopped leg under an open gate floods the boot; then LEG MOTOR STOP; hold OIL CUP a good three seconds with the leg standing; watch the column fall back below 85°C; ALARM ACCEPT; throw the test switch OUT if it was thrown; restart. Ignore her and the shaft seizes at 140°C, the leg stalls where she stands, and a hot, smoking bearing over a dusty boot is how VENT BLOWN gets written.
dryer flame failure
You see: FLAME FAIL flashing, FLAME EYE jewel dark, the burner refusing to light no matter how long you beg the igniter.
Cause: the photocell window is fouled with dust so the scanner cannot prove flame, and the fuel solenoid locks out - or TEST FLAME SCAN is thrown for drill.
Clear it: BURNER VALVE to zero; hold FLAME EYE CLEAN for its wipe; throw the test switch OUT if it was thrown; reopen the valve to twenty percent, hold BURNER IGNITER until the eye burns amber, and trim back to grade. Until the eye sees fire the panel will not sell you any burning.
