Overview

The EM-3 forms an image with electrons instead of light. A tungsten hairpin boils electrons off inside the gun; three magnetic lenses carry them through a foil of your specimen no thicker than a soap film; the projectors throw the magnified image onto a willemite screen which glows pale green where the electrons land. What you see on that screen is not a picture of the foil — it is the electrons, twenty thousand of them for every line you can resolve.

The column lives on its vacuum. Air is the enemy of every part of this instrument: it scatters the beam, it burns the filament, and it lays a carbon crust on your specimen faster than you can focus. The rotary pump roughs the column, the oil diffusion pump holds her at a few hundredths of a thousandth of a millimetre of mercury, and the cold trap under the specimen catches what the pumps cannot.

Read the interlocks as instructions, not obstacles. They were fitted after a gentleman at Crayfield drew sixty kilovolts through his own wrist, and they will be fitted after the next one, too.

Controls

Control Type Action
MAINS BREAKER bat toggle Energises the console, the rotary pump motor and the diffusion heater circuit. OFF isolates everything but the standing vacuum.
PUMP SELECTOR rotary, five positions OFF · ROUGH (rotary on the column) · BACK (column isolated) · DIFFUSION (oil pump on the column) · AUTO (diffusion held). Diffusion is interlocked against a wet column or a lost fore-line.
CAMERA PORT VALVE quarter-turn lever OPEN lines the camera chamber to the column; CLOSED isolates the camera port and its spring-cups. Close it against any leak traced to the port.
TRAP CHILL hold button Pours liquid air into the anticontaminator cold trap. Hold five seconds for a full charge; the charge melts away over some hours.
GUN HV KEY guarded keyswitch Lift the guard, turn to RUN. Applies high tension to the gun once the column is better than 2×10-4 torr. A discharge locks the gun until the key is cycled off and run again.
FILAMENT CURRENT slider Heater current to the hairpin, 0–3 A. Emission rises steeply past about 2.4 A — the knee. Sit just above it; driving hard spends the filament.
CONDENSER slider Sets the illumination cone and spot size. Low for gentle survey work, high before you expose a plate.
OBJECTIVE FOCUS micrometer slider Fine focus of the objective lens. Use with the WOBBLER: rock the beam and bring the two blurred states together onto one sharp image.
MAGNIFICATION stepped selector ×2 000 to ×50 000 in six steps. Higher steps dim the screen — raise the CONDENSER to pay for them.
STAGE SHIFT two-axis pad Moves the specimen across the pole-piece. Drag the pad or use arrow keys; the cross bears on the axis.
WOBBLER spring rocker Momentary. Rocks the beam either side of true focus so the image breathes. When the breathing stops, you are in focus.
SCREEN LEVER two-position lever VIEW drops the fluorescent glass into the beam; CAMERA lifts it so the beam falls onto the plate beneath.
EXPOSE plunger In CAMERA mode with a live image, exposes the next plate. The counter counts plates, not apologies.
WATER RESTORE button Reopens the hydrant valve to the objective lens jacket after a loss of cooling water.
NEW FILAMENT button under cover Fits a fresh tungsten hairpin and resets filament life. Open the spares cover first.
FAULT TEST SWITCHES three lidded toggles Maintenance only: injects a PORT LEAK, FILAMENT or WATER failure to prove the annunciation and drill the recovery drills.
ALARM ACCEPT pushbutton Stops the annunciators flashing. Acceptance is not agreement — read the window before you touch the switch.
LAMPS TEST pushbutton Lights every annunciator and jewel at once. A dark lamp proves nothing.
MASTER RESET pushbutton Returns the desk to cold: key out, selector to OFF, alarms cleared. For end of session or after lens service.

Normal operation

  1. MAINS BREAKER to ON. The MAINS jewel lights and the fore-line gauge wakes.
  2. PUMP SELECTOR to ROUGH. Watch the column pressure needle walk down the decades; below 6×10-3 torr she is ready for oil.
  3. PUMP SELECTOR to DIFFUSION. The interlock will refuse her if the column or fore-line is too fat — give the rotary another minute. Wait until the needle settles between 10-5 and 10-6 torr.
  4. Hold TRAP CHILL five seconds to charge the anticontaminator.
  5. Lift the HV guard and turn GUN HV KEY to RUN. The kV meter climbs to fifty and stays there only if the vacuum deserves it.
  6. Raise FILAMENT CURRENT slowly to just past the knee — about 2½ A — until the emission meter stands near forty microamperes. Never chase the last few microamps by force.
  7. Raise CONDENSER until the screen, seen with the SCREEN LEVER at VIEW, shows a bright disc; centre it with STAGE SHIFT.
  8. Bring the image to focus with OBJECTIVE FOCUS. Prove it with the WOBBLER: hold UNDER, hold OVER, and take the mid-position where neither blur remains.
  9. Select MAGNIFICATION as required, re-centring and re-focusing after each step.
  10. To photograph: SCREEN LEVER to CAMERA, let the phosphor fade, set CONDENSER a little strong, press EXPOSE. Return the lever to VIEW.
  11. End of session: filament fully back, GUN HV KEY off, ten minutes on DIFFUSION, then SELECTOR to BACK, then OFF. Breaker OFF. Leave the column holding vacuum.

Alarms

Window Meaning Action
COLUMN VACUUM HIGH Column pressure above 8×10-4 torr. Something leaks or a pump starves. Accept, then find it: try the camera port first. High tension will soon begin to hunt.
COLUMN LET UP TO AIR Column above 5×10-2 torr — effectively at air. The diffusion pump must not sit over her. Selector to OFF, isolate, and rough again from the beginning. Do not rush oil into a wet column.
GUN DISCHARGE High tension broke down inside the gun. The key has been thrown out and the gun locked. Let the vacuum recover below 2×10-4, then cycle the key OFF and RUN. Repeated discharges mean a dirty gun or a wet column.
FILAMENT OPEN The hairpin has parted. Emission is gone though high tension may stand. Open the spares cover, fit NEW FILAMENT, and recondition gently up to the knee.
COOLING WATER LOST Jacket flow below a quarter of normal. Press WATER RESTORE. Watch the lens temperature; do not run high tension while she climbs.
OBJECTIVE OVERHEAT Lens above 92 °C. High tension has been tripped and the lens may have sprung. Restore water, let her fall below forty-six degrees, cycle the key. If focus will not pull sharp afterwards, the lens needs the works — call the demonstrator and use MASTER RESET after service.

Faults and recovery

Camera port leak

You see: the column needle creeping up decade by decade with the pumps in order; the COLUMN VACUUM HIGH window flashing; ripple growing on the kV meter until the gun discharges if you leave her.

Recovery: turn the CAMERA PORT VALVE to CLOSED — the leak lives in the camera spring-cups and the valve isolates them. The diffusion pump walks the column back down inside two minutes and the window goes dark. Reopen the valve only after the port has had new cups.

Filament burnout

You see: the image vanish and the emission meter fall to nothing while kV stands honest; the FILAMENT OPEN window lit. Burnout follows old age or an impatient hand on FILAMENT CURRENT.

Recovery: filament control fully back. Open the spares cover and press NEW FILAMENT. Raise the heater slowly — a new hairpin should be taken to the knee over half a minute, not two seconds.

Cooling water loss

You see: the flow meter sagging towards zero and the lens temperature climbing away from eighteen degrees; the COOLING WATER LOST window flashing. Left alone she reaches ninety-two degrees, trips the gun and springs the lens — the OBJECTIVE OVERHEAT window follows.

Recovery: press WATER RESTORE to reopen the hydrant. Keep high tension off until the temperature falls below forty-six degrees, then cycle GUN HV KEY. If focus now wanders and will not settle even cold, the lenspole has sprung: log the defect, tell the demonstrator, and MASTER RESET only after the service is done.
