Overview

This apparatus receives the central forecasting service's analysed synoptic chart over the leased landline and draws it, line by line, on a waxed strip chart twenty-five metres to the roll. The machine was built for the Bureau by Pell & Son, instrument makers of Bristol, and delivered to this office in the spring of 1954. It is an office instrument, not a wireless set: treat her as you would a good typewriter — clean, oiled, and never hurried.

The principle is simple enough to say and delicate to keep. A synchronous motor turns the drum at the index speed; a stylus rides across the moving wax and darkens it in proportion to the strength of the incoming picture signal. For the picture to come out square, our drum must stay in step with the transmitting drum at the Centre. That is the whole art of the machine: raise enough gain to hear the line plainly, then rock the phase control until the ON SYNC eye burns steady, and keep an eye on her while she works. A receiver that runs out of phase does not merely spoil a corner — the tear runs right across the front of a depression, and the morning briefing goes to press without an analysis.

Controls

Control Type Action and positions
POWER SWITCH guarded toggle Mains to the machine. Lift the guard, then throw UP for ON, DOWN for OFF. The MAINS jewel lights when she is live.
CIRCUIT SELECTOR rotary, 3 positions Picks the incoming line. MAIN LINE is the leased pair (strong); STANDBY LINE is the old pole route (weak — expect to work the gain); TEST TONE puts the workshop's steady tone on the stylus for checking.
GAIN CONTROL knob Line amplification, minus 15 to plus 15 dB. Set so the SIGNAL LEVEL needle sits in the black band.
PHASE CONTROL knob, centre-zero Rocks our drum against the transmitter's. Watch the PHASE ERROR meter and bring her to zero; the ON SYNC eye lights steady when locked.
INDEX SELECTOR rotary, 3 positions Drum speed and index: 60 rpm at index 92, 90 rpm at index 132, 120 rpm at index 176. This office works 90 / 132 unless the Centre says otherwise.
CLUTCH LEVER lever, 2 positions STOP / RUN. Drops the stylus onto the turning drum. She will not engage without mains and a threaded chart — that is what the interlock is for.
DENSITY CONTROL knob Stylus current, positions 1 to 10. High numbers give a heavy black trace and cook the stylus; 3 to 5 is the working range.
CHART LEVER lever, 2 positions THREAD seats a fresh roll and squares it on the drum; RELEASE lets the finished or spoiled chart off. Moving to THREAD while she records cuts the chart short and wastes it.
STYLUS CLEANER pushbutton Works a brass brush across the stylus tip. Three passes, machine stopped and below 70 °C, true a burned tip. Useless while the drum turns.
ALARM ACCEPT pushbutton Steadies the annunciator flags and stops the buzzer. Accepting is not curing: the flag relights its condition until the cause is dealt with.
LAMPS TEST pushbutton Lights every flag, jewel and eye at once. Press again to cancel. Prove it each Monday morning.
FAULT TEST SWITCHES three guarded toggles Under the maintenance lid: LINE DROP, STYLUS BURN and BELT SLIP. They put a test fault on the machine exactly as the real thing would arrive. Throw down to clear the cause.
CONSOLE RESET pushbutton Returns the whole console to cold and alarm-free: mains off, clutch out, counters zero. For the start of a shift, not for an emergency.

Normal operation

Taking the morning analysis, from cold:

  1. Sight the panel. Every flag down, CLUTCH LEVER at STOP, no chart on the drum. If not, press CONSOLE RESET and begin from cold.
  2. Lift the guard and throw the POWER SWITCH up. The MAINS jewel lights, the motor hums, LINE VOLTS should read between 235 and 243.
  3. Set CIRCUIT SELECTOR to MAIN LINE.
  4. Set INDEX SELECTOR to 90 / 132, or to whatever index the Centre's schedule board shows for this transmission.
  5. Work GAIN CONTROL until the SIGNAL LEVEL needle stands in the black band. On the standby circuit you may need most of the travel; if she will not come up, see SIGNAL LOW under Alarms.
  6. Throw CHART LEVER to THREAD. Fresh paper squares itself on the drum; the plate reads READY.
  7. Throw CLUTCH LEVER to RUN. The stylus drops onto the paper and RECORDING shows on the plate.
  8. At once watch PHASE ERROR and rock PHASE CONTROL until the meter stands at zero and the ON SYNC eye burns steady. Do this in the first ten seconds — the coastline comes down the wire early and a torn coast ruins the chart.
  9. Check DENSITY CONTROL: 3 to 5 for a clean trace. If STYLUS TEMP climbs towards 95 °C, take density off rather than admire it.
  10. Stand by her while she works. Accept any flag with ALARM ACCEPT, but read the flag before you cure it. A full chart takes five minutes at index 132.
  11. On CHART END the clutch releases herself and the plate reads COMPLETE — TAKE CHART. Throw CHART LEVER to RELEASE, hang the analysis on the plotting-room hook, and leave the panel ready for the next schedule.
  12. Prove LAMPS TEST before you walk away on the late shift.

Alarms

Flag Meaning What to do
SIGNAL LOW The line is too weak to hold synchronism. Expect the phase meter to begin wandering. Raise GAIN. If the main line stays low, switch CIRCUIT SELECTOR to STANDBY LINE and re-phase. Tell the exchange the leased pair is faulty.
OFF PHASE Our drum has lost step with the Centre's. The trace begins to shear and, past 110 degrees, tears. Rock PHASE CONTROL to zero and hold her on the eye. If she will not lock, the signal is poor — cure SIGNAL LOW first. A torn chart is waste: re-thread and start again.
STYLUS OVERHEAT Stylus current too heavy or the drum stalled hot. Past 116 °C the tip burns and the trace goes pale and streaky. Take DENSITY down. If the flag came with the machine stopped, look for a seized drive. A burned tip is trued with three passes of STYLUS CLEANER once she cools below 70 °C.
DRIVE FAULT The belt is slipping on the motor pulley: drum speed wrong against the index setting. Neglected, it chews itself into a jam. Take the clutch to STOP, cycle the POWER SWITCH off and on to re-seat the belt, then restart and re-phase. A jam needs the maintenance man, not more throttle.
CHART END The roll has run out. Information only. Release the completed chart and thread fresh paper for the next schedule.
CHART WASTED A chart has been rejected — torn, burned or jammed — and is counted against the office. Note the cause in the log, cure it, re-thread and re-take. Charts cost money and the Supervisor reads the waste counter on Fridays.

On accepting alarms

ACCEPT stills the buzzer and steadies the lamps so you can read them; it clears nothing. A flag whose cause remains stays lit. Ignoring a flag is how charts are wasted: SIGNAL LOW becomes OFF PHASE becomes a ruined analysis, and STYLUS OVERHEAT becomes a burned tip that writes nothing worth keeping.

Faults and recovery

line dropout

You see: SIGNAL LOW; the SIGNAL LEVEL needle falls towards the pin; the phase meter wanders and the ON SYNC eye flickers or dies; given time, OFF PHASE and a sheared trace.

To clear: advance GAIN CONTROL; if the needle still will not stand in the band, turn CIRCUIT SELECTOR to STANDBY LINE, bring the gain up there, rock PHASE CONTROL back to zero and let the eye lock. Maintenance may prove the console by lifting the lid and throwing the LINE DROP test switch — throw it down again afterwards.

stylus burnout

You see: STYLUS OVERHEAT; the thermometer climbs past 95 °C and keeps climbing; the trace pales, streaks and finally marks nothing. The chart in progress is rejected as wasted.

To clear: stop the clutch. Take DENSITY CONTROL down to 3 or less and let the thermometer fall below 70 °C. Then press STYLUS CLEANER three times — the plate will confirm STYLUS CLEANED AND TRUED — re-thread, engage and re-phase. Maintenance may force the condition with the STYLUS BURN test switch; clear it the same way.

drive belt slip

You see: DRIVE FAULT; DRUM SPEED reads low and unsteady against the index setting; the chart runs long and the record crowds. Left alone ninety seconds, the drive jams: the clutch releases herself and the chart is rejected.

To clear: CLUTCH LEVER to STOP. Cycle the POWER SWITCH off, count three, and on again — the motor re-seats the belt on the cone. Engage, confirm DRUM SPEED against the index, re-phase, and continue or re-thread as the chart deserves. The BELT SLIP test switch forces the same behaviour for drill.
