Operating Manual for Playing Cabin No. 2, St. Amandustoren, Aldenhoorn — Inrichting No. 214, delivered Anno 1954.

This book is bolted beside the door. It is never to be removed from the tower. Amendments are pasted in by the works, initialed by the city engineer.

Overview

The St. Amandus carillon counts forty-seven bells; the twelve smallest are wired to this cabin, pitched C4 to C5. You do not play them with your arms alone — you play them through a transmission. Thirteen oak batons stand before you (the chromatic five on the upper rail); each pulls a phosphor-bronze tether wire over a guide pulley and out through the wall to the clapper of its bell. Strike the baton squarely and let go; the wire does the rest.

For the quarters and the public marches the cabin does not need you at all. A playing drum — a barrel pegged with the tune of the hour — is driven by a cast-iron weight of one and nine-tenths tonnes hanging in the stairwell. The weight has seven metres six of fall; when it is spent, the drum stops wherever it is, mid-bar if the tower wills it. A flyball governor holds the tempo while the weight lasts; as the weight empties, the march drags, and no governor can hide that.

The belfry louvres you see in the elevation are the machine's own voice: opened for feast days, closed for rain. And mind the sway. A North Sea gale rolls this tower; above forty-five millimetres at the bell chamber the interlock drops the heavy bass wires out, because a swinging bell pulled against a swinging tower snaps wires. The tower always wins. Wait for her.

Controls

BATON CLAVIER (CLAVIER)
Thirteen struck batons, naturals on the lower rail, chromatics on the upper. Each carries its bell number at the foot. A baton marked with a crimson cross has a fractured wire behind it: striking it gives a wooden knock and nothing more until the reserve set is routed. Tab to a baton and press Enter or Space to strike.
TRANSMISSION SELECTOR
Three-position rotary: OFF uncouples every wire from the clappers (batons move, bells sleep); MAIN routes the main wire set; SPARE routes the reserve set, by which a fractured wire is bypassed. Leave her on OFF whenever work is done inside the transmission.
WEIGHT BRAKE
Lever under a red guard flap, two positions: HOLD grips the drum shaft so the weight hangs where she stands; RELEASE lets the drive run. The guard must be lifted before the lever will move.
REWIND CRANK
Brass hand-wheel with a hold-to-wind push button. Winding is only possible with the WEIGHT BRAKE at HOLD and the DRUM CLUTCH OUT — you would otherwise be cranking against the whole tune. Full wind from empty takes a little under ten seconds of holding.
PROGRAMME SELECTOR
Six-way rotary over the programme card: I Aldenhoornsche Mars, II Lied van de Toren, III Avondlied, IV Gildekwartier, V Kermiswijzer, VI Stille Cantus. Turning it re-pegs the drum for the chosen march; the card frame shows what is pinned.
DRUM CLUTCH
Slide lever, OUT and IN. IN throws the drum train into mesh and the march begins at the next pin. OUT frees the drum for winding, pin-mending, or silence.
GOVERNOR TEMPO
Knob and dial, 24 to 72 strokes to the minute. The brass needle shows commanded tempo; the crimson needle shows what the drum is actually doing. When the crimson needle falls below the brass, the weight is telling you something.
MEND PIN (hold)
Hold-button beneath the clutch. With the DRUM CLUTCH OUT, hold it a full three seconds to drive a new pin into the barrel. The sage progress strip beneath the button fills as the pin seats.
SWELL SHUTTERS
Quadrant lever, 0 to 100 per cent, working the belfry louvres drawn in the tower elevation. Shutters closed, the cabin is barely audible in the square; wide open, the whole town hears her.
FLAG RESET
Cream push button on the right station. Latched alarm flags drop only when their cause has truly cleared — the button tests that and then releases the spring flag. It clears nothing that has not been put right.
FAULT TEST SWITCHES
Three guarded test levers — WIRE, PIN, GALE — for proving the alarms without climbing the tower. Each springs back; each injects the fault it names, exactly as if nature had done it.
SOUND CUT
Toggle that silences the cabin loudspeaker (the small horn behind the ceiling rose that repeats the bells for the player's family below). The bells themselves are not affected; the tower rings regardless. The speaker circuit wakes at your first touch of the cabinet.

Normal operation

From cold cabin to a march rolling over the roofs:

  1. Read the tower elevation. Wind and SWAY must sit under the crimson line (45 mm). If the pendulum is sweeping wide, do nothing yet; wait for the gust to pass.
  2. Look to the weight gauge. If she stands below 6 m, hold the REWIND CRANK (WEIGHT BRAKE still at HOLD, DRUM CLUTCH OUT) until she stands at the top of her travel.
  3. Turn the TRANSMISSION SELECTOR to MAIN.
  4. Lift the red GUARD and move the WEIGHT BRAKE to RELEASE.
  5. Choose the march on the PROGRAMME SELECTOR; confirm the title on the card frame.
  6. Throw the DRUM CLUTCH to IN. The drum gathers way and the first pin strikes.
  7. Open the SWELL SHUTTERS to about sixty per cent.
  8. Set the GOVERNOR TEMPO to forty-eight for an ordinary market day.
  9. Stand clear of the wires, watch the flag rail for the first minute, and keep the weight above her crimson line. That is all the art there is.

To play by hand instead, leave the DRUM CLUTCH OUT after step 4 and strike the CLAVIER yourself. The weight spends nothing while you play; your own arm is the engine.

Alarms

Five spring flags live in the rail above the bench, and one ruby jewel — the trembler circuit — burns while any of them stands. All latches are released only by the FLAG RESET button, and only once their cause is truly gone.

WEIGHT LOW
Fewer than 1½ metres of fall remain. The march is already dragging — listen to the crimson needle. Action: plan your rewind at the end of the current turn of the tune. If ignored: the weight lands, the drive stops dead mid-stroke and the DRIVE STALLED flag follows her down.
SWAY HIGH
The tower is rolling past 45 mm. The interlock has already dropped the bass wires; the drum's low pins strike air. Action: stop forcing the heavy batons, close the shutters against the squall if you wish, and wait. If ignored: every forced bass pull snatches the wire over its guide; three snatches and the wire goes — see FAULTS AND RECOVERY.
WIRE FRACTURED
A tether wire has parted; its baton knocks wood and rings nothing. Action: TRANSMISSION SELECTOR to SPARE, prove the baton, then FLAG RESET. The rigger splices the main set at his next visit.
PIN SHEARED
A barrel pin has gone and its note dies unplayed; the count of missed notes climbs. Action: DRUM CLUTCH OUT, hold MEND PIN three seconds, FLAG RESET, clutch IN.
DRIVE STALLED
The weight is on the floor of her pit and the drum has stopped where she stopped. Action: DRUM CLUTCH OUT, WEIGHT BRAKE to HOLD, REWIND CRANK until she tops her travel, FLAG RESET, brake RELEASE, clutch IN. The march resumes at the next pin, not where she died — the town will forgive a bar lost, not a tower silenced.

Faults and recovery

Tether wire fracture

What you see: a crimson cross at one baton, its wire slack on the wire board, the WIRE FRACTURED flag up within moments, the jewel lit. If it came from forcing the bass against a roll, the SWAY HIGH flag will have lit first — that sequence is the tower scolding you.

  1. Stop striking the dead baton; she cannot answer.
  2. Turn the TRANSMISSION SELECTOR to SPARE. The reserve wire takes the load.
  3. Prove the baton with a single strike; the bell must speak clean.
  4. Press FLAG RESET. While the reserve set carries the bell, tell the ringer's clerk, who will book the rigger.

Drum pin shear

What you see: a note missing from the march — the pattern skips like a record with a scratch, though nothing else changes — PIN SHEARED flag up, and the missed count rising on the inspection.

  1. Throw the DRUM CLUTCH OUT. The barrel must stand still for mending.
  2. Hold the MEND PIN button a full three seconds until the sage strip fills.
  3. Press FLAG RESET.
  4. Clutch IN again; the repaired pin strikes on her next revolution.

Gale squall

What you see: the cups on the anemometer quickening, the pendulum widening, the wind readout climbing towards twenty metres a second, and SWAY HIGH up as she passes the line. The bass drops out of the march — that is the interlock saving her wires.

  1. Do not force the heavy batons. Three snatches break a wire, and a broken wire in a gale cannot be fetched back.
  2. Let the drum carry on; her low pins strike air and cost the weight nothing.
  3. When the pendulum settles under 45 mm again, press FLAG RESET.
  4. If a wire went during the squall anyway, follow TETHER WIRE FRACTURE above.

Specifications

Bells wired to this cabin 12 + 1 reserve (of 47), C4 – C5
Driving weight 1.9 t cast iron, north stairwell pit
Fall of the weight 7.6 m; WEIGHT LOW at 1.5 m remaining
Playing drum Ø 900 mm barrel, six marches pinned, 96 oak pegs
Governor range 24 – 72 strokes per minute
Sag at empty weight 45 % of commanded tempo, minimum
Tether wires phosphor bronze, Ø 2.5 mm, proof 25 daN
Sway interlock drops bass wires above 45 mm at the bell chamber
Snatch tolerance three forced bass pulls against the interlock
Swell shutters 0 – 100 %, two faces, north and west
Rewind rate full wind in under 10 s at the crank
Transmission main and spare sets, guide pulleys every 1.2 m
Builder's plate Inrichting No. 214, Vanbeke & Zonen, Aldenhoorn, 1954

Never loosen any screw of this cabinet without order of the church council (kerkenraad). Weight, wires and wind are patient; the beiaardier need only be as patient as they are.
