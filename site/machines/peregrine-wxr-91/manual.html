Overview

The WXR-91 is the operator position for a pulsed-Doppler weather radar working in S-band. One cabinet does three jobs: it conditions and monitors the klystron transmitter, it drives the azimuth-elevation pedestal through selectable volume scan patterns, and it turns the returning echoes into the plan-position display you see on the cathode-ray tube. The console was derived from an airborne fire-control processor; where that set hunted aircraft, this one watches thunderstorms. It is built to military drawing practice because it has to run unattended through a night of hail, but it lives in an office, so it is dressed like office furniture. Treat it as both.

The tube uses a long-persistence P7 phosphor. A cell painted by the sweep fades over several seconds; the picture you see is the last two minutes of sky, brightest where the beam last passed. When the high voltage is off the antenna still turns, but the tube paints only receiver noise — a faint grain, no weather. That is the fastest health check you have.

Controls

Every operating control is on the desk surface. Guarded switches carry a clear flip-up cover; nothing under a guard can be moved by a sleeve.

Control Type Positions Function
MAINS POWER guarded toggle ON / OFF Desk and transmitter primary power. Lifting the guard is deliberate; closing the guard trips the switch off.
TRANSMITTER HV guarded toggle ON / OFF Klystron beam high voltage. Closes only when FILAMENT is lit and any arc timer has expired. Dropping it clears the arc detector.
SCAN MODE rotary selector STANDBY / CLEAR-AIR / PRECIP / SEVERE / MANUAL Selects the volume coverage pattern: antenna rate and elevation cut sequence. MANUAL hands the antenna to the handwheel.
AZIMUTH SLEW handwheel continuous, ±140°/s Drives the antenna by hand in MANUAL mode. Spinning it in any other mode moves nothing; the SLEW INHIBIT lamp tells you so.
STC RANGE lever slider MIN – MAX Sensitivity time control: near-range gain. Raise until close-in clutter sits mid-scale, no higher.
WG PURGE momentary button, hold held / released Runs the dry-air compressor hard. Hold three seconds to seal a guide leak and repack pressure.
PEDESTAL RESET pushbutton press Recloses the pedestal breaker after an overload trip, once drive load is back under 80 %.
ACK pushbutton press Stops annunciator flash and the audible chirp. Lit tiles stay lit while the condition stands.
FAULT INJECT keyswitch OFF / TX ARC / PED STALL / WG LEAK Maintenance test positions. TX ARC and PED STALL are momentary — the key springs back to OFF and the injected line drops out, leaving whatever damage it did. WG LEAK is maintained: the leak stays until you return the key to OFF.
SIM RESET pushbutton press Returns the entire simulation to cold and alarm-free without reloading the page.
SOUND pushbutton SOUND OFF / SOUND ON Enables the relay clacks, blower hum and alarm chirp. Silent until you ask.
MANUAL pushbutton press Opens this document over the console.

Normal operation

From cold to painting weather takes about half a minute. Follow the order; each step earns the next.

  1. Press MANUAL if you want this document open beside the desk.
  2. Lift the MAINS POWER guard and switch ON. The blower starts, the lamps prove themselves, and FILAMENT begins warming.
  3. Wait for FILAMENT to turn green — roughly six seconds. HV READY lights beside it when the transmitter may be energised.
  4. Turn SCAN MODE to PRECIPITATION for routine surveillance. The antenna leaves the brake and settles onto its first cut at 0.5°.
  5. Lift the TRANSMITTER HV guard and switch ON. FWD POWER climbs to about 750 kW and the tube paints its first sweep.
  6. Raise STC RANGE until ground clutter within 40 km sits mid-scale and the storm structure beyond stays clean.
  7. Watch the annunciator column through the first full volume scan. One steady amber tile is information; a flashing red tile is an instruction.
  8. For convection, select SEVERE — faster turns, five cuts to 2.8°. For dawn insect clears, CLEAR-AIR. For a single slow look, MANUAL and the AZIMUTH SLEW wheel.

To shut down: TRANSMITTER HV OFF, SCAN MODE to STANDBY, let the collector run below 60 °C, then MAINS POWER OFF and close both guards.

Alarms

Annunciator tiles light amber for a caution and red for an alarm, flashing until acknowledged. An unheeded red tile always ends the same way: the equipment protects itself and you lose the data.

Tile Colour Means Do this
ARC DETECTED red An RF arc struck in the tube or guide. High voltage has already dropped out. Let the 25-second arc timer expire, recycle TRANSMITTER HV. Find the cause below before holding SEVERE again.
KLYSTRON TEMP red Collector above 92 °C. Above 104 °C the tube will arc and trip itself. Drop to a slower pattern or STANDBY. Ignoring it costs you the transmitter, mid-storm, on purpose.
REFLECTED POWER amber More than 6 % of forward power coming back — usually standing water in the guide. Check GUIDE PRESSURE, purge the guide, ease the scan rate. Reflected power heats the collector.
PEDESTAL LOAD red Drive current above 84 %, or the breaker has tripped. Slow or stop the scan, let load fall under 80 %, press PEDESTAL RESET.
GUIDE PRESSURE amber Dry-air pressure below 8 psig. Moisture ingress follows, echoes fade, arcs follow that. Hold WG PURGE three seconds once the leak is sealed. Keep scanning meanwhile if you must.

Faults and recovery

Three faults account for nearly every lost hour on this set. Each is reproducible from the FAULT INJECT keyswitch for training; the recoveries below are the real ones.

transmitter arcing

You see: ARC DETECTED flashing red, FWD POWER collapsed to zero, the sweep still turning but painting nothing but grain.
Cause: collector overheated, wet guide, or a gassy tube. The arc detector has latched and dumped high voltage.
Recover:

  1. Leave the guard up and switch TRANSMITTER HV OFF. This clears the latch and resets the arcing tendency.
  2. Correct the cause: KLYSTRON TEMP falling, GUIDE PRESSURE above 10 psig.
  3. After the 25-second arc timer expires, switch TRANSMITTER HV ON. Watch one full sweep before leaving the desk.

pedestal drive stall

You see: PEDESTAL LOAD pinned near 100 %, azimuth frozen, then the breaker dropping out with a clack; DRIVE LOAD falls but the antenna is dead.
Cause: bearing seizure, icing, or a stalled servo stage. The breaker has done its job.
Recover:

  1. Take SCAN MODE to STANDBY and return the FAULT INJECT keyswitch to OFF if it is engaged.
  2. Wait for drive load under 80 % on the meter.
  3. Press PEDESTAL RESET. Restore your scan mode; listen for the brake release.

waveguide pressure loss

You see: GUIDE PRESSURE amber, the pressure readout sagging toward 1 psig, REFLECTED POWER following, storms on the tube growing dim and ragged as receiver noise climbs.
Cause: a split radome flange or failed seal letting the dry air out — and the spring rain in.
Recover:

  1. Return the FAULT INJECT keyswitch to OFF if it stands at WG LEAK. If MAINS POWER is off, switch it on; the compressor needs the desk bus.
  2. Hold WG PURGE for three seconds to seal the leak path and run the compressor hard.
  3. Confirm pressure recovering toward 12 psig and both GUIDE PRESSURE and REFLECTED POWER extinguishing. If the tile returns within the hour, write it up; the leak is real.
