OVERVIEW

No. 2 shaft is wound by a friction (Koepe) winder: one rope over a wheel at the headgear, a cage on one side and a counterweight on the other, 300 metres bank to surface. The wheel is turned by a thyristor-fed DC motor in the winder house below you. You control it with two hands — the BRAKE LEVER in your left, the POWER QUADRANT in your right — and you land the cage on the marks by eye, on the depth counter, and on the shaft section in front of you.

The brake is spring-applied and hydraulically released. With no pressure on the gauge she holds herself; pressure pulls the pads off. Lose the charge and she will hold — but she will not wind until the hydraulic pack is restored.

The indicator drum and counter are geared to the wheel. They tell you where the rope has put the cage. If rope slips in the wheel grips they lie, and the OUT OF STEP protection will stop the winder until maintenance re-marks her.

CONTROLS

MASTER KEY
Key switch, two positions vertical/horizontal. Vertical is OFF, horizontal is ON: control power to the drive, the hydraulic pack and the blower. Turning off under way dumps the drive and applies the spring brake.
MODE SELECTOR
Rotary, three positions. INSPECT — reduced power for riding an overman, about a sixth of full pull. MANUAL — normal driving from the levers. AUTO — the winder answers landing calls herself at men-riding speed; your levers follow the controller but are overridden until you leave AUTO.
BRAKE LEVER
Bail-handled lever, left of the bench. Down = APPLIED (pads on, no pressure). Pull up = RELEASE (pressure up to 95 bar, pads off). Release her only when you mean to move.
POWER QUADRANT
Notched quadrant lever, right of the bench. Centre zero. Forward of centre = RAISE, behind = LOWER. Full travel either way; power fades above 6 m/s as the field weakens.
BELL PLUNGER
Rings the banksman's bell. One stroke STOP, two LOWER, three RAISE; repeated strokes within a moment count on the tally beside the plunger.
BANK CALL / SURFACE CALL
Lamped pushbuttons registering a winding call from the bank or the surface. In AUTO a registered call is answered automatically; the lamp goes out when the cage lands there. Press again to cancel a call.
ALARM ACCEPT
Steadies any flashing annunciator window once you have seen it.
HORN CUT
Silences the alarm horn. A new alarm sounds again regardless.
TRIP RESET
Resets the protective trip after a stop, provided she stands still, the quadrant is at zero, the out-of-step mismatch has been re-marked away, and a thermally tripped motor has cooled below 140 °C.
MAINTENANCE TESTS
Hinged cover below the calls. Behind it, three test buttons used by the electricians to prove the protections: BRK HYD LEAK, ROPE SLIP and MOTOR HEAT. Each press puts the condition onto the winder; press the same button again to take it off. Leave the cover closed when not testing.

NORMAL OPERATION

From cold, cage standing at the bank:

  1. Walk the plant: rope on the wheel, gates at both landings, no workmen in the shaft. Blower running.
  2. Turn the MASTER KEY to ON. MAINS and DRIVE ON lamps light. The blower starts.
  3. Pull the BRAKE LEVER to RELEASE and watch the brake gauge come up to 95 bar. If it will not come up, stop — see FAULTS AND RECOVERY.
  4. Set the MODE SELECTOR to MANUAL (AUTO only when told).
  5. SIGNAL the bank on the BELL PLUNGER and wait for the banksman's answer before moving.
  6. Ease the POWER QUADRANT forward a notch. When she lifts off, bring the quadrant round steadily; keep rope speed inside 8 m/s for men, 12 m/s for material.
  7. Watch the shaft section and the depth counter. As the cage comes up to the landing marks, bring the quadrant back through zero.
  8. Land her gently: ride the BRAKE LEVER down against her weight until she stops ON MARK, then apply fully.
  9. Answer further calls the same way. In AUTO: register the call, and she winds, lands and brakes herself — watch her do it.
  10. When finished: cage landed and braked, quadrant at zero, MASTER KEY OFF, cover closed.

ALARMS

Window Meaning What to do
OVER-SPEED Rope speed past 13 m/s. Trip at 15. Ease the quadrant back and check her on the brake. Reset after the stop if all is well.
OVER-WIND Cage past a landing into the overtravel. Trip has already stopped her. Wind her back onto the mark slowly, then TRIP RESET.
BRAKE FAULT Hydraulic charge low or leaking; she cannot be released properly. Do not wind. Land or hold her, isolate, call the electricians. See FAULTS AND RECOVERY.
TEMP HIGH Motor windings past 150 °C. Thermal trip at 175. Shut down the winding, let the blower run her cool below 140 °C before restarting.
DEPTH FAULT The indicator disagrees with the cage by more than 2 m — rope slipping in the grips. Stop winding. Trip comes at 8 m. Maintenance to re-clamp and re-mark; see FAULTS AND RECOVERY.
WINDER TRIPPED A protection has stopped the winder. Note the cause, clear it, then TRIP RESET.

New alarms flash and sound the horn. ALARM ACCEPT steadies them; HORN CUT silences the horn only. A window goes out when its condition clears — except the trip windows, which stay lit until TRIP RESET.

FAULTS AND RECOVERY

Brake hydraulic leak

You see: BRAKE FAULT flashing within seconds of the test press; the brake gauge falls away and will not hold 95 bar; pulling the BRAKE LEVER does not release the pads, so the motor stalls against the brake if you try to wind — amperes high, speed nil.

To clear: stop attempting to wind. Open MAINTENANCE TESTS and press BRK HYD LEAK again to close the leak. Pull the BRAKE LEVER and let the pack rebuild 95 bar, verify on the gauge, then wind normally. TRIP RESET if she tripped meanwhile.

Rope slip

You see: DEPTH FAULT flashing while the cage creeps off the mark with the counter unmoved — the section shows the truth, the counter lies. At 8 m of disagreement the OUT OF STEP trip stops everything.

To clear: let her stand. Open MAINTENANCE TESTS and press ROPE SLIP again; maintenance re-clamps the rope and the counter walks back onto the true position at walking pace. When DEPTH FAULT goes out, TRIP RESET and prove the landings at INSPECT speed before men ride.

Motor overtemperature

You see: TEMP HIGH as the thermometer climbs past 150 °C (the test press heats her fast, like a blocked filter); at 175 °C the thermal trip kills torque mid-wind and WINDER TRIPPED lights.

To clear: she will roll on the brake — land her where she stands or hold her. Open MAINTENANCE TESTS and press MOTOR HEAT again to remove the simulated fault. Let the blower cool the windings below 140 °C, then TRIP RESET and continue at moderate power.

SPECIFICATIONS

Item Value
Shaft No. 2 South, 300 m bank to surface
Winder Friction (Koepe) wheel, single rope, c/w counterweight
Rope pull at full quadrant 26 kN at the rope, fading above 6 m/s
Men-riding / material speeds 8 m/s / 12 m/s
Overspeed alarm / trip 13 m/s / 15 m/s

Armature current 0–800 A; sustained full-pull about 640 A
