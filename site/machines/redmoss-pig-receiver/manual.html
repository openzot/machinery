PIG RECEIVER No. 1
OPERATING MANUAL

REDMOSS TERMINAL — 36-INCH SALES GAS LINE — TRAP SKID 2

← BACK TO THE PANEL TERMINAL DIRECTORY ↑

NOTE. This manual is bolted to the canopy post beside the panel. It is issued with the falling-pin closure and applies to Receiver No. 1 only. The No. 2 trap, being older, has a swing-bolt door and its own book.

Overview

Pig Receiver No. 1 is the shore-end receiving trap for the 36-inch sales gas line that runs 34 kilometres from the Redmoss field manifold to this terminal. Cleaning and inspection pigs travel that line pushed by field compression against plant sales pressure; this skid catches them, takes the liquids they push ahead of them, and hands the gas to the plant through the geared mainline valve.

A receipt has five movements: put the barrel on the line (the kicker bypass comes off), let her come home easy into the arrestor, isolate the barrel from the field and the plant, prove it dead and drain it, and only then swing the clamp ring on its falling-pin interlock and pull the pig. The interlock pin is the law here: while the barrel gauge shows more than fifty millibar the pin stays in and the ring cannot leave LOCKED. There is no override, and there has not needed to be one.

The panel is daylight plant. What you see lit is flat LED indication and one xenon beacon; the flow computer LCD is reflective and reads best with the sun on it.

Controls

Control Kind Positions and use
PANEL BREAKER Rocker Panel supply. OFF is down. Indication, the LCD and the beacon are dead with it off; the valves remain as left.
INLET VALVE Quarter-turn lever Field gas into the barrel. SHUT is handle across the pipe, OPEN along it.
KICKER VALVE Quarter-turn lever Bypass around the whole trap. OPEN sends the plant its gas without entering the barrel; SHUT puts the barrel on the line.
MAINLINE VALVE Geared handwheel Ten turns shut to fully open. Barrel outlet to the plant. Turn it with the wheel plates or the arrow keys; watch the percent on the computer.
TRAP VENT VALVE Quarter-turn lever Barrel to the flare stack. Used to trim the pig on approach and to blow the barrel down after isolation.
TRAP DRAIN VALVE Quarter-turn lever Barrel liquids to the drains tank. Watch the sight glass, not your memory.
CLAMP RING Ratcheted tommy bar LOCKED – CRACKED – OPEN. Each press swings the ring one sector, but only when the interlock pin is out (barrel under 50 mbar). Swing it back the same way to close.
PIG PULLER Held T-bar Hold three seconds to walk the pig out. Needs the door open, the arrestor undamaged and the barrel drained below a quarter metre.
LAMP TEST Pushbutton, hold Lights every annunciator lens while held.
ALARM ACCEPT Pushbutton Stops the flash and the beacon; the window stays lit while the condition stands.
ALARM RESET Pushbutton Extinguishes windows whose condition has cleared. Will NOT clear ARRESTOR DAMAGE or LIQUID SPILL - those need the CONSOLE RESET after the fitters have been.
SIGNALLER RESET Pushbutton Drops the S1/S2 passage flags and re-arms both signaller heads after a failure.
MAINTENANCE TESTS Guarded buttons Lift the chequer guard to reach TEST PIG STALL, TEST INLET PASSING and TEST SIGNALLER FAIL. Each injects its training fault from the section Faults and recovery; they are not to be used to prove plant on stream. Lift, press, put the guard down after.
CONSOLE RESET Pushbutton Returns panel and plant to cold and clear: valves shut, ring locked, pig back on the line, every latched trip out.

Normal operation

  1. Cold panel. Check the mimic against the skid: kicker bypass OPEN, inlet and vent and drain SHUT, mainline geared shut, ring LOCKED, door shut, barrel gauge reading zero.
  2. Raise the PANEL BREAKER. POWER lights and the computer wakes.
  3. Hold LAMP TEST; every lens burns. Let go; nothing stuck on.
  4. Line the barrel up: open the INLET VALVE, gear the MAINLINE VALVE open the full ten turns, then close the KICKER VALVE. THROUGH BARREL lights and the plant now takes its gas through the barrel.
  5. Watch the profile strip and the RANGE drum. At two kilometres the S1 signaller raises its flag and APPROACH lights.
  6. If she slows to a crawl in the last half kilometre, crack the TRAP VENT VALVE for a few seconds to lighten the back pressure, then shut it again - she coasts. Do NOT hold the vent open on top of her: the arrestor takes at most fourteen kilometres an hour.
  7. She hits the arrestor and PIG RECEIVED lights. Gear the MAINLINE VALVE shut and close the INLET VALVE. The barrel is now trapped between two shut steel gates.
  8. Open the TRAP DRAIN VALVE and let the slug go to the drains tank, then open the TRAP VENT VALVE and blow the barrel down to the flare. Both needles fall; when the trap gauge sits at zero and the pin drops, RING FREE lights.
  9. Shut the vent and the drain. Swing the CLAMP RING twice - CRACKED, then OPEN. The door swings. If you did not drain her, you will wear the slug, and LIQUID SPILL latches for the environmental report.
  10. Hold the PIG PULLER three seconds. She comes out. RECEIPT COMPLETE.
  11. SIGNALLER RESET drops both flags for the next run.
  12. To put her back on line: door shut, ring swung to LOCKED, inlet open, mainline geared open, kicker shut. Leave the paperwork in the tray.

Alarms

Window Meaning Action
POWER (green) Panel supply healthy. -
THROUGH BARREL (green) Inlet open, mainline past half, plant fed through the barrel. -
APPROACH (amber) Pig inside the last mile and a half, or S1 fired. Eyes on the drum; think about the vent.
PIG RECEIVED (green) Pig in the arrestor. Isolate: mainline shut, inlet shut.
RECEIPT COMPLETE (green) Pig out on the rack. Paperwork; restore the line.
RING FREE (green) Interlock pin out, barrel proved under 50 mbar. Ring may be swung.
LIQUIDS HIGH (amber) More than about three and a half cubic metres in the barrel. Open the drain before thinking about the door.
VENT TO FLARE (amber) Gas going up the stack, by hand or by relief. Expected while blowing down. If unexplained, find out why.
TRAP OVERPRESSURE (red) Barrel above 33.5 bar. Something is feeding it. Vent to flare at once; hunt the feed. The relief lifts at 35 to save the shell.
PIG STALLED (red) Pig stopped in the line with drive applied. See Faults and recovery.
SIGNALLER FAIL (amber) An S1/S2 head is not answering the poll. Run on the RANGE drum, then see Faults and recovery.
ISOLATION LEAK (red) Barrel pressure creeping with every valve shut. A seat is passing. See Faults and recovery.
ARRESTOR DAMAGE (red, latched) She arrived over fourteen kilometres an hour and bent the fins. Stop. Report. Fitters, then CONSOLE RESET.
LIQUID SPILL (red, latched) Door opened on liquid. Drain and bund work, report, then CONSOLE RESET.

Faults and recovery

A pig stalls in the line

What you see: the RANGE drum stops turning, the SPEED figure dies away, and inside a minute PIG STALLED flashes red with the beacon. The usual cause is liquids settling under her in a dip.

Recovery: confirm the barrel is on the line (THROUGH BARREL lit). Crack the TRAP VENT VALVE. Flaring drops the back pressure at the pipeline end, the differential across her builds, and she breaks free - the fault clears itself once she is moving again. Shut the vent as soon as the drum turns, let her coast, and repeat in pulses rather than riding the vent all the way home. Accept and reset the alarm.

An inlet valve passes

What you see: with the inlet, mainline, vent and drain all shut, the trap gauge climbs - ISOLATION LEAK flashes red. Logged as: inlet valve passing. Ignore it and the barrel will pack up to line pressure, the relief will lift to flare and the pin will stay in, locking the ring.

Recovery: the inlet seat is holding something off its face. Open the INLET VALVE smartly and shut it again - one full stroke. That usually clears the seat and the creep stops. Prove it: needles steady, leak timer quiet. Then carry on with the blowdown. If a single stroke does not cure it, vent to flare, keep the barrel low, and phone the mechanical desk; do not force the ring.

A trap inlet signaller fails

What you see: logged as trap inlet signaller failure. SIGNALLER FAIL flashes amber within the minute - the panel polls both heads continuously. Neither flag will rise, and APPROACH stays dark even as the drum counts down.

Recovery: run the approach on the RANGE drum alone and treat the last half kilometre as live: ease her with short vent pulses and take the arrival slow. Once the pig is in and things are quiet, press SIGNALLER RESET - it frees the stuck head and drops the flags. The amber goes out; accept and reset.

Specifications

Line 36 inch OD sales gas, wall 12.7 mm, 34 km from field manifold
Maximum operating pressure 70 bar (MAOP); field normal 38 bar, plant normal 27.5 bar
Receiver barrel 12 cubic metres water capacity, approx 6.1 m barrel length
Closure Cam-sector clamp ring, ratcheted tommy bar, falling-pin pressure interlock at 50 mbar
Arrestor Four-fin energy arrestor, capture limit 14 km/h
Relief Barrel PSV to flare, set 35 bar, modulating above 34.9
Overpressure alarm 33.5 bar on the trap gauge
Signallers S1 at 2.0 km, S2 at 0.5 km, polled continuously
Flare back pressure 60 mbar at the trap vent header
Drains tank 8 cubic metres, atmospheric, level by sight glass
Panel supply 240 V, 50 Hz, single phase, 6 A breaker
Indication Flat LED clusters; xenon rotating beacon; reflective LCD flow computer
Panel NVP/TRAP/R1, hot-dip galvanised, Issue 3, Redmoss instrument shop, 1994

ISSUE 3 supersedes Issue 2 entirely. The guarded test once marked TEST KICKER PASSING is re-marked TEST INLET PASSING, the fault being in the inlet seat; recorded here so nobody writes it on the fascia in grease pencil again.
