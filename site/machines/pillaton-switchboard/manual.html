GENERAL POST OFFICE · FACTORIES DEPARTMENT INSTRUCTION KE 1142

PILLATON MANUAL EXCHANGE
SWITCHBOARD POSITION No. 1

Operating instructions for the attendance of telephonists. Third edition, 1962. This book is the property of the Crown and is bolted to the position frame; it must not be removed from the exchange room.

Overview

Position No. 1 answers twelve subscribers' lines of the Pillaton 218 group by means of three cord pairs worked across a lamp-and-jack multiple. Ringing current is generated on the position by a motor-driven alternator with a flywheel; speech current is fed from the fifty-volt central battery through the position main fuse.

The telephonist's day is a contest of attention. Calls arrive whether or not the flywheel is up to speed; the common battery sags whenever too many circuits are worked at once; and the belt which couples the motor to the alternator wears exactly as fast as the traffic justifies. A call answered inside forty seconds never reaches the supervisor's ledger. One answered late does.

Everything on the position is worked by hand and judged by eye: cords are lifted from their wells and seated in the multiple, the lever keys are thrown while the plug is held, tickets are written for every connection, and the two flush meters tell at a glance whether the plant behind the panel is behaving itself.

Controls

The legends printed on the panel are listed here in working order, left bay to right bay.

RING GENERATOR SELECTOR

Bakelite rotary switch with pointer, two positions: OFF and RUN. RUN starts the motor and brings the flywheel toward its standing speed of about 1480 rpm. The amber neon beside it shows rectifier live. Keyboard: focus and press Enter to change over.

HAND CRANK

The knurled wheel below the selector. Press or hold (mouse or Space) to turn the flywheel by hand when the motor cannot. Cranking raises the wheel to about 1350 rpm; enough for ringing, not enough for a full day's work.

SPARE FUSE CLIP

Carrier holding the spare cartridge main fuse. If the main fuse blows, press the clip once to offer the spare and a second time to home it home into the live clips. Two presses, deliberate ones.

BELT TENSION LEVER

Quadrant lever marked SLACK and TIGHT. Only effective when the motor is afterwards restarted: see Faults and Recovery. Keyboard: Enter swings the lever.

THE MULTIPLE AND PAIR KEYS

The board carries twelve line columns, each with an ivory calling-lamp cap over its number strip, a FRONT (answering) jack and a REAR (calling) jack. Below the multiple lies the keyshelf:

  • CORD PAIR 1, CORD PAIR 2, CORD PAIR 3. Each station holds one red-braided answering plug and one black-braided calling plug in their wells. Click a plug (or focus it and press Enter) to lift it; click the wanted jack to seat it; click a seated plug or its socket to return it to the well. Answering plugs seat in FRONT jacks only, calling plugs in REAR jacks only.
  • PAIR KEYS. The oxblood paddle beside each pair, with three positions: STANDBY, TALK, RING. Operating the key steps it STANDBY → TALK → RING → STANDBY. TALK connects your speech to the answering side; RING sends ring current down the calling side provided the flywheel is above 900 rpm.

TICKET PAD

Paper pad at the right hand of the keyshelf. Every answer, connection and clearing is written here automatically in the order given; a position without tickets was not worked.

NIGHT SERVICE KEY

Toggle at the supervisor's corner. In DAY the calling lamps announce across the multiple; in NIGHT the lamps are suppressed and calls announce at the flashing NIGHT CALL lens instead. Keyboard: Space.

BUZZER CUT-OFF

Black pushbutton. Silences the night buzzer for forty-five seconds. It does not clear anything; it only buys quiet.

LAMPS TEST

Black pushbutton. Hold pressed: every lamp cap lights, both meter needles sweep their scales, and the neon strikes. Release and the panel returns to truth.

FAULT TEST SWITCHES

Three guarded switches at the bottom right, for maintenance proof only: GEN BELT SLIP, MAIN FUSE and CORD REVERSAL. Lift a guard, throw the switch, and the corresponding fault is imposed on the position exactly as if it had occurred in service. They are not to be operated during traffic without the supervisor's leave.

Normal operation

  1. Open the position cold. Confirm the alarm lenses are dark, the main fuse healthy, both plugs of all three pairs home in their wells, and all keys at STANDBY.
  2. Start the plant. Turn the RING GENERATOR SELECTOR to RUN. Watch the FLYWHEEL RPM meter come up to about 1480 and hold there. BUS VOLTS should stand near 50.
  3. Watch for arrivals. A calling subscriber lights her lamp cap steadily (in NIGHT service, the NIGHT CALL lens flashes instead).
  4. Answer. Lift the red-braided answering plug of any free pair and seat it in the FRONT jack of the calling line.
  5. Speak. Throw that pair's key to TALK. The caller states the number she wants; the ticket appears on the pad. Write nothing twice.
  6. Call out. Lift the black-braided calling plug of the same pair and seat it in the REAR jack of the wanted line.
  7. Ring. Throw the key to RING. Bell current passes while the flywheel has the speed; within a few seconds the far party answers and the connection is marked CONNECTED on the pad.
  8. Stand by. Return the key to STANDBY. The through conversation continues on the cords alone; the supervisor's counter records the call handled.
  9. Clear. When the two lamps flash together, the conversation has ended. Lift either seated plug; both lines fall idle. Return both plugs to their wells before taking fresh work.
  10. At night, operate the NIGHT SERVICE KEY before leaving the room, and let the buzzer do the watching.

Alarms

Lens Means Do
BATTERY VOLTS LOW Common battery below 44 V under load — too many circuits worked at once, or ringing heavily. Stop mass ringing, let conversations finish, clear spare cords. If ignored, sustained collapse below 38½ V blows the main fuse and the whole position falls dead.
MAIN FUSE BLOWN
(POWER FAIL lens)		 The position main fuse has cleared; lamps dark, meters fall, every call is lost. Fit the spare with the SPARE FUSE CLIP (two presses), then resume from step 2 of Normal operation.
BELT SLIP The slip detector counts motor revolutions against flywheel revolutions and they disagree: the drive belt is slipping. Recover per Faults and Recovery. Ringing will be weak or absent meanwhile.
REVERSED CORD PAIR The automatic pair test finds the pair re-terminated reversed: connections on it will howl instead of speaking. Take the affected pair out of service and prove it per Faults and Recovery.
CALLS UNANSWERED A calling lamp has burned more than forty seconds. Answer now. At fifty-five seconds the caller abandons and the matter leaves your hands.
SUPERVISOR COMPLAINT An abandonment has been recorded in the ledger. Note the ticket, work briskly; the complaint cools in ninety seconds if no further call is lost.
RING FAILURE A key was thrown to RING with the flywheel below 900 rpm, or the battery dead. Crank by hand or wait for the motor to recover speed, then ring again.
NIGHT CALL Night service: a subscriber is calling. Wake, light the room, answer as in daylight.

Faults and recovery

Fault 1 — generator belt slip

You see: the BELT SLIP lens; the RPM needle sagging toward 700 and hunting; ringing that does not carry; RING FAILURE when keys are thrown against a slow wheel.

  1. Turn the RING GENERATOR SELECTOR to OFF and let the wheel run down.
  2. Swing the BELT TENSION LEVER from SLACK to TIGHT.
  3. Return the selector to RUN. Within four seconds the detector agrees with itself, the lens goes dark, and the needle climbs to its old standing.

Until recovered, the HAND CRANK will furnish ringing current for one call at a time.

Fault 2 — battery fuse blown

You see: the POWER FAIL lens and the MAIN FUSE BLOWN annunciator; both meters falling to zero; every lamp dark; all calls on the position lost at the instant of the blow.

  1. Press the SPARE FUSE CLIP once — the carrier rises offering the spare.
  2. Press again to home the spare into the live clips.
  3. BUS VOLTS returns toward 50; the lenses clear; take fresh traffic. Do not work six circuits and ring at once immediately afterwards — a second blow follows a second greed.

Fault 3 — reversed cord pair

You see: the REVERSED CORD PAIR lens, raised by the automatic pair test whether or not the pair is in use; if the pair is worked, the caller reports a howler and no speech — the ticket is marked HOWLER. Pair 2 is the pair under suspicion.

  1. Lift both plugs of the suspect pair and return them fully home to their wells.
  2. Work that pair's key from STANDBY to TALK and back to STANDBY. The reversal relay restores and the lens goes dark.
  3. Hand the pair to maintenance at the next quiet minute; the re-termination at the plug end wants soldering properly.
