TYPE 9/F PROCESS COMPUTER
OPERATING MANUAL

PUBLICATION 9F‑MAN‑003 · ISSUE 2 · MARCH 1974

For operators and maintenance engineers. Read Section 3 before setting the machine to work. This manual is bolted to the cabinet door; leave it there.

Overview

The Type 9/F is a twelve-bit digital process computer with 4096 words of magnetic-core store. It was built to stand in a factory annex and hold a paper mill, a substation telemetry scheme or a chemical batch on its own for weeks at a time. Everything the operator needs is on the front panel: the register lamps that show what the processor holds, four nixie clusters that read out address, memory buffer and program output in octal, two banks of twelve paddle switches, and the keys that load, examine, deposit, start and stop her.

The resident diagnostic sweeps odd words for primality and posts each prime it finds to the PROGRAM OUTPUT cluster; she will run it for as long as you care to watch. The machine asks little of you: keep the +5 V rail inside its green arc, keep the core stack out of its amber band, and answer the horn when she raises one of the six annunciators. The CLOCK RATE knob is a bargain struck between speed and heat — every tenth of a megahertz above the nominal mark costs you degrees, and the core remembers every degree you spend.

Controls

Control Type Action
MAINS BREAKER guarded toggle Lift the clear guard and throw to ON to energise the supply. Throwing to OFF strips all power.
MODE SELECTOR rotary, five positions OFF dead; STANDBY powered, clocked but idle; LOAD for depositing core by hand; RUN frees the clock; MAINT for single-step work.
CLOCK RATE rotary fine-rate knob Sets the beat, 0·2–1·6 MHz. Arrows or wheel work it from the keyboard. Nominal mark 1·2.
ADDRESS SWITCHES twelve paddles Sets an address word. Paddle up is a ONE. Bit 11 is at the left.
DATA SWITCHES twelve paddles Sets a data word for DEPOSIT.
ADDRESS LOAD key Takes the address switches into the address register; the ADDRESS nixie follows.
EXAMINE key Reads core at the addressed word into the MEMORY BUFFER display.
DEPOSIT key Writes the data switches into core at the addressed word, then steps the address on by one.
CLEAR key Zeros accumulator, multiplier-quotient and link. Also purges a memory scramble and restores the protected core image.
START key Frees the clock at the loaded address. Lives only in RUN mode, and refuses while a trip stands.
STOP key Holds the clock. The HALT jewel lights when the processor stands.
SINGLE STEP key One instruction per press, in RUN or MAINT.
BLOWER guarded toggle The core-stack cooling fan. Cycling it OFF then ON restarts a tripped motor.
ALARM ACKNOWLEDGE key Silences the horn and steadies the flashing lenses. Accepts the thermal trip once she has cooled.
LAMPS TEST key, hold down While held, every lamp, lens and nixie digit lights. Hold for two seconds each shift.
MAINTENANCE TESTS hinged tray Opens the fault-injector tray: PARITY TEST, DC LOAD TEST and BLOWER TRIP TEST latching switches and the PARITY CLEAR key.

Normal operation

  1. Inspect: MODE SELECTOR at OFF, MAINS BREAKER OFF and guarded, BLOWER ON.
  2. Lift the breaker guard and throw MAINS BREAKER ON. The POWER jewel lights and the DC LOW lens shows while the rails rise; it must go dark within three seconds.
  3. Turn MODE SELECTOR to STANDBY and listen for the blower. AIRFLOW LOSS must stay dark.
  4. Set CLOCK RATE to the nominal mark, 1·2 MHz.
  5. Set the ADDRESS SWITCHES to the diagnostic entry, octal 7600: paddles 11, 10, 9 up; the rest down. The factory leaves them set thus.
  6. Press ADDRESS LOAD. The ADDRESS nixie reads 7600.
  7. Press EXAMINE once to confirm core reads back, then press CLEAR.
  8. Turn MODE SELECTOR to RUN and press START. The RUN jewel lights, the register lamps dance, and primes begin to post on PROGRAM OUTPUT.
  9. After five minutes, read the meters: +5 V RAIL in its green arc between 4·9 and 5·3 volts; CORE STACK settling in the mid-fifties with OVERTEMPERATURE dark.
  10. To stop for the night: press STOP, turn MODE SELECTOR to STANDBY, and leave the blower running until CORE STACK falls below forty degrees. Then MODE to OFF and throw MAINS BREAKER OFF.

Alarms

Lens Meaning Immediate action
PARITY ERROR A core word failed its sweep check. She halts where she stood and the halt address sits in the ADDRESS nixie. Silence the horn, go to Section 5, parity recovery. She does not restart herself.
DC LOW +5 V rail out of its green arc, on load or on test. Check the meter. If the rail is genuinely low, shed load: CLOCK RATE down a mark, or STOP. Ignored, she scrambles memory.
OVERTEMPERATURE Core stack past 65 °C and climbing. Confirm blower running, CLOCK RATE back towards 0·6. If ignored she trips herself at 80 °C.
THERMAL TRIP Core stack reached 80 °C; the clock has been stripped and she is halted. Let her cool below 55 °C, then press ALARM ACKNOWLEDGE to rearm, and restart per Section 3.
AIRFLOW LOSS The plenum switch feels no air: blower tripped, or thrown off with the machine powered. Cycle the BLOWER breaker OFF then ON. If the lens holds, do not run the clock; call maintenance.
MEMORY SCRAMBLE The rail sagged too long and core wrote badly. Registers are refuse and she is halted. MODE to STANDBY, press CLEAR to restore the protected core image, acknowledge, then repeat the start-up procedure from paragraph 6.

A new alarm flashes until accepted; accepted alarms burn steady until their condition clears. The horn runs only while a flashing lens stands. Jewels: POWER amber, BLOWER ivory, RUN green, HALT red — indicators, not alarms.

Faults and recovery

Core memory parity error

You see: PARITY ERROR flashing, the horn sounding, HALT jewel lit and the RUN jewel dark. The ADDRESS nixie holds the failing word. The machine will not restart until the error is cleared — this is deliberate; a doubtful word is worse than a stopped one.

  1. Press ALARM ACKNOWLEDGE to steady the lens and silence the horn.
  2. If the PARITY TEST switch in the maintenance tray stands latched, release it.
  3. Turn MODE SELECTOR to MAINT.
  4. Press PARITY CLEAR. The lens goes dark and the failing bit is rewritten.
  5. Return MODE SELECTOR to RUN and press START. She resumes at the diagnostic entry.

Power supply undervoltage

You see: DC LOW flashing and the +5 V needle fallen out of its green arc. If the sag stands below 4·55 volts for some eighteen seconds the MEMORY SCRAMBLE lens joins it, the registers fill with refuse and she halts.

  1. Release the DC LOAD TEST switch if it stands latched in the maintenance tray.
  2. Shed load: CLOCK RATE down a mark, or press STOP, until the needle sits in its green arc.
  3. Acknowledge DC LOW once the rail holds 4·9 or better for a minute.
  4. If MEMORY SCRAMBLE lit: MODE to STANDBY, press CLEAR — the protected core image is restored — acknowledge, then repeat Section 3 from paragraph 6.

Blower failure

You see: AIRFLOW LOSS at once, then CORE STACK climbing through the amber band; OVERTEMPERATURE follows, and at 80 °C she trips herself and halts with THERMAL TRIP burning.

  1. Throw the guarded BLOWER breaker OFF, count three, throw it ON. The motor restarts and AIRFLOW LOSS goes dark.
  2. Release the BLOWER TRIP TEST switch if it stands latched in the maintenance tray.
  3. Hold CLOCK RATE at or below the nominal mark until CORE STACK falls under 55 °C.
  4. Press ALARM ACKNOWLEDGE to rearm the thermal trip, then press START.
  5. If the motor will not turn, leave her stopped and call the Ferriday service engineer. Do not run the clock on a dead plenum.

Note on the injector: the test switches drive the detector lines upstream of the DC interlock, which is why a latched test shows its lens even on a cold machine. They are for proving the alarm chain at each shift handover, not for amusing visitors.

Specifications

Word length 12 bits
Store 4096 words, magnetic core, 3 µs full cycle
Clock 0·2–1·6 MHz, nominal 1·2 MHz
Instruction rate approximately 133 000 operations per second at nominal
Supplies +5 V 6 A regulated; rails must hold 4·9–5·3 V on load
Core stack limits OVERTEMPERATURE at 65 °C; thermal trip at 80 °C; rearm below 55 °C
Cooling one 230 V plenum blower, airflow sensed at the stack
Mains 200–250 V ~ 50 Hz, single phase, 380 VA
Room conditions 5–40 °C, 20–80% relative humidity, free of grit
Cabinet crinkle-enamelled steel, 1850 ×760 ×640 mm, 295 lb
Diagnostic entry octal 07600, resident, protected against scramble
