Sallowfield Explosives Co. Ltd — Works Instruction Book

Nitration House No. 3
Operating Book for the Watch Bench

Issue 4 · August 1927 · To be kept in the cabinet at the bench · Read before touching a valve

Section 1Overview

This house nitrates glycerine for the cordite plant. Mixed acid stands in the nitrator, nine hundred and fifty pounds of it; glycerine is run in through the drip bulb while air lifted from the compressor keeps the charge moving and brine through the jacket carries the heat of reaction away. A made charge is about one hundred and twenty pounds of glycerine. When the feed is done the charge is left to settle, the spent acid is drawn from below, and the product goes forward to the wash house in the runaway pots.

The whole art of the house is temperature. Feed gently and she sits at seventy-two to seventy-five degrees Fahrenheit for as long as you like. Feed greedily, lose your brine, or let the air lift die, and the mercury climbs — and nitroglycerine does not argue. The drowning tank beneath the pot is kept full of water for one purpose only: the DUMP LEVER drops the entire charge into it in four seconds. No man was ever blamed for pulling her too soon.

Section 2Controls

Control Fitting Action and positions
AIR COMPRESSOR SWITCH Knife switch, slate base Lifts the blade onto the contacts. OFF, blade open; ON, blade home. Start this first — nothing else works without air.
AGITATION AIR VALVE Regulating rotary, brass handwheel Throttles air to the lift pipe. SHUT to five turns; two turns is the working charge. Watch the receiver gauge hold forty-five pounds.
GLYCERINE FEED VALVE Multi-turn needle valve Ease open in quarter turns, counting the feathered spindle. SHUT, then up to four turns. One and a half turns feeds a steady charge.
BRINE COCK Quarter-turn plug cock SHUT across the line; OPEN along it. Full open before the first drop of glycerine, always.
SPENT ACID COCK Quarter-turn plug cock Draws spent acid from beneath the settled charge. SHUT until the sight glass shows the split. Never open against an unsettle charge.
VENT DAMPER Slide damper on the stack breech SHUT to OPEN on the slot. Full open gives near half an inch of water on the draught gauge.
DUMP LEVER Brass T-lever under oak cover, lead sealed First press breaks the seal and lifts the cover; second press pulls the lever and blows the bottom plug. Whole charge to the drowning tank.
ALARM BELL CUT Latching push Silences the gong after you have seen what it is saying. It does not mend anything.
FLAGS RESET Push by the flag strip Flips tripped roundels back to edge-on once the condition behind them is truly gone.
FLAG PROOF Push, momentary Shows every roundel face-on so you know none is stuck shut. Use it at the start of every shift.
TEST BRINE LOSS Sealed test switch Puts the house into brine failure without touching a pipe. For proving the flags and the wits of new men.
TEST AIR FAILURE Sealed test switch Bleeds the air line as if the compressor had thrown its belt.
TEST FEED OVERRUN Sealed test switch Makes the feed valve pass glycerine regardless of its setting.
RESTORE BRINE Fitters' push, maintenance tray Returns the brine circuit to service after a loss. Clears the fault the test switch made.
FREE AGITATOR Fitters' push, maintenance tray Clears the air line and puts the lift back on its beat.
RESET FEED LIMITER Fitters' push, maintenance tray Re-seats the feed valve gland so she holds her setting again.
RESET CHARGE Fitters' push, maintenance tray Re-arms the house: fresh acid standing, valves shut, flags down, dump re-latched and sealed. Used after a dump or a completed batch.

Section 3Normal operation

From cold, acid standing, everything shut and the flags edge-on:

  1. Press FLAG PROOF and satisfy yourself every roundel shows; press again to return them. Press FLAGS RESET to clear any stragglers.
  2. Put the AIR COMPRESSOR SWITCH ON and watch the receiver rise. Do not proceed under thirty-five pounds; work at forty-five.
  3. Open the VENT DAMPER full. The draught gauge should stand near half an inch and breathe easy.
  4. Open the AGITATION AIR VALVE two turns. Bubbles should break evenly over the bottom of the pot.
  5. Open the BRINE COCK. The flow sight must run hard before a drop of glycerine goes in.
  6. Begin feeding: ease the GLYCERINE FEED VALVE out a quarter turn at a time. Drips begin at half a turn.
  7. Trim feed against brine to hold the thread between 72 and 75 °F. If she drifts up, shut in a quarter turn before you reach for anything else.
  8. At one hundred and twenty pounds fed, shut the feed valve smartly. Leave the services running and let the charge settle — the sight glass will show the pale product standing over darkening acid.
  9. When the split is clean, open the SPENT ACID COCK and draw the acid layer down to the product line, then shut it. The batch is complete; hand the pots to the wash house.
  10. Press RESET CHARGE to re-arm the bench for the next batch, and write the time in the book.

At 79 °F the TEMP HIGH roundel trips and the gong speaks. At 86 °F you pull the DUMP LEVER — you do not debate it, you do not try one more quarter turn. Twenty-five seconds above eighty-six and the house decides for itself.

Section 4Alarms

Roundel Meaning What you do
TEMP HIGH Charge at 79 °F and rising matters. Brine losing the race against the feed. Shut the feed valve a quarter turn at a time, confirm the BRINE COCK is full open and the flow sight runs, confirm bubbles. If the thread passes 86, pull the DUMP LEVER.
AIR LOW Receiver under thirty-two pounds. Compressor off its belt, or the line leaking. Cut the feed at once — without agitation your brine is cooling one corner while the rest cooks. Send for the fitter; FREE AGITATOR when he has it clear.
BRINE LOW No water in the jacket while feeding — cock closed or the circuit failed. Stop the feed. If the cock is shut, open it and watch the sight. If the circuit has failed and the thread still climbs, dump before 86 and be glad of it.
FEED EXCESS The valve is passing more than the orifice allows — a stripped gland or an overrun. Shut the feed valve fully. If the drips keep falling anyway, that is the overrun; RESET FEED LIMITER after the batch is safe, and treat the charge as a hot one meanwhile.
DRAUGHT LOST Fumes not leaving while you feed. Damper shut or the stack fouled. Open the VENT DAMPER. Acid mist in the house eats men as surely as it eats iron.
FUMED OUT The office interlock has stopped the feed — the house has had enough. Vent first. When the draught gauge proves and the mist clears, FLAGS RESET releases the lockout and you may feed again, slower.
CHARGE DUMPED The drowning tank has taken the charge. Nothing to save. Shut every valve, silence the gong, send for the foreman, and RESET CHARGE when he says so.
HOUSE LOST The charge went up in the pot. You are reading this from the wrong side of a very bad morning. RESET CHARGE rebuilds the bench for training purposes only.

Section 5Faults and recovery

Brine cooling loss

You see: the brine flow sight falls slack, the jacket return stops steaming, and the thread begins to walk upward whatever the feed is doing. BRINE LOW trips within the minute.

Recovery: stop the feed at once and hold the charge where it is with what cooling remains. If she is already past 82 °F, dump — acid can be mixed again. Otherwise send the fitter to the cooler; when the circuit is made good, press RESTORE BRINE, prove the flow sight running, then FLAGS RESET, and take the feed up again a quarter turn at a time. A test-switch fault clears the same way: switch the test off, or simply press RESTORE BRINE.

Agitation air failure

You see: bubbles thin and stop, the receiver needle sags toward thirty, AIR LOW trips, and the charge begins to stratify — hot pockets with cold between.

Recovery: cut the feed dead; a still charge cannot be cooled honestly. Check the compressor switch and listen at the line. Press FREE AGITATOR when the obstruction is cleared, bring the receiver back to forty-five, prove the bubbles even across the pot, FLAGS RESET, and feed again slowly — the charge may have hot pockets yet, so expect the thread to bump.

Glycerine feed overrun

You see: drips continuing with the feed valve shut hard, FEED EXCESS tripped, the pot level creeping and the thread climbing faster than the feed you believe you are giving.

Recovery: open the brine wide, keep the lift full on, and do not fight the valve — it has lost its gland and will not hold. Hold her under 79 with cooling alone if you can; if not, dump without regret. After the batch is safe, press RESET FEED LIMITER to re-seat the gland, prove her holding at SHUT, then FLAGS RESET.
