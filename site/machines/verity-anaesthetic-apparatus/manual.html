Overview

This apparatus delivers oxygen and nitrous oxide, with halothane vapour when required, to a patient breathing on a circle circuit. It was designed, machined and assembled in Sheffield by men who make surgical steel for a living, and it will outlast the theatre that houses it if you treat it as it asks: keep the floats on their lines, keep the bag moving, and never ignore a red lamp.

Cylinders feed yokes at the left of the panel. Needle valves meter each gas through glass flow meters; the vapouriser bleeds a calibrated fraction of the fresh gas through halothane and returns it to the circuit. The patient's expired breath passes back through soda lime, which strips carbon dioxide; the rebreathing bag and the adjustable pop-off valve set the pressure under which the whole circle breathes.

The mains switch feeds only the lamps, the two patient meters and the whistle cut-out. Gas flows by pressure alone: the machine will run its pneumatics quite happily with the electricity off, which is precisely why you are given eyes instead of dials.

Controls

Control Kind Action
OXYGEN NEEDLE VALVE Multi-turn needle valve Sets oxygen flow, 0–10 L/min. Turn by dragging the knob, spinning the wheel over it, or with the arrow keys. The red scribe on the tube marks 2 L/min.
NITROUS OXIDE NEEDLE VALVE Multi-turn needle valve Sets nitrous oxide flow, 0–10 L/min. A cut-off provision drops this flow automatically whenever the oxygen supply fails.
HALOTHANE PERCENT DIAL Calibrated dial, 0–4% Selects the vapouriser output. Turning fully to zero re-seats the vapour seal after a leak. The small amber jewel beside the dial glows while the seal is open to atmosphere.
POP-OFF VALVE Adjustable relief valve Sets airway relief from about 46 cmH₂O fully closed to 2 cmH₂O wide open. For spontaneous breathing leave relief near 10 cmH₂O.
YOKE CHANGEOVER LEVER Two-position lever Selects DUTY or RESERVE oxygen cylinder. Move to RESERVE whenever the duty cylinder is spent.
MAINS SWITCH Toggle Powers the lamps, patient meters and whistle circuits. On does not mean flowing: watch the tubes, not the toggle.
EMERGENCY OXYGEN FLUSH Momentary button Floods the circuit with roughly 12 L/min of oxygen for about a second and a half. Use to refill a collapsed bag or to wash out vapour quickly.
CANISTER SWING BOLTS Twin quarter-turn bolts Open both bolts a quarter turn together and the spent soda-lime charge swings out and a fresh one seats itself. Close by letting both bolts spring home.
WORKS TEST SWITCHES Three guarded toggles Factory test gear behind the drawer front: drains the duty oxygen supply, opens the vapour seal, or floods the canister with spent soda lime. Used for drills only, and labelled accordingly.
ALARM WHISTLE CUTOUT Latching button Silences the oxygen-failure whistle once acknowledged. The lamp stays lit until the fault is cleared.
SERVICE RESET Button (in drawer) Returns the apparatus to cold: valves shut, dial at zero, cylinders charged, alarms dark. The theatre equivalent of starting the shift again.

Normal operation

  1. Stand at the left of the trolley. Check both cylinder gauges: duty above 40 bar, reserve full. If the duty gauge reads low now, change over before you begin.
  2. Switch MAINS ON. The green pilot jewel should light and both patient meters should wake.
  3. Open the OXYGEN NEEDLE VALVE until the black float sits level with the red scribe: 2 L/min. Do this within the first minute; an empty circuit desaturates a patient faster than a kettle boils.
  4. Add nitrous oxide on the second valve to 4 L/min. The mixture is then one third oxygen. Never chase a lighter plane of anaesthesia by starving the oxygen line.
  5. Set the HALOTHANE PERCENT DIAL to 1.5%. Watch the delivered figure settle in the window below one percent as the circuit washes in.
  6. Set the POP-OFF VALVE to give relief near 10 cmH₂O — about seven turns from closed — so the bag moves freely with each breath.
  7. Confirm the REBREATHING BAG rises and falls at twelve or so breaths a minute, the DEPTH meter rides inside the green surgical band, and SATURATION holds above 95%. Note the canister window shows FRESH.
  8. You are now running. Every ten minutes glance at the canister colour; violet means change it before the CO₂ lamp speaks for it.

Alarms

Lamp Meaning What to do
MAINS Mains present. Not a fault; a courtesy. No action.
OXYGEN FAILURE Duty supply lost below 15 bar. The nitrous cut-off has already dropped the second gas, and the gas-powered whistle is sounding. Acknowledge with the ALARM WHISTLE CUTOUT, throw the YOKE CHANGEOVER LEVER to RESERVE, re-establish 2 L/min of oxygen, then restore nitrous.
VAPOUR LOSS The vapour seal monitor finds agent escaping the vapouriser body. Turn the HALOTHANE PERCENT DIAL fully to zero to re-seat the seal, wait for the amber jewel to darken, then bring the setting back gradually.
CO₂ REBREATH Soda lime exhausted; the patient is breathing his own carbon dioxide. Operate the CANISTER SWING BOLTS to swap in a fresh charge. End-tidal figures fall within half a minute.
AIRWAY PRESSURE Relief above 34 cmH₂O; the pop-off has been closed down on a breathing patient. Open the POP-OFF VALVE until pressure falls below 30 cmH₂O. Ignored, this becomes barotrauma and a crisis.
PATIENT CRISIS Saturation below 78%, end-tidal CO₂ above 14, depth beyond 4.6%, or pressure held dangerously high. The lamp flashes because your eye should. Fix the cause first: oxygen on, vapour off, pop-off open, flush as needed. The lamp steadies and goes out only when the patient is truly out of danger. SERVICE RESET ends the shift entirely.

Faults and recovery

Rule of the house: every fault below may be drilled without risk using the WORKS TEST SWITCHES in the lower drawer. Practise each recovery until your hands know it before your memory does.
Fault What you see Recovery
oxygen cylinder depletion DUTY gauge falls fast; OXYGEN FAILURE lights; the whistle sounds; the nitrous float sinks to zero of its own accord; saturation begins to slide. Press ALARM WHISTLE CUTOUT. Throw the YOKE CHANGEOVER LEVER to RESERVE. Re-open the OXYGEN NEEDLE VALVE to 2 L/min, confirm saturation recovering, then restore nitrous oxide and vapour.
vaporizer leak Amber VAPOUR LOSS jewel and lamp; delivered percent falls away although the dial still reads its figure; the surgical band drifts light. Close the HALOTHANE PERCENT DIAL fully to zero — this re-seats the seal and puts the jewel out. Re-select the working percentage and let the circuit wash back in.
soda lime exhaustion Canister window turns violet and reads EXHAUSTED; CO₂ REBREATH lights; end-tidal climbs past 30 mmHg toward 5%. Open both CANISTER SWING BOLTS a quarter turn together; the charge swings clear and a fresh one seats. Confirm the window reads FRESH and end-tidal falls.

If a drill leaves the apparatus untidy, SERVICE RESET returns everything to cold and alarm-free without touching the mains lead.

Specifications

Gas supplies
Oxygen cylinders Two yokes, duty and reserve; 122–132 bar charged; fail-safe below 15 bar
Flow ranges Oxygen and nitrous oxide each 0–10 L/min, tapered-glass meters, steel bobbins
Vapouriser
Agent Halothane, temperature-compensated draw-over plenum
Dial range 0–4.0% in tenths; saturated output diluted by fresh-gas flow
Seal monitor Back-pressure comparison; amber indication and lamp on loss of seal
Circle circuit
Canister Soda lime, nominal life 35 minutes at ordinary breathing; mica-slot colour window
Pop-off relief 2–46 cmH₂O; alarm at 34 cmH₂O
Bag 2 litres, leather-edged rubber
Flush Emergency oxygen, approx. 12 L/min for 1.5 s per press
Patient instruments
Depth meter Alveolar halothane, 0–4%; green surgical band 0.75–1.3%
Saturation meter Ear-piece oximeter, 50–100%; red below 90%
End-tidal CO₂ Displayed numerically in state; alarm on rebreathing
Electrical
Mains 230 V, lamps and meters only; pneumatics unaffected by power failure
Whistle Gas-powered, sounds without electricity; silenced by the ALARM WHISTLE CUTOUT
