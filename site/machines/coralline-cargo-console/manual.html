Overview

This console governs discharge of crude cargo from the three centre tanks of the motor tanker Coralline — 212,000 tonnes deadweight, Helsingør build, 1972 — to a shore terminal through two isolation valves, a common suction header and three steam-turbine cargo pumps.

The plant is a whole, not a collection of gauges. What you take out of a tank moves the ship's weight; where the weight sits bends the hull; how hard the pumps work raises the pressure in the shore line; and the fire that does not happen is kept from happening by the inert gas plant, whose generator holds the tank atmosphere under 8 % oxygen. The console exists so that one officer, standing here with coffee in hand, can see all of these quantities argue with each other — and settle them before the shore does it for you.

Three defects are provided for on the maintenance plate: a fouled strainer on No. 2 pump (which cavitates her once she is worked), a jammed hydraulic actuator on valve V3, and failure of the inert gas generator. Each has its recovery procedure in Faults and recovery. Ignore what the annunciator tells you at your peril: an unaccepted alarm left ringing becomes a tripped pump, and a red lamp left burning long enough becomes a bent ship.

Controls

TANK VENT selector (VENT SELECTOR)
Three-position rotary: CLOSED seals the tanks; INERTED admits inert gas from the generator and holds the oxygen down; VENTED opens the tanks to atmosphere for cleaning or entry. Discharge is only permitted with the selector at INERTED.
SEALINE VALVE
Quarter-turn knob on the mimic. Isolates the shore hose from the ship's lines. Open (knob index across the line, cell lit) before any pump is turned up.
RISER VALVE
Quarter-turn knob. The ship's manifold valve between the header and the sealine. Both this and the sealine must be open or the line chokes and the pressure climbs.
TANK V1 SUCTION · TANK V3 SUCTION · TANK V5 SUCTION
Three quarter-turn knobs beneath the tank sight-glasses, one per centre tank. Every open tank shares the flow equally; strip them evenly or answer to the stress card.
PUMP 1 THROTTLE · PUMP 2 THROTTLE · PUMP 3 THROTTLE
Notched lever and quadrant per cargo pump, 0–10 turns of steam. Ease them up in stages; a pump slammed open against a cold line will be heard in the vibration bar.
BALLAST SELECTOR (BALLAST SELECTOR)
HOLD leaves the ballast as it lies. AUTO TRIM pumps ballast fore and aft automatically against the bending moment, within ±72 % capacity.
ALARM ACCEPT
Ivory mushroom, bottom shelf. Stops the buzzer and pins a flashing window steady while its condition lasts.
LAMPS TEST
Lights every annunciator window for inspection. Hold-and-release; restores itself.
PUMP RESET
Re-arms a tripped cargo pump after its cause has been found and cured.
FAULT TEST SWITCHES (maintenance plate)
Behind the yellow-and-black header, authorised persons only: P2 STRAINER, V3 ACTUATOR JAM and I.G. GEN FAIL inject the three standing defects. Return all switches to normal after use.
CONSOLE RESET
Returns panel and plant to the cold, arrived-loaded condition. Used after a latched event has been investigated and cleared by the chief officer.
EMERGENCY DROP VALVE
Red mushroom under a yellow guard. Lift the guard, then strike DROP: every cargo valve shuts and every pump trips. For loss of hose, fire at the manifold, or any emergency faster than thought.

Normal operation

  1. Console cold: all valves shut, throttles at zero, vent selector CLOSED, ballast HOLD, no windows lit. Read the tank glasses — each centre tank should show 3149 m³.
  2. Turn the TANK VENT selector to INERTED. Confirm the green IG RUNNING jewel lights and the O2 gauge reads below 5 % and falling.
  3. Set the BALLAST SELECTOR to AUTO TRIM.
  4. Open SEALINE VALVE, then RISER VALVE. The amber SHORE LINKED jewel lights when both are made.
  5. Open the tank suction valves V1, V3 and V5 — all three, so the stripping stays even.
  6. Ease PUMP 1 THROTTLE up to about 5 on the quadrant and watch the shore line pressure settle near 2–3 bar. Then bring up PUMP 2 and PUMP 3 the same way.
  7. Take the total flow to whatever the terminal allows — three pumps at full gate give about 34 m³/min and some 9 bar. Keep the stress pointer in the white; auto trim will hold it there while the middle tank runs down ahead of the wings.
  8. As each tank strips below 8 % the LEVEL LOW window warns you; shut that tank's suction valve and let the other two carry, easing their throttles if the vibration bar creeps.
  9. When all three tanks read empty, close V1/V3/V5, throttle the pumps to zero, close RISER then SEALINE, return the vent selector to CLOSED and the ballast to HOLD.
  10. Accept the final alarms, note the drum counter, and log the berth against Manual M-117 page 44.

Alarms

Window Meaning Action
OXYGEN HIGH Tank atmosphere above 8 % O2. Suspend discharge; verify IG RUNNING; if the generator has failed see Faults. Do not pump with air in the tanks.
LINE PRESSURE Shore line above 8½ bar — usually a valve shut the wrong way. Check SEALINE and RISER open. At 10 bar the pumps trip themselves; find the closure, press PUMP RESET, ease up again.
CAVITATION A pump is starving or her strainer is fouling — vibration bar climbing. Throttle back the offending pump, close up on an empty-tank valve, or clear the strainer (see Faults).
LEVEL LOW An open suction is drawing on less than 8 % in its tank. Shut that tank's valve; redistribute the throttles.
STRESS HIGH Bending moment beyond ±6000 tm. Open a wing tank suction or check AUTO TRIM. Beyond ±7500 tm held forty seconds the hull suffers — permanently.
VALVE JAM V3 actuator stalled half-open. See Faults and recovery.
HULL DAMAGE Overstress held too long. Latched. All pumps tripped, valves shut. Investigate, then CONSOLE RESET.
VAPOUR EVENT Pumped against air in the tanks; flammable mixture found a spark. Latched. Inert the tanks, restore the atmosphere, then CONSOLE RESET.

Faults and recovery

  1. Cargo pump cavitation (P2 STRAINER test switch). What you see: the fouling builds over about ten seconds, the CAVITATION window lights, No. 2 pump's flow dial sags and, once her throttle passes half, the vibration bar climbs. Left alone at high gate she trips inside two minutes.
    Recovery: throttle PUMP 2 back below 40 %, return the P2 STRAINER switch to NORMAL, and stand by while the strainer clears itself — health returns over roughly half a minute. The alarm drops out as the differential falls. Then open her up again gently.
  2. Valve actuator jam (V3 ACTUATOR JAM test switch). What you see: the VALVE JAM window lights at once; V3 sits jammed half-open (its knob shows the jam ring), so No. 3 tank gives only half her share and the header runs short if you rely on her alone.
    Recovery: return the V3 ACTUATOR JAM switch to NORMAL to restore oil pressure to the actuator, then cycle V3 — press it closed, press it open again — to reseat the gate. Confirm the drop highlight flows and the share evens out.
  3. Inert gas generator failure (I.G. GEN FAIL test switch). What you see: the IG RUNNING jewel goes dark; with the vent selector at INERTED the O2 gauge now climbs about one point every seven seconds. The OXYGEN HIGH window lights at 8 %; if you go on pumping past 13 % the VAPOUR EVENT follows and the plant shuts herself down — latched.
    Recovery: suspend discharge (throttles to zero), return the I.G. GEN FAIL switch to NORMAL, keep the vent selector at INERTED and watch the oxygen fall back below 5 %. If the vapour event latched, investigate, then CONSOLE RESET and begin again from Normal operation step 1.

Specifications

Cargo pumps 3 × steam turbine, 11.5 m³/min each at full gate
Centre tanks served No.1 fore, No.3 middle, No.5 aft — 3280 m³ each, 96 % loaded on arrival
Total discharge rate 34 m³/min maximum, terminal limited
Shore line pressure 2.2 bar + 0.075 bar per m³/min; alarm 8.5 bar; trip 10 bar
Tank atmosphere Inert gas, O2 ≤ 5 % working, 8 % limit, 13 % hazardous when pumping
Hull bending moment Working limits ±6000 tm; damage threshold ±7500 tm held 40 s
Ballast trim ±72 % capacity, automatic trim against the bending moment
Cargo valves Hydraulically actuated, quarter-turn indication, amber film cells
Ambient conditions Bridge wing, daylight lit; console lighting by incandescent jewel and backlit film only
Panel supply 220 V DC ships' service, 50 V annunciator bus
