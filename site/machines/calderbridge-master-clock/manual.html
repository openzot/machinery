Overview

The Calderbridge Master Clock No. 1 is a gravity-driven pendulum of the two-seconds beat, housed in an ebonised case and wound weekly by hand. At every beat her driving contact closes; at every minute her count wheel closes the impulse line for half a second, and every slave dial connected to the board advances one minute. Forty dials in four circuits — Post Office, Town Hall, Police and Schools — hang their time entirely upon this instrument.

She asks three things of her keeper: that the driving weight be kept up, that the primary cells be kept above their working pressure, and that the rate be corrected little and often. A pendulum starved of impulse does not argue; she stops, and the borough stops with her. The board before you carries the meters, the knife switches, the fuses and the drop-flag annunciators by which her health is read at a glance. Read it every visit. Correct what it tells you the same hour. The town will not wait.

Controls

WINDING CRANK
On the trunk below the movement. Hold the crank through its arc and the ratchet lifts the driving weight; the drum wheel turns as she rises. A full wind carries roughly three hours' running. Never wind against a stopped pendulum without first giving her the start lever.
PENDULUM START LEVER
A small brass lever beside the winding boss. One pull gives the bob a gentle departure. Use it after any stoppage, and never use a finger through the hood door — the suspension is not to be touched.
RATING KNOB
Knurled knob under the dial, graduated SLOW to FAST, six seconds a day either way. One step alters the day's rate by one second. Move her only after comparing the master against Greenwich over a full hour.
HANDS KNOB
Silvers the master hands forward thirty seconds at each turn. Forward only — the mechanism cannot be turned back without injury. To bring a fast clock to time, open the circuits and let true time overtake her.
BATTERY SELECTOR
Three-position rotary switch: OFF, CELL I, CELL II. Always break to OFF before changing cells, and never leave her OFF beyond the minute — the pendulum lives on the battery's impulse.
CIRCUIT A SWITCH / CIRCUIT B SWITCH / CIRCUIT C SWITCH / CIRCUIT D SWITCH
Four slate-mounted knife switches, one to each borough circuit: A Post Office (ten dials), B Town Hall (ten), C Police (eight), D Schools (twelve). Close firmly home. An open switch leaves that circuit's dials standing, and they fall behind minute by minute.
FUSE RESTORE
Press to fit fresh fuse wire to any carrier that has gone. Restore only after the cause has been found and the faulty circuit opened — otherwise the wire goes again at the next impulse.
CATCH-UP LEVER
On the borough diagram card. Each pull advances every connected, sound circuit one minute toward the master. Repeat until the lag tags clear. Dials are never set back by hand.
ALARM BELL SWITCH
Small knife switch beneath the selector. Closed, the trembler bell rings when a flag drops; open, the bell is silent but the flags still fall. Silence is not evidence of health.
FLAG RESTORE
Lifts any annunciator flag whose condition has cleared. A flag that will not stay up is telling the truth; find the fault before you lift it again.
REPLACE CELLS
Two pushes at the battery shelf. Fitting a fresh CELL I or CELL II restores that jar to full charge. Break the selector to OFF first, as for any work upon the battery.
MAINTENANCE TESTS
Engineer's trial keys under the wooden cover at the foot of the board: TEST CELL EXHAUSTION, TEST LINE SHORT and TEST PENDULUM STOP. They put artificial faults upon the plant so the keeper may prove his answers. Restore the plant and clear the trial key after every drill.

Normal operation

From cold — pendulum at rest, selector OFF, circuits open — take her up as follows:

  1. Read the drop-flags. On taking over a cold board none should stand dropped; if one does, work section 5 before starting.
  2. Wind the driving weight with the WINDING CRANK until the weight stands near the top of its run. Ten seconds or so serves.
  3. Turn the BATTERY SELECTOR from OFF to CELL I. Watch the LINE VOLTS meter kick near two volts at each beat once she runs; if the needle stands low, pass to paragraph 4 below.
  4. Close the four circuit switches — A POST OFFICE, B TOWN HALL, C POLICE, D SCHOOLS — sharply and fully home.
  5. Pull the PENDULUM START LEVER once. Within half a minute the beat should settle, the IMPULSE mA meter kicking toward ninety at each minute.
  6. Confirm the ALARM BELL SWITCH is closed, so that any fault finds you and not the coroner.
  7. Compare the master against Greenwich time. If she is behind, advance the HANDS KNOB until the error is inside fifteen seconds; if ahead, note the amount and correct the RATING KNOB one or two steps SLOW.
  8. Glance at the borough card: all forty dials stepping together, no LAG tags showing. Where a circuit lags, pull the CATCH-UP LEVER until it is level.
  9. Enter the reading in the log: weight height, cell on load, rate setting, and the state of the flags. Leave the cover of MAINTENANCE TESTS closed.

Alarms

Flag Drops when What to do
TIME ERROR The master differs from Greenwich by more than fifteen seconds, either way. If behind, advance the HANDS KNOB until inside fifteen seconds. If ahead, ease the RATING KNOB toward SLOW and let true time catch her; the HANDS KNOB goes forward only.
BATTERY LOW The cell on load falls below about 1.9 volts at each beat — the VOLTS needle dipping into the red sector tells the same tale. Change over: SELECTOR to OFF, then to the other cell. Fit a fresh jar with REPLACE CELLS at leisure. Do not nurse an exhausted cell; the pendulum starves quietly and then stops.
LINE FAULT A line is shorted, or a fuse carrier has gone. Note which fuse shows red, open that circuit's knife switch, press FUSE RESTORE, satisfy yourself the line is clear, then reclose. If the wire goes again at once, the short remains — leave the circuit open and telephone the linesman.
CLOCK STOPPED The pendulum has fallen out of beat: weight down, battery dead, or an obstruction. The whole town is now late. Wind the weight, prove the battery on the VOLTS meter, clear any obstruction, pull PENDULUM START, and give the borough its minutes back with the CATCH-UP LEVER.

Flags latch down until lifted, so that a fault seen at midnight is still confessed at dawn. Lift them with FLAG RESTORE only when the condition has been answered. The trembler bell rings while the ALARM BELL SWITCH is closed; it may be silenced for working, but never left open overnight.

Faults and recovery

Battery cell exhausted
You see: the VOLTS needle kicks lower at every beat and into the red sector; BATTERY LOW drops; the beat grows faint and finally ceases. Recovery: turn the BATTERY SELECTOR to OFF, then onto the sound cell, and pull nothing else — the pendulum will pick herself up. Put a fresh jar on the empty shelf with REPLACE CELLS before leaving. Prove both cells monthly with the MAINTENANCE TESTS key TEST CELL EXHAUSTION, restoring the plant afterwards.
Slave line short circuit
You see: the IMPULSE mA needle leaping off scale toward six hundred, the fuse carrier of the affected circuit showing red, LINE FAULT dropping, and that circuit's dials standing. Recovery: open the faulty CIRCUIT SWITCH, press FUSE RESTORE, satisfy yourself the line is clear, reclose the switch, and lift the flag with FLAG RESTORE. If the fuse blows again at the next minute impulse the short persists — leave the circuit open, its dials will lag and must be caught up later with the CATCH-UP LEVER. Drill with TEST LINE SHORT under the MAINTENANCE TESTS cover.
Pendulum stoppage
You see: the bob standing dead in the trunk, CLOCK STOPPED dropped, no beat on the VOLTS meter, every slave dial frozen at the same minute. Recovery: satisfy yourself the case of the trouble — weight down (wind), battery off-load (select a cell), or obstruction (the engineer removes it; try the MAINTENANCE TESTS key TEST PENDULUM STOP to rehearse). Then pull the PENDULUM START LEVER once and watch the amplitude gather. When she beats steadily, repay the borough its lost minutes with the CATCH-UP LEVER and lift the flags.

In every case the order of work is the same: remove the cause, restore the plant, repay the time, lift the flags, and write it in the log. A fault cured but unlogged will be suffered again by whoever follows you.

Specifications

Pendulum Compensation rod, two-seconds beat; nominal amplitude 2½ degrees
Driving weight Cast iron on braided steel; full wind, approximately three hours' run; wound by front crank
Contact Closes each beat (two seconds); count wheel closes impulse line half a second in each minute
Battery Two-volt primary cells, glass jars; two on shelf, one selected to line; changeover by rotary selector
Working voltage About 2.05 volts fresh; alarm below 1.9 volts on load
Impulse current Ninety milliamperes with all four circuits connected; six hundred scale full; fuses to each circuit
Rate adjustment Rating knob, six seconds per day either way, in one-second steps; allowance for temperature by the Department's table
Hands correction Forward only, thirty seconds per turn of the knob
Distribution Forty slave dials in four circuits: A Post Office ten, B Town Hall ten, C Police eight, D Schools twelve
Annunciators Four enamel drop-flags, latching: TIME ERROR, BATTERY LOW, LINE FAULT, CLOCK STOPPED; trembler bell, cut-out switch
Case Ebonised mahogany with glazed hood; slate switchboard in cast frame; maker's plate Ashworth & Pye Ltd, Manchester
