RETORT HOUSE STOKING BENCH No. 4 — FIVE-RETORT SETTING
FOR THE USE OF THE STOKER-IN-CHARGE · ISSUED 1926 · SUPERSEDES ALL LOOSE LEAVES

Overview

This bench carbonises coal in five horizontal retorts set over a producer-gas firing system, and delivers the gas made to the holder through the water-sealed hydraulic main and the exhauster. Coal is charged by hand through the front doors; the retorts stand red throughout the shift; the gas passes up the ascension pipes and dips under the water of the main, whence the exhauster draws it away to the gasholder.

Three quantities rule the bench, and they act upon one another. The fires give the retorts their working temperature. The hot retorts give gas, which presses on the hydraulic main. The exhauster takes that gas away, and its speed sets the main’s pressure — an inch of water is the figure to hold. Neglect any one and the other two will soon tell you: starve the fires and gas make falls; flood the main and the safety lids lift and waste gas; run the exhauster hard against an empty main and you will suck air backwards through an open door, which no man at Marlpit has done twice.

KEEP THE SEAL WET — KEEP THE MAIN AT AN INCH — NEVER LEAVE A DOOR OPEN IDLE.

Controls

All controls are worked from the stokers’ side of the bench. Every one may be reached by keyboard in turn, and moved with the arrow keys; buttons answer to Enter or Space.

CHARGE LEVER
Pull the lever fully and let it fall. One stroke drops one charge (5 cwt) into the retort named by the selector. A lever worked against a live charge does nothing; the retort must first be spent.
RETORT SELECTOR
Five-position rotary switch naming the retort you are working — charging it, opening its door, raking it. Positions 1 to 5, engraved round the dial.
RETORT DOOR
Two-position lever for the selected retort: SHUT and OPEN. An open door shows the charge glowing but wastes heat; open it only to rake or to judge the charge by eye.
STOKING RAKE
The long slide bar beneath the main. With the selected retort’s door open, work the bar full out and full home — each complete stroke breaks up the charge. Three good strokes will clear a fouled flue. It does nothing against a shut door, as any apprentice knows.
PRODUCER DAMPER
Vertical lever, 0 to 10, admitting air to the producer fires. High numbers burn the fires fierce; low numbers let them sulk.
FIRING VALVE
Quarter-turn tap feeding producer gas to the bench burners, 0 to 8. This, with the damper above it, sets the bench temperature. To shut the fires: valve to nought.
EXHAUSTER GOVERNOR
Handwheel, 0 to 10, setting exhauster speed to 28 revolutions per minute for each division. Turn the right half to raise, the left half to lower. This is your pressure control: nurse it.
SEAL WATER COCK
Cross-handled cock on the service main. Hold it open to run water into the hydraulic main trough and restore the seal. Watch the sight glass; six inches and a half is the working level.
TEST BURNER COCK
Lever drawing a sample of gas to the test burner bracket at the right of the bench. ON and OFF. Keep the flame tip on the etched scribe line: short means poor or wet gas; a flame lying along the pipe means your main is in suction.
ALARM ACCEPT
Ivory push. Steadies every dropped shutter that is showing. It silences nothing that is still true.
LAMPS TEST
Hold down: every annunciator window and pilot jewel lights. Release: all return. Prove the windows before you trust them, at the start of every shift.
TRIP RESET
Red-collared push. Restarts the exhauster after an overload trip, relights the bench after a backfire (shut the firing valve first), and releases a scorched retort once she has cooled below 1900 °F.
FAULT TEST SWITCHES
Three guarded switches on the maintenance plate: SEAL, CLINKER and EXHTRIP. Wire a fault into the bench deliberately — for instruction, or to prove the alarms. Press once to inject; press again, or clear the cause, to withdraw it.

Normal operation

From cold iron to steady making:

  1. Lamps test. Hold LAMPS TEST and see every window light; release. A window you have not proved is a window you cannot believe.
  2. Light the producer and open the FIRING VALVE two turns. Ease the PRODUCER DAMPER up to about five. The pyrometer will begin to move within the minute.
  3. Bring the pyrometer to the working band, 1500 to 1900 °F, trimming valve and damper together. Above 2050 the OVERHEAT window drops; do not go there.
  4. Select RETORT 1, open her RETORT DOOR, and pull the CHARGE LEVER once. Shut the door. Charge the remaining retorts in turn the same way.
  5. As gas begins to make, start the EXHAUSTER GOVERNOR up — division three is a fair beginning — and watch the manometer.
  6. Trim the governor until the hydraulic main stands at one inch of water. Between half and one and a half is tolerable; below nought is suction, above two-and-a-half the safety lids lift.
  7. Open the TEST BURNER COCK and bring her onto the sample. Keep the flame on the scribe line while the bench settles.
  8. Watch the seal glass. Top up with the SEAL WATER COCK whenever she falls below six inches; never let the dip pipes show above the water.
  9. Work the shift by the clock card. Recharge each retort as her charge burns out; a spent retort makes next to nothing.
  10. Keep the holder between a quarter and three-quarters where you can. A full holder backs the main up; an empty one has the town calling.
  11. If a door must be opened on a live bench, mind the manometer while it stands open, and shut it when the job is done.
  12. To bank the bench: firing valve to nought, governor to nought, doors shut, and leave the seal full.

Alarms

Window Means What to do
SAFETY LIFTS Main above two-and-a-half inches; the trough lids have lifted and gas is going to waste. Ease the EXHAUSTER GOVERNOR up, or stop charging. Lids reseat below two-and-a-quarter. Accept the alarm.
MAIN SUCTION Main below minus a quarter inch; the exhauster is beating the make. Ease the governor down at once. If a door stands open, shut it before you draw air through her.
OVERHEAT Bench above 2050 °F, or a retort scorched. Turn the FIRING VALVE back and ease the damper. If the iron has actually scorched, let her cool under 1900 °F and press TRIP RESET to release her.
GAS ESCAPE Seal below four inches; raw gas is breaking through the dips. Hold the SEAL WATER COCK and refill past five and a half inches. Find where the water is going before you blame evaporation.
LOW GAS MAKE Little gas arriving, or the flues choking hard — the window will drop even on a cooling bench when the flues foul right up. Fouled flues, or a bench of spent charges. Rake the fouled retort (three strokes, door open) or charge fresh coal. Compare the test burner with the pyrometer.
HOLDER FULL Holder at ninety-six per cent or better; she is backing the main. Ease the governor down. Tell the works; a full holder is nobody’s secret for long.
HOLDER LOW Holder under eight per cent. Make gas, not excuses: fires up, fresh charges in, governor as the main allows.
EXHTRIP Exhauster stopped on overload. Press TRIP RESET. If she will not hold in, ease the governor to nought, reset, and bring her up slowly.
FLAME FAIL The fires have been blown out by suction — a backfire. Shut the FIRING VALVE to nought, see the main above minus a tenth, then press TRIP RELIGHT — TRIP RESET relights the bench. Bring the valve back gradually.

Faults and recovery

Fault 1 — water seal blown

What you see: the sight glass and the trough falling fast, GAS ESCAPE inside the minute, the main sagging however hard the exhauster pulls, the test burner guttering. Left alone, the dips run dry and raw gas comes into the house.

Recovery:

  1. Ease the EXHAUSTER GOVERNOR down — do not suck the main dry.
  2. Hold the SEAL WATER COCK until the glass reads five and a half inches or better.
  3. Bring the main back to an inch on the governor. Accept the alarm; the window clears itself when the seal holds.
  4. Press the SEAL test switch to withdraw the injection once the level stands.

Flue clinker fouling

What you see: the pyrometer climbing while the bench goes quiet — gas make falling, LOW GAS MAKE dropping in, the test burner flame shortening. The flues are choked and the heat cannot reach the charges.

Recovery:

  1. Select the worst retort and open her RETORT DOOR.
  2. Work the STOKING RAKE three full strokes, out and home each time. Each stroke breaks the clinker off.
  3. Watch the pyrometer come honest and the make recover; repeat on any other retort that wants it.
  4. Press the CLINKER test switch to withdraw the injection when the flues run clear.

Exhauster overload trip

What you see: the exhauster wheel standing still, the jewel dark, EXHTRIP dropped, and the main creeping up towards the safety lids as the gas has nowhere to go.

Recovery:

  1. Ease the EXHAUSTER GOVERNOR to nought.
  2. Press TRIP RESET; the starter takes her in again.
  3. Bring the governor back up steadily and settle the main at an inch.
  4. Accept the alarm. If the lids lifted while she stood, they will have reseated below two-and-a-quarter inches.

Specifications

Retorts Five, horizontal D-section, 20 ft × 14 in, fireclay
Charge per retort 5 cwt (254 kg), house coal, screened
Working band, bench 1500 – 1900 °F (815 – 1040 °C)
Overheat alarm 2050 °F; scorching damage held at 2250 °F for twenty minutes
Carbonisation Nominal ten hours a charge at working temperature
Hydraulic main Working pressure 1.0 – 1.5 in W.G.; safety lids lift at 2.55 in W.G.
Suction limit Minus 0.25 in W.G.; backfire risk below minus 0.45 with a door open
Water seal Dip depth 6½ in; alarm at 4 in; refill rate ¾ in to the inch of cock
Exhauster Rotary, 28 rev/min per governor division, 280 rev/min maximum
Gasholder No. 1 120,000 cubic feet; town take-off 28 per cent of full draw per hour
Test burner Scribe line 26 divisions; sample cock on the main
