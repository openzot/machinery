Overview

This board holds Crude Unit No. 2 on its toes. Charge is pumped from the drum through the pre-exchange and into fired heater H·1; the coil outlet, part vaporised, enters atmospheric column C·1 part way up. Overhead vapour is condensed, collected in the reflux drum, and pumped back by P·3 as top reflux, with the balance drawn to storage. Bottoms run off to the vizor breaker.

Three pneumatic controllers do the steady work: TIC·1 throttles the fuel gas to hold the coil outlet temperature, PIC·2 works the condensing (reverse acting: more output, more condensing, lower pressure), and FIC·3 sets the reflux flow. Each controller is a square-cased receiver instrument working on 3–15 lb/in² of instrument air from the header in the bay below. The twin-pen circular chart keeps the shift's record: red ink for the coil outlet, blue for column pressure.

The board is honest but it is not clever. It does exactly what the needles say and nothing the charts do not show. Keep the air up, keep the pens inked, and answer every lamp before it answers you.

Controls

ALARM ACCEPT
Pushbutton on the annunciator rail. Turns the flashing lamps to steady and silences the horn for a new alarm. A condition that clears puts its lamp out; a condition that persists stays lit until it is put right.
LAMPS TEST
Pushbutton. Lights every annunciator lamp, the flame eye and the mode windows so dead bulbs are found before they matter.
CHART MOTOR
Toggle on the recorder. Starts and stops the chart drive. Pens lift when the motor is stopped; no turn, no record.
TIC·1 SET POINT / OUTPUT
Bakelite knob on the temperature station. In AUTO it sets the outlet temperature set point against its scale. In MANUAL the same knob is the valve position, 0–100% of the fuel range. The amber BALANCE needle shows how far knob sits from measurement while in MANUAL.
TIC-1 AUTO/MANUAL
Lever on the temperature station. Swapping to MANUAL drives the knob to the present output (no bump); swapping to AUTO hands the loop back from where it stands. The window shows which master holds the valve.
PIC-2 SET POINT / OUTPUT and PIC-2 AUTO/MANUAL
The same pair on the pressure station, working column pressure in lb/in² gauge. Remember PIC·2 is reverse acting: raising the output condenses harder and brings the pressure down.
FIC-3 SET POINT / OUTPUT and FIC-3 AUTO/MANUAL
The same pair on the flow station, working top reflux in m³/h against P·3's discharge.
FUEL GAS COCK
Three-position lever in the burner bay: CLOSED, LIGHT (low fire, about a third open) and OPEN. It passes nothing until TIC·1's output is above its zero, and it is slammed shut by the protective gear when the unit trips.
AIR REGISTER
Damper knob beside the cock, percent open. Fifty-five percent suits the burners as commissioned; too little air smokes and starves the box, too much wastes her heat up the stack.
PILOT TEST
Held pushbutton. Hold it two seconds to run the igniter and prove the pilot on the flame eye; release, and the pilot stays lit until the gas fails or the unit trips. Light the main cock only off a proven pilot.
FLAME EYE
Amber jewel behind brass. Amber means fire in the box - pilot or main. Dark means none, whatever the fuel cock says.
CHARGE PUMP START / STOP
Mushroom heads on the operating shelf. P·1 takes a few seconds to come onto the line; watch the rate climb under the dial.
CELL SELECTOR (TC SELECTOR)
Rotary switch by the temperature station: RUN, DUPLEX, CHECK. RUN reads the working thermocouple. DUPLEX swings the indicator and the trip on to the standby cell - this is also how you put a drifting run cell out of service. CHECK compares the two against each other.
HI-TEMP TRIP RESET
Key under a hinged red guard. The high-temperature trip shuts the fuel for keeps once it operates; lift the guard, press to reset, then re-light by the procedure.
AIR COMPRESSOR RESET
Recessed pushbutton in the air bay. Restarts the instrument-air compressor after it has dropped. The header rebuilds at its own pace; controllers stay dead until the gauge passes ten.
MAINTENANCE TESTS
Four-position selector marked OFF, AIR FAIL, TC DRIFT and CAVITATION, for proving the protective gear with the unit live. Each test injects its fault exactly as the real thing would arrive. Return the selector to OFF when the test is done - and mind that OFF does not itself undo the fault; the proper remedy below does that.
REFLUX PUMP VENT
Held pushbutton by the mimic's P·3. Hold it to blow the vapour lock clear of the pump; the flow comes back as she primes.
UNIT RESET
Pushbutton, far right. Returns the whole board to cold - pumps stopped, cocks closed, trips reset, lamps dark, chart cleared. For a new start or after a bad shift.

Normal operation

From cold and alarm-free to steady on-specification running. Take it in order; each step depends on the one before.

  1. Press UNIT RESET. Confirm every annunciator lamp is dark and the air gauge settles at twenty.
  2. Open the AIR REGISTER to about fifty-five percent.
  3. Switch the CHART MOTOR on and check both pens ride their arcs.
  4. Set the stations: TIC·1 to five hundred five degrees, PIC·2 to one point two bar, FIC·3 to forty-five cubic metres an hour. Leave all three levers on AUTO; you will take them to MANUAL as needed.
  5. Press CHARGE PUMP START and let P·1 come onto the line.
  6. Hold PILOT TEST for two seconds until the flame eye burns amber, then move the FUEL GAS COCK to LIGHT. The eye stays lit when you release.
  7. Take TIC-1 AUTO/MANUAL to MANUAL and walk the output up. Watch the coil temperature climb through four hundred; open the cock to OPEN as she takes the fire comfortably.
  8. Near five hundred, centre the amber BALANCE needle and transfer TIC-1 back to AUTO. She should hold the line without wandering.
  9. Bring reflux: take FIC-3 AUTO/MANUAL to MANUAL, raise the output until the flow sits near forty-five, balance and return it to AUTO.
  10. Column pressure will settle toward one point two as the reflux bites. If it hangs off, trim PIC-2 in MANUAL the same way and hand it back to AUTO.
  11. Stand watch a minute or two. Steady pens, no lamps, air at twenty: hand her to the shift. Accept any alarm that flashes, and always for a reason.

Alarms

FLAME FAILURE (flashing until accepted)
The cock demands fire and the flame eye sees none. If you were lighting up, wait six seconds past opening the cock before believing it. If she was running, the fuel has been taken from her: find why (trip, slam, or air below ten), put it right, and re-light from step 6 of Normal operation.
OUTLET TEMP HIGH
Coil outlet above five hundred forty-five degrees. Ease TIC·1 - lower the set point or close the manual output - and watch the red pen fall. Above five hundred eighty-five the trip acts regardless of what you do.
COLUMN PRESS HIGH
Pressure above two point one. Add reflux (raise FIC·3), cool the top (raise PIC·2 output), or cut the fire. Two point six lifts PSV·2.
RELIEF LIFTED
The safety valve is passing to the flare. Cut the heat at once and bring the reflux up. Do not let her hang there: seventy seconds on the flare slams the fuel and puts the whole unit out.
REFLUX FLOW LOW
P·3 has fallen away with her discharge valve open. Suspect a vapour lock; hold REFLUX PUMP VENT until the flow returns. Meanwhile watch the pressure - it will be climbing.
TC CELL FAULT
The run thermocouple has drifted away from her standby sister. Swing the CELL SELECTOR to DUPLEX, prove the reading against the chart, and have the run cell changed at opportunity.
INSTRUMENT AIR LOW
Header below fourteen. Below ten every controller on the board is dead and the outputs bleed toward zero - the fuel valve fails shut and the fire goes out. Return MAINTENANCE TESTS to OFF if it was on AIR FAIL, press AIR COMPRESSOR RESET, wait for the gauge to rebuild, and re-light by the procedure.

Faults and recovery

  1. Instrument air failure

    You see: the header needle sagging, INSTRUMENT AIR LOW flashing, and then every mode window going DEAD as the outputs bleed down. The fuel valve fails shut and FLAME FAILURE follows. Nothing you do to a knob moves anything while the gauge reads under ten.

    To recover:

    1. Return MAINTENANCE TESTS to OFF if the test selector caused it.
    2. Press AIR COMPRESSOR RESET and watch the gauge rebuild to twenty.
    3. When the controllers answer again, re-light by steps 6 to 8 of Normal operation. The pilot will need proving again.

  2. Thermocouple drift

    You see: TC CELL FAULT after a few minutes, and before that a coil reading that will not sit where the fire says it should - the run cell is reading cold while the metal runs hot. Chasing the set point only pours it on: the red pen tells the true story if the chart is turning. Left alone, the column climbs, the relief lifts, and the unit slams herself out.

    To recover:

    1. Turn the CELL SELECTOR to DUPLEX - the indication jumps to truth and the fault clears.
    2. If OUTLET TEMP HIGH shows, ease the set point until she settles.
    3. If the trip has operated, lift the guard on HI-TEMP TRIP RESET, press it, and re-light by the procedure.
    4. CHECK may be used at any time to compare cells; RUN returns you to the drifted cell, so leave her on DUPLEX until the cell is changed.

  3. Reflux pump cavitation

    You see: REFLUX FLOW LOW with the discharge wide open, the flow needle sagging toward nothing as P·3 loses her suction to vapour. Column pressure follows her down inside a minute or two.

    To recover:

    1. Hold REFLUX PUMP VENT - a good three or four seconds' hold - until the flow climbs back toward her duty.
    2. If pressure has already lifted the relief, cut the fire and hold reflux on MANUAL until COLUMN PRESS HIGH drops out.
    3. If the unit slammed on the flare, reset the trip under its guard and start again from light-up.
