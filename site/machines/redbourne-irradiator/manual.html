Overview

Cell No. 1 disinfects medical packs and spices by gamma radiation. Ninety-six cobalt-60 pencils stand in a stainless rack at the bottom of a six-metre deionised-water pool. When the rack is down, five metres of water sit between you and it, and you may work beside the pool in shirtsleeves. When the rack is up — still under more than two metres of water — product totes ride the maze past the window and take their dose.

The desk before you does four things and is forgiven for nothing else: it raises and lowers the rack on its hoist; it walks totes around the ten-station maze against a dwell timer; it watches the water, the temperature and three area monitors; and it drops the rack at the first sign that any of those duties are being neglected. There is no button on this panel that moves gamma rays faster than water can stop them. Remember that the other way round, too.

THE THREE RULES. One: never less than 5.35 m of water over a raised rack. Two: nobody in the cell during a search sweep or a transit — the beacon and the horn say so. Three: an alarm you do not understand is a shutdown, not a puzzle.

Controls

CONTROL KIND POSITIONS / USE
CONTROL KEY keyswitch OFF — everything dark, rack held down. STANDBY — monitors, vent fan and interlock chain live. OPERATE — permits the hoist. Turns only through the order OFF → STANDBY → OPERATE; press the barrel to step it.
SEARCH BUTTON illuminated push Starts the ten-second search sweep with the rack down and the maze off. The window counts SWEEP 10…1, then READY lights. OPERATE refuses without READY.
SOURCE HOIST LEVER three-notch maintained lever RAISE — rack travels up in 24 s, beacon turning. HOLD — motion stops, rack stays. LOWER — controlled descent in 14 s. Arrow keys work the notches.
EMERGENCY SOURCE DROP guarded red mushroom Lift the yellow collar, strike the mushroom: gravity takes the rack to storage inside six seconds. Lights DROP USED; TRIP RESET and a fresh search are wanted before OPERATE again.
CONVEYOR SELECTOR rotary, three positions OFF — maze parked. SINGLE STEP — each PRODUCT INDEX press advances the train one station. AUTO CYCLE — the maze indexes itself every DWELL TIMER interval. Indexing is locked while the rack is in transit.
DWELL TIMER graduated knob, 6–60 s Seconds the train waits per station. Press for +2 s, shift-press for −2 s, arrow keys to trim. Licence dwell for medical packs: 12 s (≈2.5 kGy in four passes).
PRODUCT INDEX amber push Advances the maze one station in SINGLE STEP; refused during transit, a jam or a conveyor trip.
JAM CLEAR ROCKER two-way rocker Hold REV for three seconds to back the train one station off a wedge. NORM is the rest position.
TRIP RESET grey push Clears the hoist thermal overload (only once it has cooled, about forty seconds), the conveyor trip, and the DROP USED latch.
WATER TOP-UP VALVE hold-open lever Mains make-up into the pool. Hold open and watch the level climb; the MAKE-UP WATER lamp burns while filling.
AREA MONITOR SELECTOR rotary, three positions Chooses which detector feeds the log ratemeter: POOL TOP, MAZE DOOR or STACK. The digital window under the dial echoes the choice.
ALARM ACCEPT push Silences the horn and stops the windows flashing. It accepts nothing away: a lit window stays lit until the condition is genuinely cured.
LAMPS TEST push Lights every indicator window on the desk for four seconds. Do it at STANDBY, every shift.
FAULT TEST SWITCHES three maintained toggles under the rail Maintenance only: TRANSIT JAM, POOL LEAK and MAZE JAM inject the corresponding plant fault so the interlocks may be proved. Each is released automatically when its recovery procedure has actually been carried out.

Normal operation

  1. Desk cold, key at OFF. On the mimic confirm the pointer at STORAGE and the cover reading 5.0 m or better; on the record confirm POOL TEMP below 30 °C.
  2. Turn the CONTROL KEY to STANDBY. CHAIN MADE UP and CELL VENT FAN light; the ratemeter needle wakes to the pool-top background, about 0.04 µSv/h.
  3. Press LAMPS TEST. Every window on the desk must burn for four seconds. Any dead window: stop, call maintenance.
  4. Check the annunciator strip is dark — no POOL LEVEL, no TRANSIT, no RADIATION.
  5. Press the SEARCH BUTTON. The sweep counts ten seconds. Stand where you can see the door stays shut. READY lights at the end.
  6. Turn the CONTROL KEY to OPERATE. (It refuses, and INTERLOCK OPEN flashes, if READY is missing.)
  7. Put the SOURCE HOIST LEVER to RAISE. The beacon turns, IN TRANSIT burns, and twenty-four seconds later the pointer sits at IRRADIATE and SOURCE UP lights. The lever returns itself to HOLD at the top.
  8. Read the desk: cover 2.1 m or more; POOL TOP monitor no more than 3 µSv/h; POOL TEMP beginning its slow climb towards thirty.
  9. Set the DWELL TIMER to the licensed dwell — 12 s for medical packs.
  10. Put the CONVEYOR SELECTOR to AUTO CYCLE. The maze begins indexing once per dwell; totes load at the left tower and discharge at the top return. DOSE PER BOX reads the last tote to leave — 2.4 to 2.7 kGy is grade.
  11. Hourly: level 5.8 m, temp below 34 °C, strip dark, counters moving.
  12. To shut down: CONVEYOR SELECTOR OFF, let the maze empty, then SOURCE HOIST LEVER to LOWER. Fourteen seconds later the pointer rests at STORAGE, SOURCE UP goes dark; turn the key to OFF.

Alarms

WINDOW MEANING WHAT TO DO
SOURCE TRANSIT Rack stalled part-way on its guides; hoist current pegged. A stalled motor cooks: the TRIP lamp follows within forty-five seconds. Leave the lever at HOLD. When tripped, wait the cooldown, press TRIP RESET, put the lever to LOWER and let her walk to the bottom — the fouler drops into the basket. Search again, raise again.
POOL LEVEL Water below 5.35 m with a leak somewhere below the liner flange. Cover is being eaten. Hold the WATER TOP-UP VALVE and keep her above the line; send a fitter to the culvert. Do not raise the rack with this window lit — the key logic already agrees.
HIGH RADIATION A monitor past its limit (pool top 25, door 10, stack 2 µSv/h). The desk has already dropped the rack on its own authority. Stand clear until the pointer reads STORAGE. Telephone the RPS, ext 271. Then TRIP RESET, search, and only then consider restarting — after finding why the water went thin.
PRODUCT JAM The maze has wedged a tote and the train cannot advance. Hold the JAM CLEAR ROCKER at REV for three seconds — the train backs one station. Resume with PRODUCT INDEX or AUTO CYCLE.
OVERDOSE DAMAGED A tote stood at the window past 4.0 kGy. It has been quarantined for assay and the conveyor tripped. SCRAPPED has counted it. Clear the jam first (REV), then TRIP RESET, then re-select AUTO CYCLE. Find what stopped the train before you blame the timer.
INTERLOCK OPEN You asked for a stage out of order — OPERATE without READY, RAISE without the key, indexing during transit — or the emergency drop has been used. Nothing is broken. Put the sequence right; after a drop, TRIP RESET and a fresh search are wanted.

Faults and recovery

source rack jams in transit

You see: SOURCE TRANSIT flashing, the pointer frozen between zones, the winch straining on the mimic; after forty-five seconds of trying, TRIP lights and the lever goes dead.
Why: a pencil sleeve has fouled the guide shoe.
Recovery: hands off — never hold RAISE against a stall. Wait the thermal cooldown (forty seconds), press TRIP RESET, put the lever to LOWER and let the rack walk down in its own time; at the bottom the fouling piece drops into the guide basket, SOURCE TRANSIT goes dark, and the maintenance toggle releases itself. Prove the run: search, raise, watch her pass the old height smartly.

pool water leak

You see: POOL LEVEL flashing inside a minute of the breach, the surface line on the mimic sinking, cover falling at roughly a centimetre every ninety seconds; ignore it long enough and HIGH RADIATION drops the rack for you.
Why: liner seep, usually at the north flange.
Recovery: hold the WATER TOP-UP VALVE and keep the level climbing; when she stands at 5.6 m or better the seep is considered found and sealed — POOL LEVEL goes dark and the maintenance toggle releases. If HIGH RADIATION dropped the rack meanwhile: TRIP RESET, search, raise, and write it in the log regardless.

product jam in the maze

You see: PRODUCT JAM flashing, the chain dashed lines on the mimic stopped, PRODUCT INDEX refused; a tote cooking at the window — at the 12 s dwell she gains a kilogray every ten seconds she sits there, and at 4.0 kGy OVERDOSE DAMAGED quarantines her and trips the line.
Why: a swollen corner or a stray strap.
Recovery: hold the JAM CLEAR ROCKER at REV a full three seconds — the train steps back one station and the wedged tote falls free; PRODUCT JAM goes dark. Then TRIP RESET to clear the overdose trip, and CONVEYOR SELECTOR back to AUTO CYCLE. The scrapped tally keeps the loss honest.

Specifications

Source Cobalt-60, 96 pencils in a stainless rack, 148 PBq (4.0 kCi) at installation, half-life 5.27 years
Pool 6.0 m deep, deionised water, conductivity below 10 µS/cm; nominal level 5.80 m; low alarm 5.35 m
Rack travel 2.9 m; up 24 s, down 14 s, emergency drop within 6 s by gravity
Hoist 2.2 kW drum winch, thermal overload class 20, cooldown 40 s
Maze 10 stations, four effective passes; dwell 6–60 s per station; throughput ≈30 totes per hour at 12 s
Dose Nominal 2.5 kGy per tote at 12 s dwell, uniformity 1.3:1; quarantine threshold 4.0 kGy
Area monitors Log ratemeter 0.1 µSv/h to 100 k µSv/h; limits — pool top 25, maze door 10, stack 2 µSv/h; any exceedance drives the automatic drop
Decay heat Pool water 18 °C cold, settles near 34 °C with the rack up, alarm-free limit 40 °C
Supplies 240 V 50 Hz mains; panel QP-1; drop gear on its own battery
Licence RSA-17/114; dwell changes only with RPS countersignature on form QP-31
