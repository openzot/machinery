Operating Handbook

DRIVE, DOME & GUIDING DESK No. 2 — CARN LLWYD OBSERVATORY — 36-INCH REFLECTOR — WORKS No. 1187 — SECOND EDITION, OCTOBER 1961

ISSUED WITH THE DESK — KEEP UNDER THE COUNTER

Overview

This desk carries everything the night assistant touches after the dome is shut and the public has gone down the mountain: the clock drive that turns the telescope against the heavens, the dome shutter motor, the air float that carries the 36-inch mirror, and the photometer integration for whatever programme the director has left on the clip. It is built heavy because it is used in the dark, by feel, at two in the morning, by someone whose hands are cold. Every legend on the desk is engraved and filled with dull red paint. White light is the enemy; the WORKING LIGHT switch exists for dropped spanners and nothing else.

The plant behind the desk is simple and honest. A synchronous motor drives the worm through a governed train; if the governor hunts, the star walks out of the field and you walk it back. The dome follows the telescope through the same desk, by hand on the jog rockers or on FOLLOW. The mirror rests on an annular air bag fed from a small compressor in the pier; the bag wants twelve pounds and forgives very little below nine. The photometer counts while conditions are good and forgets nothing.

NIGHT VISION. Twenty minutes to earn, one second to lose. Keep the WORKING LIGHT off during an exposure. The guide star will fade to nothing under white light and take some minutes to come back even after the lamp is cut.

Controls

MAINS BREAKER
Guarded switch, lower-left of the drive bay. Positions: guard closed, guard lifted, ON, OFF. Lift the guard before throwing. Feeds the drive motor and the desk circuits; the MAINS annunciator burns gold when made.
DRIVE CLUTCH
Lever on a quadrant, marked OUT and IN. IN drops the sidereal drive into gear; a relay will be heard and the DRIVE annunciator lights while the worm is actually carrying. The dome interlock throws this lever out on its own if the slit strikes the telescope.
RA TRIM HANDWHEEL
Sixteen-spoke wheel below the error meter. Turns the telescope in right ascension against the drift; use it to lay the star exactly on the crosswire. Arrow keys answer like the wheel when the desk is driven remotely.
DOME MODE SELECTOR
Three-position rotary: OFF, MAN, FOLL. OFF leaves the shutter where it stands. MAN arms the jog rockers. FOLL sends the shutter after the telescope axis at its working speed.
DOME JOG
Pair of spring-return rockers, CCW and CW. Live in MAN only; each second held moves the slit about three degrees. Dead if the dome breaker is out.
DOME BREAKER
Green-cap pushbutton beside the rockers. Resets the shutter motor circuit after an overload, and releases the dome interlock once the slit is back inside fifteen degrees of the axis.
FOCUS MICROMETER
Fluted knob under the eyepiece. Range ±900 microns against the temperature drift of the tube. Turn for the smallest star image on the SEEING readout.
GUIDE N-S / GUIDE E-W
Two pairs of spring toggles flanking the eyepiece. They nudge the telescope in declination and right ascension respectively to hold the guide star on the crosswire against atmospheric wander. Spring back to centre when released.
FLOAT CHARGE VALVE
Red handwheel, right bay. Hold open to feed the mirror air bag; roughly half a pound a second. Held past about 11½ lb/in² the surge will blow a fouled seat clear — see Faults and recovery.
WORKING LIGHT
Bat-handle toggle on the bottom strip. Ordinary white bench light. Destroys dark adaptation; the WHITE LIGHT annunciator burns while it is on.
ALARM ACCEPT
White-cap pushbutton. Silences the bell and acknowledges the present set of annunciators. A new alarm rings again from cold.
LAMPS TEST
Amber-cap pushbutton. Hold in; every annunciator must light. Report the dead one, not the live ones.
FAULT TEST SWITCHES
Three miniature switches on the bottom strip under the TEST legend — GOVERNOR, AIR LEAK, RUNAWAY. Each imposes its condition on the plant exactly as the real failure would, for drill and for proving the alarms. Clear by the recovery procedure or by the engineer's reset.
ENGINEER RESET
Brass keyswitch, bottom right. Returns the whole desk to cold standby: breaker off, clutch out, dome released, float recharged, integration wound back, alarms cleared. To be used after the mirror has been re-floated, not as a substitute for finding a fault.
SOUND CUT
Small bat-handle toggle beside ALARM ACCEPT. Kills the drive hum, the relays and the alarm bell. The lamps still speak.

Normal operation

  1. MAINS BREAKER: lift the guard and throw ON. Confirm the MAINS annunciator shows gold and the sidereal clock is turning.
  2. Hold LAMPS TEST; every annunciator must light. Release.
  3. Read the MIRROR FLOAT gauge: twelve pounds, inside the green. If it is down, hold the FLOAT CHARGE VALVE until the needle settles back in the band, then let go.
  4. DOME MODE SELECTOR to MAN. Jog with DOME JOG until the slit needle stands upright — better than two degrees either side — then put the selector to FOLL.
  5. Throw the DRIVE CLUTCH to IN. The DRIVE annunciator lights; the drive hum begins.
  6. With the RA TRIM HANDWHEEL lay the guide star exactly on the crosswire centre. The RA ERROR needle should sit near zero; expect a few tenths of a second of arc of natural drift.
  7. Turn the FOCUS MICROMETER until the SEEING readout shows the smallest image — about one second of arc on an ordinary night. Touch GUIDE N-S and GUIDE E-W only when the wander takes the star off the cross.
  8. The INTEGRATION drum now counts towards 0900. Guard it: keep the star on the cross, the slit upright and the float in the green. When the count is complete the MEASUREMENT lamp burns and the run belongs to the director.

Alarms

Annunciator Means To do
TRACKING ERROR Star more than twelve seconds of arc off in right ascension — governor hunting, or the drive has dropped while engaged. Find which. If the needle swings rather than walks, treat as governor hunt (below). Otherwise re-engage and lay the star back with the handwheel.
SLIT OFFSET Shutter more than eight degrees off the telescope axis. Jog the dome onto the axis, or put the selector to FOLL and let her come round.
DOME INTERLOCK Slit passed fifteen degrees; the interlock has thrown the clutch out to protect the telescope. Bring the slit back inside fifteen degrees, then press DOME BREAKER to release the interlock before engaging the clutch again.
FLOAT LOW Mirror air bag below nine pounds. Hold FLOAT CHARGE VALVE; watch the gauge into the green band. Investigate the loss before trusting the night.
MIRROR SEATED The float has failed entirely; the mirror rests on its stops. Observing is over — the image is ruined beyond the desk's help. Close the dome. Telephone the engineer. Only ENGINEER RESET, after the pad is fed, restores the desk.
Steady indications MAINS and DRIVE show the healthy state of their circuits; WHITE LIGHT warns that adaptation is being spent; MEASUREMENT shows the integration complete. No action unless a steady indication contradicts the lever in your hand.

Faults and recovery

Clock-drive governor hunt
You see: the RA ERROR needle swinging wider each pass instead of creeping; TRACKING ERROR follows; the star slides off the crosswire in lurches.
Clear it: throw the DRIVE CLUTCH to OUT and accept the alarm. Lay the star back on the crosswire with the RA TRIM HANDWHEEL. Engage the clutch again; the governor pawl reseats itself about four seconds after re-engagement and the drift returns to its normal few tenths.
Mirror-support air leak
You see: the float gauge falling steadily, roughly three-quarters of a pound a minute; FLOAT LOW at nine.
Clear it: hold the FLOAT CHARGE VALVE wide until the needle passes eleven and a half pounds — the surge blows the fouled seat clear — then ease off and settle her in the green band. If she falls away again, the joint needs wire and soap, not the desk. Letting the bag fall to three and a half pounds seats the mirror; that is MIRROR SEATED and only the engineer's reset answers it.
Dome shutter drive runaway
You see: the slit walking off the axis on its own, half a degree a second, without the rockers being touched; SLIT OFFSET, then the interlock throwing the clutch out at fifteen degrees.
Clear it: press DOME BREAKER to isolate the shutter motor; the crawl stops. Put the selector to MAN, jog the slit back inside fifteen degrees, press DOME BREAKER again to drop the interlock, return the selector to FOLL and engage the clutch.

Specifications

Telescope & drive
Aperture 914 mm (36 in.), paraboloid on air float
Focus f/14 Cassegrain; guiding head scale 6.9″/mm
Sidereal rate 15.041″ per mean second; clock correction +1.00274
Normal drift not worse than 0.05″/s; free drift 15.2″/s
Governor hunt onset oscillation reaching 12″ within about 40 s
Dome
Shutter travel 4.6 m on twin rails; jog rate ±3.2°/s; follow rate 2.2°/s
Alignment limits alarm at 8°; interlock trip at 15°; travel ends ±25°
Mirror float
Working charge 10.5–13.5 lb/in², nominal 12; low alarm at 9
Charge rate / leak +0.5 and −0.075 lb/in² per second respectively
Seat point 3.6 lb/in² — mirror on stops, observing ended
Observing
Photometer Halswell GM-21 cell, signal 0–100 μA, good counting above 48
Integration target 900 s effective; drum to 9999 s
Focus range ±900 μm; tube drift about 260 μm/°C of fall
Dark adaptation lost at 9 %/s under white light; regained in about five minutes
Desk
Supply 230 V, 50 Hz, single phase; breaker 15 A
Night air, design range 3–10 °C at the eyepiece
Finish wrinkle enamel, black; legends engraved, luminous red filled
