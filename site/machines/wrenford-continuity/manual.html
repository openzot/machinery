Overview

This desk is the voice of Wrenford Vale Radio between programmes. Everything the audience hears — network line, cartridge jingles and spots, the studio microphone — is brought up on the fader bank, mixed onto the programme bus, and passed down the line to the transmitter at WRO Little Welford, whose remote meters stand at the right-hand end of the panel. When you are on duty the transmitter is radiating; the desk rests overnight carrying the network with the NETWORK fader at seven. Your job is that the bus is never quiet: a silence sensor stands over the programme feed, and dead air is treated as a fault against the station, not against the weather.

The alarm lamps and relays are fed from the standby supply and are live whether or not the desk is switched on. Programme equipment is not. Do not defeat the interlocks, do not lift the maintenance lid without the engineer's say-so, and log every fault in the continuity book.

Controls

Control Type Action and positions
CONSOLE MAINS Bat toggle Programme supplies. ON lights the console pilot, meter lamps and clock. OFF kills the programme chain only; the annunciators stay live.
CARTRIDGE SELECTOR Rotary, 6 positions Selects the cart machine, 1 to 6, whose label strip is lit. Click or press arrow keys to step.
CART START Illuminated bar Starts the selected cartridge. Bar glows while tape is running; the deck readout gives state and seconds remaining.
CART STOP CUE Pushbutton Stops transport and re-cues the selected cartridge to zero. Clears a stalled deck.
NETWORK SELECTOR Rotary, 3 positions MAIN LINE, the ITV-cleared feed; STANDBY FEED, the reserve music and news circuit; OFF, line disconnected.
MIC KEY Guarded bat toggle Lift the hinged guard, then throw the key to put the studio microphone on the MIC channel. Lamp MIC LIVE confirms.
CART WALL FADER Slide fader, 0–10 Cart machine output to the programme bus. 7 is the marked operating point.
NETWORK FADER Slide fader, 0–10 Network line to the bus. Left at 7 overnight; raise to full for the join.
MIC FADER Slide fader, 0–10 Mic channel level. Keep down until the red light is wanted; 8 for normal speech.
MONITOR SELECTOR Rotary, 3 positions AUDITION hears the bus off-air; AIR is the transmitted feed returned; CUE cues a cart on headphones only.
DELAY ARM Bat toggle Arms the profanity-delay store; the hold builds to 8.0 seconds. Arm before opening the microphone.
DELAY DUMP Red bar Dumps the stored delay to zero in one movement. The store rebuilds while ARM remains on.
TX RAISE / TX LOWER Pushbuttons Transmitter carrier power setpoint in quarter-kilowatt steps, 0 to 5 kW. Nominal 4 kW.
TX RESET Pushbutton Resets a transmitter trip once reflected power and PA temperature have been brought down and programme is restored.
ALARM ACCEPT Pushbutton Stops the flash on accepted alarms; the lamp burns steady while the condition persists and goes out when it clears.
LAMPS TEST Pushbutton, hold down Holds every lamp, meter lamp and annunciator alight. Release to return.
TEST CART JAM Test key (lid) Fires the cart transport into a bound-tape stall for alarm training.
TEST NETWORK LOSS Test key (lid) Simulates loss of the main network feed.
TEST TX OVERLOAD Test key (lid) Simulates antenna mismatch and reflected-power rise at the transmitter.
TEST TONE Pushbutton, hold down Injections 1 kHz reference tone onto the bus for meter checking. Hold briefly only.
CONSOLE RESET Key switch Returns the desk to handover standby: network on air at seven, carts cued, transmitter at 4 kW, alarms cleared.

The six annunciators along the bottom rail — SILENCE, NET FAIL, CART JAM, PA TEMP, TX TRIP, OFF AIR — are described under Alarms. The PEAK lamp beside the VU meter warns of modulation above +1.5 dB.

Normal operation

  1. Confirm CONSOLE MAINS is ON: pilot lit, VU face illuminated, clock running. If the desk is dark, operate MAINS and wait ten seconds for the supplies.
  2. Note the transmitter page: FORWARD 4.00 kW, REFLECTED under 40 W, TX READY lit, no annunciator showing.
  3. Put MONITOR SELECTOR to AUDITION. LINE OK should be lit and the network bar moving: the overnight programme is riding the bus on the NETWORK fader at seven.
  4. Turn CARTRIDGE SELECTOR to cart 1, OPEN THEME, and confirm the label and READY state on the deck readout.
  5. Raise CART WALL FADER to 7. At forty seconds to the hour, press CART START and ride the theme under the network.
  6. On the hour, raise NETWORK FADER towards 8 for the join and pull the CART WALL FADER to zero as the theme ends. The deck returns itself to READY on the end-of-message tone.
  7. Before speaking, throw DELAY ARM and watch HOLD build to 8.0 s.
  8. Lift the MIC KEY guard, throw the key, raise MIC FADER to 8, and keep peaks on the VU between −3 and +2. Dump the delay if anything untoward reaches air, and take the mic fader down afterwards.
  9. Between items keep one source always on the bus. Never pull the last fader with the transmitter radiating: the sensor gives you twelve seconds' warning and thirty before she drops herself.
  10. On leaving, restore NETWORK FADER to seven, cue the next hour's carts, and leave the desk as found.

Alarms

Annunciator Meaning Immediate action
SILENCE Programme feed to the transmitter below −27 dB for 12 s. At 30 s the transmitter drops itself. ACCEPT the flash and put a source on the bus at once — network fader up, cart started, or tone.
NET FAIL Main network line lost at the line supervision relay. ACCEPT, turn NETWORK SELECTOR to STANDBY FEED and announce. Engineers restore the main line, typically inside three minutes.
CART JAM Tape bound in the selected machine; transport stalled. Press CART STOP CUE to free and re-cue; use another cart. Report the cartridge for resplicing.
PA TEMP Reflected power above 260 W or PA temperature above 82 °C. Watch REFL and TEMP. If rising, TX LOWER in steps; report the antenna to Little Welford.
TX TRIP Transmitter protection operated: overload held, or PA at 92 °C. TX LOWER to bring REFL under 240 W, let TEMP fall below 80 °C, then TX RESET.
OFF AIR The transmitter has dropped. You are off the air. Restore programme first, clear the cause, TX RESET, then TX RAISE to 4 kW. Log the outage.
PEAK (lamp) Modulation exceeding +1.5 dB on the bus. Ease the offending fader down; sustained peaks distort the transmission.

Faults and recovery

cart jam
You see: the deck readout holds STALLED, the START bar burns without tape moving, the hubs stand still, and CART JAM flashes. What to do: press CART STOP CUE, which frees the transport and re-cues the cartridge; the annunciator goes out when the deck reports READY. Select another cartridge and carry on. Hand the faulty cart to engineering for resplicing. The test-key version behaves identically and is cleared the same way.
network line loss
You see: NET FAIL flashing, LINE OK out, the network bar flat, and — if the line was your only source — the silence timer beginning to run. What to do: ACCEPT the alarm, turn NETWORK SELECTOR to STANDBY FEED and raise the NETWORK FADER; the standby feed carries the station until the main line is re-established. The main line returns of its own accord once the engineers have patched it; return the selector to MAIN LINE when LINE OK burns steadily again. If you do nothing the silence sensor will drop the transmitter thirty seconds after the last note.
transmitter overload
You see: REFLECTED climbing past 260 W, PA TEMP passing 82 °C, PA TEMP flashing, then FOLDBACK lit and FORWARD pulled back to about 1.6 kW. Ignored, the PA reaches 92 °C and the transmitter trips: TX TRIP and OFF AIR together. What to do: press TX LOWER in steps until REFLECTED reads under 240 W; when PA TEMP falls below 80 °C press TX RESET. Keep the carrier low until Little Welford reports the matcher drained — typically three minutes — then TX RAISE to 4 kW in quarter-kilowatt steps, watching REFL at each move.
