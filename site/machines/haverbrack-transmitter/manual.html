Overview

Transmitter No. 2 radiates the overseas programme on 31, 41, 49 or 61 metres at a carrier power of twelve kilowatts. Power is taken from the station mains through the MAIN BREAKER; the mercury-arc rectifiers deliver high tension at eleven thousand volts; a class-C power amplifier is tuned by hand into the selected aerial system through the open-wire feeders.

The transmitter asks three things of its operator. First, sequence: filaments and cooling water before high tension, never the reverse. Second, resonance: the tank is brought to tune by hand on the TANK TUNING CRANK, and an untuned tank will cook its valve. Third, vigilance: the cooling water carries away some eight kilowatts of anode heat, and a lost pump becomes a ruined anode in two minutes if it is not met with the standby.

An untuned tank, a sick feeder or a dead pump are all announced on the six-window annunciator at the head of the panel. Every alarm demands action; ignored alarms end in a trip, and a trip in daylight costs the Corporation its evening audience.

Controls

MAIN BREAKER
Knife switch, left bay. ON connects station auxiliaries; OFF kills everything downstream and drops high tension at once. AUX LIVE burns when it is made.
BLOWER SWITCH
Toggle. Starts the cabinet blower and the main cooling pump. Both must run before high tension may be asked for.
FILAMENT SWITCH
Toggle. Energises the valve heaters. Allow fifteen seconds for FILAMENT VOLTS to reach its green sector and the amber FIL READY jewel to light. High tension on cold filaments strips their thoria in moments.
STANDBY PUMP SWITCH
Toggle. Starts the emergency cooling pump. It comes up to full delivery in about six seconds and silences the WATER FLOW alarm.
HT START
Mushroom push under a hinged guard. Lift the guard to arm, press to energise the rectifiers. Refuses unless the breaker is in, water is flowing, filaments are ready, and no trip or arc-back stands.
HT TRIP RESET
Pushbutton. Clears a protective trip. Locked out after a thermal trip until the water falls below 52°C.
RECTIFIER RESTORE
Pushbutton. Resets a rectifier that has arc-backed. Dead until the HT has collapsed; after restoring, clear the trip and restart.
TANK TUNING CRANK
Flywheel with vernier drum, 0 to 100. Rotates the tank capacitor. Watch the P.A. ANODE AMMETER fall to its dip and the AERIAL AMMETER rise to its peak: that is resonance. Arrow keys turn it; hold SHIFT for coarse turns.
ANTENNA LOADING LEVER
Five-notch quadrant, 0 to 4. Selects tap changes on the coupler. Notch 2 suits Rhombic South; heavier loading drives more current into the aerial but loads the valve harder.
DRIVE CONTROL
Bakelite knob, 0 to 10. Sets oscillator drive to the power amplifier. Raise it last, watching both R.F. meters stay in their green sectors.
AERIAL SELECTOR
Rotary, four positions: DUMMY LOAD, DIP N, RHOM S, SPARE. Changing it moves the resonance point on the drum — retune afterwards. Taking it off DIP N or RHOM S isolates a faulty main feeder.
WAVECHANGE SELECTOR
Rotary, four bands: 31, 41, 49, 61 metres. Changes tank taps; always retune after changing waveband.
ALARM ACCEPT
Pushbutton. Stops the flashing of accepted alarms and the annunciator buzzer. An alarm that is acknowledged but not cured stays lit.
LAMPS TEST
Pushbutton. Hold down to light every window and jewel on the panel.
MASTER RESET
Pushbutton. Returns the transmitter to cold, clears all faults, alarms and trips. Used after maintenance, not as a cure in programme hours.
FAULT TEST SWITCHES
Maintenance tray, bottom right, behind the dashed border. Three buttons apply the fault conditions ARC-BACK, WATER SYS. and FEEDER for drill and demonstration. Not to be used in programme hours without the Engineer-in-Charge's authority.

Normal operation

  1. Make the MAIN BREAKER. The AUX LIVE jewel lights and the mains hum begins.
  2. Put the BLOWER SWITCH ON. The BLOWER and PUMP jewels light and the flow sight-glass fills.
  3. Put the FILAMENT SWITCH ON. Wait some fifteen seconds until FILAMENT VOLTS stands in its green sector and the amber FIL READY jewel burns.
  4. Select the aerial on the AERIAL SELECTOR (RHOM S for the South American service) and the waveband on the WAVECHANGE SELECTOR.
  5. Lift the guard and press HT START. H.T. KILOVOLTS rises into its green sector and the rectifier columns take their blue burn.
  6. Advance the DRIVE CONTROL to about 3 so the R.F. meters respond.
  7. Set the ANTENNA LOADING LEVER to notch 2.
  8. Turn the TANK TUNING CRANK, slowly, until P.A. ANODE AMPS shows its dip and AERIAL AMPS its peak. If the amps run away, you are tuning through too fast; back off and try again.
  9. Raise the DRIVE CONTROL until AERIAL AMPS sits between 8½ and 9½ amperes in the green sector, with P.A. ANODE AMPS below 3.
  10. Press ALARM ACCEPT to silence any standing announcement. When the ON AIR lamp burns steady, advise the continuity announcer that Haverbrack Second is radiating.

From cold to carrier takes forty seconds when it is done without fumbling. Water temperature settles near 45°C; anything climbing through 60 is a matter for Section 5 below, not for watching.

Alarms

Window Meaning Action
ARC BACK A rectifier column has struck back; high tension has collapsed. Restore the rectifier, reset the trip, restart. See Fault 1.
WATER FLOW LOW The main cooling pump has failed or is failing. Start the STANDBY PUMP SWITCH at once. See Fault 2.
WATER TEMP HIGH Anode cooling water above 60°C and climbing. Restore flow. At 68°C the transmitter trips itself and locks out until the water falls below 52°C.
PA OVERLOAD Anode current beyond rating — usually an untuned tank or a failed feeder. Retune; check the feeder. Reset the trip before restarting.
FEEDER FAULT Reflected power rising on a main feeder. Transfer the AERIAL SELECTOR to SPARE and retune. See Fault 3.
HT LOCKED OUT The thermal lockout is holding the transmitter off after a water-temperature trip. Cool the water below 52°C, then press HT TRIP RESET.

New alarms flash and sound the buzzer; ALARM ACCEPT steadies them. An alarm that is acknowledged but not cured remains lit, and the condition behind it continues — a steady window is a fact, not a formality.

Faults and recovery

Fault 1 — Rectifier arc-back

You see: the rectifier glow dies, ARC BACK flashes, the TRIPPED flag drops, H.T. KILOVOLTS falls to zero, and HT START refuses.

To recover: press RECTIFIER RESTORE once — the window goes dark when the column has resettled. Press HT TRIP RESET. Lift the guard and press HT START again. The tank holds its tune throughout; no retuning is needed.

Fault 2 — Cooling water flow loss

You see: the flow sight-glass empties, WATER FLOW LOW flashes, and the water thermometer begins its long climb towards the red sector. Ignore it and the transmitter trips at 68°C and locks itself out.

To recover: put the STANDBY PUMP SWITCH ON (the breaker must be in). The standby comes up in six seconds, the alarm clears and the thermometer falls. If the transmitter has already tripped, wait until the water reads below 52°C, press HT TRIP RESET, and restart high tension. Tag the main pump for the millwrights; a MASTER RESET puts matters right only after they have attended.

Fault 3 — Main feeder fault

You see: REFLECTED WATTS climbing into its red sector, AERIAL AMPS falling away, P.A. ANODE AMPS rising, FEEDER FAULT flashing. Ignored, this becomes a PA OVERLOAD trip.

To recover: turn the AERIAL SELECTOR off DIP N / RHOM S to SPARE — this isolates the faulty feeder and extinguishes the alarm. The spare tunes elsewhere on the drum: bring the TANK TUNING CRANK round until the aerial ammeter peaks again, then trim the DRIVE CONTROL back into the green. Report the feeder to the line party; a MASTER RESET restores the selector scheme after repair.

Specifications

Electrical
Frequency coverage 9.7 / 7.3 / 6.1 / 4.9 Mc/s (31, 41, 49, 61 m)
Carrier power to aerial 12 kW nominal
High tension 11,000 V from twin mercury-arc columns
P.A. anode current, rated 2.2 A d.c.; overload trip at 5.2 A
Aerial current, working 8½–9½ A r.f. (green sector)
Reflected power limit 300 W
Valve filaments 33 V, thoriated tungsten, 15 s warming time
Modulation high-level, up to 96 % on speech peaks
Cooling
District of distilled water 45 litres per minute through both anodes
Inlet temperature 17.5°C nominal
Normal running about 45°C
Alarm / thermal trip 60°C / 68°C; lockout released below 52°C
Mechanical
Panel polished Vitrolite in mild-steel angle frame
Tuning scale vernier drum 0–100, reduction 36 : 1
