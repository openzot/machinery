OVERVIEW

The Meridian H.86 is a four-engined long-range monoplane of conventional metal construction, powered by four HALCYON KESTREL-9 nine-cylinder air-cooled radials driving constant-speed propellers. The second officer's station stands behind the pilots' seats and carries the engine instruments, the fuel system controls, the central warning cluster, and the power quadrant. The first officer flies the aeroplane; you make it possible. Your business is the balance of power against temperature: every pound of boost you demand must be paid for in cooling air, and the cowl flaps are how you pay.

This manual covers the station as fitted at the works for training use. All limits quoted are AIR REGISTRATION BOARD approved values for the Kestrel-9 at 100/130 grade fuel. Where this manual and the flight crew's copy disagree, the flight crew's copy rules the sky; report the discrepancy to your station engineer.

NORMAL OPERATION

From cold to cruising at FL180 west of Shannon. Allow twenty minutes of fuel for the climb and keep the boost gauges matched within two inches throughout.

  1. LAMPS TEST: press and confirm every window, jewel and drum lights. Release; all must go dark. ANNUNCIATOR ACCEPT if the caution holds.
  2. MASTER SWITCH ON. The electrics come alive; the phase line reads ENGINES COLD.
  3. FUEL PUMP SWITCHES: all four ON. Confirm FUEL PRESS strips stand near 17 psi.
  4. COWL FLAPS: set PORT and STBD to about one quarter open (25%) for ground running.
  5. Stiffen the quadrant FRICTION collar. Crack throttle No.1 a shade (about 6°). STARTER SELECTOR to 1; crank until she fires — watch R.P.M. rise through idle — then selector back to OFF. Repeat for Nos. 2, 3 and 4.
  6. Warm up at 800–1,000 R.P.M. Watch OIL T rise past 40°C before you ask for serious power. Expect OIL P near 60–80 psi on each engine.
  7. Run-up check per engine: throttle to 30°, ENGINE ANALYSER across each probe in turn; no cylinder-head should lead its neighbours by more than 20°C.
  8. Take-off set: PROPELLER GOVERNOR to 2,400, COWL FLAPS fully OPEN, throttles smoothly to 44 inHg or less. Climb away at 120–130 kt.
  9. In the climb keep CYL HEAD below 232°C by easing the COWL FLAPS as required. Above 5,000 ft the pumps are already on — leave them so.
  10. Level at cruise: reduce boost to hold 1,900 R.P.M. governed, close the COWL FLAPS to the minimum that keeps heads under 200°C, and trim the tanks even with INTER crossfeed if the IMBALANCE window shows.

FAULTS AND RECOVERY

The three training faults are injected from the FAULT TEST SWITCHES (or by the instructor). Each proves its own warning window within seconds. Clearing a fault is a two-part act: fly the machine out of danger first, then obtain engineering clearance on the REPAIR ACTIONS row.

NUMBER THREE CYLINDER-HEAD OVERHEAT

You see: CYL HEAD HOT; the No.3 head climbing through the red arc whatever the power; the analyser confirms on probe 3.
Do: COWL FLAP STBD fully open, throttle No.3 towards zero, and keep speed up. When the analyser reads below 200°C on probe 3, press REPAIR BAFFLE — the baffle is made good and the fault clears.
If ignored: above 262°C the charge detonates; within half a minute No.3 lets go for good (ENGINE OUT). Only SECURE RESET brings her back.

NUMBER TWO OIL-PRESSURE LOSS

You see: OIL PRESS LOW; No.2's OIL P strip falling towards zero while the engine still turns.
Do: break the guard and press FEATHER BUTTON No.2 within forty-five seconds of the pressure collapse. With her feathered and stopped, press RESTORE OIL — the line is renewed and the fault clears. She may be restarted normally.
If ignored: forty-five seconds of turning without oil and the big-end seizes (BEARING SEIZURE). That is the end of No.2 until the aircraft is secured and reset.

NUMBER FOUR BOOST-PUMP FAILURE

You see: FUEL PRESS LOW; No.4's FUEL P strip sagging, worse the higher you climb.
Do: CROSSFEED SELECTOR to X-FEED so No.4 drinks from Tank 2's healthy pump; the strip recovers. Then press CHANGE PUMP and restore NORMAL when convenient.
If ignored: eight seconds below the vapour threshold and No.4 flames out (ENGINE OUT). Gravity feed will hold her below 5,000 ft; above that she cannot survive without crossfeed.
