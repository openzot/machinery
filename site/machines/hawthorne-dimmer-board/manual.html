Operating Manual

HAWTHORNE GRAND THEATRE — STAGE LIGHTING CONTROL — DIMMER BOARD No. 1 PUBLICATION P.V. 1948/117 · ISSUE 2 · TO BE KEPT IN THE COVER AT THE RIGHT-HAND END OF THE BOARD

Overview

This board controls the six tungsten lighting circuits of the Hawthorne Grand stage through two cast-iron resistance-dimmer cages, mounted behind the board and ventilated through the louvres above. Each circuit is worked by a cranked handle travelling along its copper arc from OFF (0) to FULL (10); the GRANDMASTER lever beneath the arcs scales every circuit at once and is the handle you will hold during the show. The proscenium mimic at the right of the board shows the stage as the audience sees it — battens left and right, the cyc, the tabs, the footlights and the front of the gods — so that a dark bulb is seen before an usher reports it.

Resistance dimmers waste the voltage they take from the lamps as heat, and they waste most when set near the middle of their travel. The cage thermometers therefore read highest on a quiet scene, not a bright one. This is expected. What is not expected is written in the ALARMS section, and it is expected that you will read it before the first performance.

Controls

Control Type Positions and action
SUPPLY SELECTOR Rotary switch OFF · MAINS · AUX PLANT. MAINS is the corporation supply at 240 volts. AUX PLANT feeds the board from the stage-door generator at reduced voltage — scenery will light roughly one shade dimmer, and the voltmeter will sit near 205.
MAINS BREAKER Guarded lever OPEN / CLOSE, 80 amperes. The copper guard lifts to the hand. If the board has tripped on overload, the first operation of the lever resets the latch and it must be operated again to close.
GRANDMASTER Counterweighted lever, full width Master level 0–10 for every circuit. Left hand during the show; dips are made here, never by walking the six arcs.
DIMMER 1 Cranked handle on arc FRONT BATTEN LEFT, 0–10.
DIMMER 2 Cranked handle on arc FRONT BATTEN RIGHT, 0–10.
DIMMER 3 Cranked handle on arc CYC (sky cloth), 0–10.
DIMMER 4 Cranked handle on arc TABS (curtain warmer), 0–10.
DIMMER 5 Cranked handle on arc FOOTLIGHTS, 0–10.
DIMMER 6 Cranked handle on arc THE GODS (balcony front), 0–10.
LAMPS TEST Pushbutton Proves every indicator filament on the board — annunciator jewels and circuit pilots — for about two seconds.
ACKNOWLEDGE Pushbutton Stops the fresh-alarm blinking once the condition has been seen. It does not clear the fault and does not excuse it.
THERMAL RESET Pushbutton Restores a cage that has tripped at 150 °C. Refuses while either thermometer stands above 95 °C.
SPARE FEED Two-way lever NORM / SPARE. Transfers circuit 4 (the tabs) onto the spare way when its filament has failed.
WORKING LIGHT Toggle switch The electrician's desk lamp over the plotting shelf.
FAULT TEST SWITCHES Three switches under the flap Maintenance proof of the protective gear: SCENERY ON CAGE B, SCENE-DOCK KETTLES, TABS FILAMENT OPEN. All OFF for running; see FAULTS AND RECOVERY.

Normal operation

  1. Switch the WORKING LIGHT on. Read this list no faster than the board.
  2. Turn the SUPPLY SELECTOR from OFF to MAINS. The knob points at the legend you are choosing.
  3. Lift the guard and close the MAINS BREAKER. The MAINS jewel lights, the VOLTS meter stands between 230 and 240, and both house wall lamps on the mimic come up — these are non-dim and follow the breaker only.
  4. Press LAMPS TEST and satisfy yourself that every jewel and pilot answers.
  5. Preset the six arcs to the opening plot: DIMMER 1 and DIMMER 2 to about 7, DIMMER 3 to 4, DIMMER 4 to 3, DIMMER 5 to 5, DIMMER 6 to 6.
  6. Raise the GRANDMASTER steadily to 10 and watch the AMBERES meter: the needle must stand short of the red arc at all times. If she climbs towards it, lower a batten arc before anything else.
  7. Watch the BANK A and BANK B thermometers settle. A running show sits anywhere between 40 and 95 °C. Above 120 °C the OVERHEAT annunciator blinks — act on it, do not admire it.
  8. During the performance work the GRANDMASTER only. Cues calling for individual circuits are rehearsed and set before the half, never walked cold in the dark.
  9. At the interval bring the GRANDMASTER to 0, then run the house lights from the foyer switchboard as usual.
  10. End of night: GRANDMASTER to 0, all six arcs to 0, open the MAINS BREAKER, return the SUPPLY SELECTOR to OFF, WORKING LIGHT off. The board is then cold.

Alarms

Indicator Meaning Action
MAINS jewel (ivory) Board live, breaker closed, supply healthy. None. Dark when the breaker is open or the selector is OFF.
OVERLOAD jewel (red) Board current has passed 80 amperes or is closing on it. Lower the GRANDMASTER or the heaviest arc at once. Ignored, the breaker trips itself and the stage goes dark — see FAULTS AND RECOVERY.
OVERHEAT jewel (amber) A cage thermometer stands above 120 °C, or a cage has already tripped at 150 °C. Take load off the affected bank (its three arcs down), find what is lying against the cage, let the iron cool, THERMAL RESET.
CIRCUIT jewel (amber) A filament has gone open — the circuit's pilot shows a red ring and the mimic shows the dark spot. Throw SPARE FEED to SPARE and carry on; re-lamp at the half.

A new alarm blinks until ACKNOWLEDGE is pressed. Blinking means unseen; steady means known; dark means well. LAMPS TEST proves the messenger, never the message.

Faults and recovery

Dimmer bank overheat

You see: the OVERHEAT jewel blinking, BANK B thermometer climbing through 120 °C even though the arcs have hardly moved; the smell of hot varnish from the louvres. Cause: the flymen have lashed folded scenery against the cage B casing and the warm air cannot leave.

  1. Bring DIMMER 4, 5 and 6 to 0 (bank B carries the right-hand circuits).
  2. Send someone to clear the scenery from the louvres.
  3. When BANK B reads below 95 °C, press THERMAL RESET if the cage has tripped, and bring the arcs back one at a time, watching the thermometer.
  4. If the bank tripped unattended at 150 °C the tabs, footlights and gods went out together — expect the stage manager's knock, and expect to explain it at the Monday call.
  5. To prove the protection without the scenery, open the maintenance flap and hold the SCENERY ON CAGE B test switch on; the same sequence runs itself. Switch OFF afterwards.

Mains overload trip

You see: the OVERLOAD jewel blinking and the AMBERES needle over the red arc; within half a minute the MAINS BREAKER falls to OPEN on its own, the MAINS jewel dies and the whole stage goes dark. Cause on this board's test rig: the scene dock's kettles plugged into a spur off the lighting board — ninety-six amperes of tea.

  1. Strike the kettles from the spur (test switch to OFF).
  2. Operate the MAINS BREAKER once: this resets the trip latch. Operate it again to close.
  3. Bring the GRANDMASTER back up gently and watch the AMBERES needle as she rises.

Stage circuit failure

You see: the CIRCUIT jewel blinking, pilot 4 wearing a red ring, and the tabs dead black on the mimic however far DIMMER 4 is raised. Cause: the filament of the tabs circuit has failed — more likely still, its flexible has parted inside the tab.

  1. Set DIMMER 4 to 0.
  2. Throw SPARE FEED from NORM to SPARE.
  3. Raise DIMMER 4 again; the circuit returns through the spare way, marginally dimmer on the voltmeter, and the CIRCUIT jewel goes out.
  4. Re-lamp the tabs at the interval, return SPARE FEED to NORM, and file the fault card. To prove the alarm from the board, use the TABS FILAMENT OPEN test switch under the maintenance flap.

Specifications

Supply 240 v, 50~, single phase, corporation mains; AUX PLANT position for the stage-door generator (approx. 210 v on load)
Main breaker 80 A thermal pattern; trips after sustained overload
Circuits 6 × 2 kW tungsten at 240 v — battens L/R, cyc, tabs, footlights, gods; house wall lamps non-dim
Dimmers Resistance pattern, cast-iron grid cages, banks A (circuits 1–3) and B (circuits 4–6); greatest heat at mid travel
Cage temperatures Normal running up to 95 °C · alarm at 120 °C · thermal trip at 150 °C · restore below 95 °C only
Indication Tungsten jewel annunciator (MAINS, OVERLOAD, OVERHEAT, CIRCUIT), six circuit pilots, moving-coil VOLTS and AMBERES instruments, liquid cage thermometers
Mimic Proscenium elevation: battens, cyc, tabs, footlights, gods and non-dim house lamps, worked live from the circuits
Builders Peel & Vanner Ltd., Hackney N.E.; works number 1948-HG-117; any repair beyond fuses and lamps to be referred
