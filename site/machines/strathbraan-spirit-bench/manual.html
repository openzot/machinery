Operating Manual No. 7

SPIRIT STILL No. 2 — CUTTING BENCH

FORBES & METHVEN, DUNDEE WORKS No. 2194 · 1968
← Still house bench All machines →

Overview

This bench drives the second distillation of every gallon this house makes. A charge of low wines is pumped into Still No. 2, fired on a steam coil, and the vapour is led along the lyne arm into the worm condenser, which stands in cold spring water in its oak tub. The runnings leave the worm, pass through the sealed brass spirit safe — where the excise man's hydrometer floats in its parrot glass — and are routed by the receiving tap into one of three vessels: foreshots, spirit, or feints.

Your work is the cut. Fire and water must be kept in step: too little water and good spirit blows off the worm hot; too much steam and the wash primes over into the receivers. Watch the vapour head, watch the water out of the tub, and above all watch the parrot. The safe is sealed by HM Excise: if that seal is broken, collection stops until it is remade and entered in the loss book.

Controls

CHARGE COCK
Quarter-turn lever, positions SHUT and OPEN. Pumps low wines from the charge tank into the still at about seventy litres a second. The cock closes itself when the charge stands at 3,200 litres. It will not open against a still that is already running.
STEAM REGULATOR
Spoked handwheel, 0 to 10 turns. Six turns brings a full charge to the boil in about four minutes and carries a steady spirit run. Beyond seven the head climbs and the wash will prime. The safety slam shuts this valve if the worm runs dangerously hot or the head runs away.
CONDENSER WATER VALVE
Handwheel, 0 to 10 turns, feeding the worm tub from the burn. Five turns gives roughly ten litres a minute. Keep the water out of the tub below 27 °C; above that the worm loses condensing duty and raw vapour goes up the vent.
STANDBY WATER COCK
Guarded lever beneath a red lid, marked LIFT GUARD. Feeds the tub from the standby tank if the burn supply fails. Lift the lid, throw the lever, and the supply restores itself inside a few seconds.
SAFE TAP
Four-position rotary at the foot of the spirit safe: SHUT, FORE, SPIRIT, FEINT. Routes the runnings to the matching vessel. The cut is made here — nowhere else.
RESEAL SAFE
Brass push with a ruby centre. With the SAFE TAP at SHUT, press and hold a full two seconds to remake the excise seal after a breakage. Enter it in the loss book regardless.
ALARM ACCEPT
Pushbutton. Steadies the flashing annunciator window and silences the horn. It accepts the message; it does not cure the fault.
LAMPS TEST
Pushbutton. Lights every annunciator window, the safe pilot and the test lamp for two seconds so you know a dark lamp means good news and not a dead bulb.
RUN RESET
Red-collared pushbutton. Returns the whole bench to cold and alarm-free — charge returned to tank, receivers emptied to the feints charger, trips and faults cleared — without reloading the page. Use between runs.
FAULT TEST SWITCHES
Works-test panel, right of the office column. Selector for NONE, WATER, PRIME, SEAL, and an INJECT button. These put the named fault onto the bench exactly as nature would. Not to be used during a lawful run — the excise officer reads this paragraph too.
SOUND CUT
Bat-handled switch. Brings up or silences the plant hum, the worm trickle and the horn. Sound starts only after you touch something; this switch is the master.

Normal operation

  1. Press RUN RESET and satisfy yourself the bench is cold: charge tank full, still empty, no windows lit, both wheels at zero, SAFE TAP at SHUT.
  2. Throw the CHARGE COCK to OPEN and pump the charge. At 3,200 litres the cock closes itself and the sight glass on the tank shows what is left for the next round.
  3. Open the CONDENSER WATER VALVE five turns and confirm the tub is running — the inlet arrows on the board should be alive before there is anything to condense.
  4. Ease the STEAM REGULATOR up two turns at a time. The pot thermometer in the manway climbs first; the vapour head follows as she comes to the boil.
  5. At six turns the head steadies around 78–80 °C and the first runnings drip through the safe. Set the SAFE TAP to FORE and collect the foreshots — the heads go to their own ninety-litre vessel, never to the spirit still's charge.
  6. When the runnings run clean and the parrot holds near eighty per cent, turn the SAFE TAP to SPIRIT and start filling the spirit receiver.
  7. Ride the run. Keep the head under 86 °C by trimming the steam wheel, keep the water out under 27 °C with the water wheel, and keep an eye on the circular chart: a rising pen late in the run is the charge telling you the spirit is nearly gone.
  8. The moment the parrot falls to 63 per cent, cut to FEINTS. Dither and the weak runnings foul the gyle — the batch is marked off-grade and goes back with the feints whatever you do next.
  9. When the run finishes, close the STEAM REGULATOR, let the worm drain, return the SAFE TAP to SHUT, and accept any outstanding windows.
  10. Record your litres and proofs in the ledger. Press RUN RESET and the bench stands ready for the next charge.

Alarms

Window Meaning Action
CONDENSER HOT Water out of the tub above 31 °C — flow short or supply failing. Spirit is leaving by the vent. Open the CONDENSER WATER VALVE; if the burn supply has failed, lift the guard and throw the STANDBY WATER COCK. Ignored, the safety slams the REGULATOR TRIP at 38 °C and the run is done.
VAPOUR HIGH Head above 86 °C: firing too hard, or the charge is spent and water is coming over. Ease the STEAM REGULATOR. Above 95 °C held, the safety trips the regulator on its own.
PRIMING Wash frothing over the swan neck into the runnings. The charge is foul or she is driven too hard. Throttle the STEAM REGULATOR below two turns and let her settle. The window drops out when the charge settles with it. Carry-over already in the spirit receiver puts the gyle off-grade.
PARROT BELOW CUT Strength at the safe has fallen under 63 per cent while tapping SPIRIT. Cut to FEINTS at once. Twenty-five seconds of dither marks the gyle off-grade permanently.
RECEIVER FULL The vessel under the open tap is full; runnings going to waste. Change the SAFE TAP or shut it. Every litre overflows to the drains and the revenue man counts it against you.
SEAL BROKEN The excise seal on the spirit safe is broken; the safe is open to the air and spirit is evaporating. Bring the SAFE TAP to SHUT, then press and hold RESEAL SAFE a full two seconds. Enter the loss in the book.
REGULATOR TRIP Safety has slammed the steam valve: worm dangerously hot or head runaway. Find why — usually the condenser water. Cure the cause, then RUN RESET and begin the run again. The trip does not release itself.

Accept a window to steady it and silence the horn; the lamp stays lit while the condition lasts. A window that comes straight back after you accept it deserves respect, not another jab of the finger.

Faults and recovery

condenser water loss

What you see. The inlet arrows die, the flow gauge sags towards nothing, the water-out needle climbs past 31 °C and CONDENSER HOT lights. Vapour plumes rise from the vent stack and the parrot begins to sag as uncondensed spirit escapes.

Recovery. Lift the guard and throw the STANDBY WATER COCK; the supply builds back within seconds. Verify the water-out needle falls below 27 °C and the vent stops breathing. If the run has already tripped, cure the water first, then RUN RESET.

priming carry-over

What you see. PRIMING lights within moments. Once the charge is hot the head grows unsteady and the cloudiness creeps up — wash carried bodily into the worm and the receivers.

Recovery. Take the STEAM REGULATOR below two turns and let the bench settle; the window drops out when the charge settles. Bring her back gently — a primed still does it twice as easy the second time. Spirit already fouled is off-grade: cut to FEINTS and charge the loss.

excise seal broken

What you see. SEAL BROKEN lights immediately, the wax seal swings broken on its ribbons, and the evaporation loss mounts whether or not anything is running.

Recovery. Put the SAFE TAP to SHUT — the law and the mechanism agree here — then press and hold RESEAL SAFE for two full seconds. The seal remakes, the window drops, and you write the entry in the loss book for the excise officer to countersign.

Specifications

Charge, working 3,200 litres of low wines (tank stock 3,400 L)
Still Copper, direct steam coil, swan neck, 6,500 L nominal
Steam regulator 0–10 turns; boiler working pressure 40 psi, dial to 60 psi
Vapour head, normal 78–82 °C on the run; alarm at 86 °C; trip at 95 °C held
Condenser Copper worm in oak tub; 20 L/min at 10 turns; water out ≤ 27 °C
Safety trip Water out 38 °C held, or head 95 °C held — slams the regulator
Cut point Parrot 63 % alcohol by volume, temperature-corrected by eye
Receivers Foreshots 90 L · Spirit 1,100 L · Feints 1,400 L
Chart recorder Vapour head, one revolution per fifteen minutes
Bench Oak, brass and cream stove enamel; Forbes & Methven, Dundee, 1968
