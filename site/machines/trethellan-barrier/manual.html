Overview

Trethellan Sound Surge Barrier spans the narrows between Rosevennor Point and Carn Du. Six rising-sector gates, each twenty-four metres across, lie folded in the sill on every normal tide and let the estuary breathe. When the tide net telegraphs a surge, the gates are walked up out of the sill and hold the sea off the river. The barrier exists for perhaps forty hours a year; the other nine thousand hours it is watched by one person at this desk, and that person is you.

The desk does three things. It passes the tide: gates down, sluice shut, pumps standing. It closes the river: six gates raised in sequence before high water, never against more than three metres of differential, the sluice cracked whenever the upstream impoundment needs relief. And it tells you honestly when something is wrong: every red lamp on this panel has an entry in the Alarms section, and every fault that can put the barrier out of service has a recovery written here. The plant forgives patience and punishes stubbornness — close her early against a small head, and let the weir and the sluice do the waiting for you.

Controls

CONTROL SELECTOR
Three-position selector, stepped by clicking or with Enter: OFF (desk dead, drives and pumps inhibited), TEST (proves every annunciator lamp for two seconds), OPERATE (control power on; drives and pumps available). The current position prefixes the status chip.
GATE SELECTOR
Seven-position rotary: G1…G6 selects one gate for the drive lever, ALL couples the lever to the whole barrier. Click steps clockwise; arrow keys work it too.
GATE DRIVE LEVER
Spring-return lever, three positions: RAISE, HOLD (rest), and LOWER. It moves only the gate or gates the selector has given it, and springs back to HOLD the moment you let go. Hold ArrowUp / ArrowDown to work it from the keyboard.
SURGE CLOSE
Red pushbutton under an orange lifting guard. With the desk in OPERATE, lift the guard and press: the barrier raises itself gate by gate in overlapping pairs. The sequence is a polite subordinate — whenever the differential passes three metres it holds where it is until the sluice settles the levels.
OPEN ALL
Pushbutton. Runs the same sequence downward — gates fold away in overlapping pairs, holding whenever the differential forbids motion.
SLUICE BYPASS VALVE
Handwheel, five turns shut to open. Passes up to 460 m³/s through the culverts to equalise the head across the gates, or to relieve the impounded river while the barrier is closed. Turn anticlockwise to open; the counter reads the turns off the seat.
DUTY PUMP SWITCH
Starts and stops HPU 1, the duty hydraulic power unit. A tripped unit locks out; switching it off resets the lockout, switching it on restarts.
STANDBY PUMP SWITCH
Starts and stops HPU 2. Either unit alone holds full pressure; both together share a heavy sequence.
TIDE DRUM
Fifteen-day predictor roller under a fixed reading cursor. Wind it with a finger drag, the mouse wheel, or the arrow keys to bring any tide to the cursor; the red needle beside the cursor is the live gauge. The scribed line always reads now when the drum is centred — TO NOW recentres it.
ALARM ACCEPT
Steadies all flashing lamps and disarms the horn for the alarms showing. New events flash and sound again.
HORN SILENCE
Kills the alarm horn without touching the lamps. Use it once you have seen the cause.
LAMPS TEST
Lights every annunciator window while pressed. Dead lamp, no protection — test each shift.
TEST GATE SKEW
Fault-test toggle behind the FAULT TEST PANEL lid. Injects a sheared position pin on gate 4 exactly as the real failure presents. Used for training; the recovery is the real procedure.
TEST PUMP TRIP
Fault-test toggle. Trips the duty HPU breaker on lockout as an electrical protection operation would.
TEST DEBRIS STRIKE
Fault-test toggle. Arms a floating strike on gate 18 seconds ahead — enough time to be doing something else when it lands.
SKEW RESET
Re-seats the gate 4 position drive after a skew event. Refuses unless the differential is below 1.2 m and nothing is driving.
DEBRIS CLEAR
Held pushbutton — keep it down a full two seconds. Backs the gate off its obstruction and restores drive.
MAINTENANCE RESET
Black keyswitch. Returns the whole desk to cold: gates down, pumps off, selector OFF, every alarm and latch cleared. The only way back after an OVERTOPPING or SCOUR DAMAGE trip.
SOUND
Header pushbutton. Enables the desk audio — pump hum, detent clacks and the alarm horn — after your first click anywhere on the page.

Normal operation

  1. CONTROL SELECTOR to TEST. Every annunciator window proves for two seconds. Any lamp lit at rest is a fault — read it before going further.
  2. CONTROL SELECTOR to OPERATE. The status chip shows [OPERATE] BARRIER OPEN.
  3. DUTY PUMP SWITCH on. The accumulator needle comes up to about 180 bar inside half a minute. STANDBY stays off.
  4. Centre the TIDE DRUM on now (TO NOW), confirm the printed curve against the day, and read the time of the next high water. An amber SURGE AMENDMENT slip across the drum means the atmosphere disagrees with the paper — believe the slip.
  5. Confirm the standing state: all six gate bars at 0%, SLUICE BYPASS VALVE shut, differential needle near zero, chip BARRIER OPEN. You are on duty and passing the tide.
  6. On SURGE FORECAST SEVERE: accept the alarm, ease the SLUICE BYPASS VALVE two turns, leave GATE SELECTOR at ALL, lift the guard and press SURGE CLOSE. The gates walk up one after another; watch the differential needle and hold (HOLD, or let the sequence stand) if it nears the red.
  7. While closed, watch the upstream pane. If the river climbs through 6.8 m, open the sluice further — the weir at 6.0 m cannot carry a flood alone. HIGH WATER U/S at 7.2 m means the impoundment is winning; open the sluice full.
  8. When the surge has passed and the falling sea brings the differential under 3 m: open the sluice fully for a minute to settle the levels, then press OPEN ALL. Confirm every bar reads 0%.
  9. Shut the SLUICE BYPASS VALVE, silence whatever needs silencing, and write the closure in the log. Return to step 4 and stay awake — the second event of a pair is the one that catches the unwary.

Alarms

Lamp Means Do
SURGE FORECAST SEVERE AMBER A surge residual over 0.85 m — or a predicted level above 7.6 m — arrives within four minutes on the model run. Normal operation steps 6–7. Close before the sea makes the decision for you.
HIGH WATER UPSTREAM RED The impounded river has reached 7.2 m CD against the embankments. Sluice full open. If the barrier is closed and the sea has fallen, prepare to reopen.
DIFFERENTIAL HEAD HIGH RED More than 3 m across the gates while driving or while the automatic sequence stands held; 5 m standing parked. Stop driving. Equalise with the sluice before moving anything again.
HYD PRESSURE LOW RED Duty HPU locked out, or the accumulator below 120 bar with the desk on OPERATE. Faults and recovery, “hydraulic power unit trip”.
GATE 4 SKEW RED Gate 4’s position drive disagrees with its command; sheared pin suspected. Faults and recovery, “gate skew jam”.
DEBRIS IMPACT RED A floating object has taken gate 4; drive to that gate is cut. Faults and recovery, “floating debris strike”.
SCOUR DAMAGE RED The sill under the gates has been worked on by driving against head. The barrier is out of service. Nothing at the desk. Call the civil engineer, then MAINTENANCE RESET for the training rig.
OVERTOPPING RED The upstream embankment has been topped at 7.8 m CD. The barrier is out of service and the marsh behind you is drowning. As above. This is the lamp that ends careers; the procedure exists so you never meet it.

Ignoring HIGH WATER UPSTREAM, or leaving the gates open through a surge, ends the same way: the upstream level passes 7.8 m for twenty seconds and the barrier trips to OVERTOPPING. Only MAINTENANCE RESET returns the desk to cold afterwards.

Faults and recovery

Gate skew jam

You see: GATE 4 SKEW flashing; the SKEW flag on gate 4’s bar; gate 4 frozen wherever it was while the others answer; the skew angle creeping up whenever the lever insists.

  1. Stop driving gate 4 — lever to HOLD, or let an automatic sequence stand.
  2. Open the SLUICE BYPASS VALVE until the differential needle reads under 1.2 m.
  3. Press SKEW RESET. The pin re-seats, the flag drops, gate 4 answers again.
  4. Log it; gate 4 gets an engineering visit at the next spring low.

Hydraulic power unit trip

You see: HYD PRESSURE LOW; the accumulator needle sagging toward zero; gates slowing, then refusing; the DUTY HPU switch dark.

  1. Switch the DUTY PUMP SWITCH off — this resets the breaker lockout — then on again. Pressure rebuilds in about thirty seconds.
  2. If the movement will not wait, STANDBY PUMP SWITCH on carries the load alone while you sort the duty unit.
  3. If it trips twice, leave it tripped and run on standby. Log both events.

Floating debris strike

You see: DEBRIS IMPACT, eighteen seconds after the arming event; gate 4’s drive cuts and the bar freezes mid-travel.

  1. Let go of everything — the gate is already held.
  2. Press and hold DEBRIS CLEAR for a full two seconds. Drive is restored when the button lights its completion clack.
  3. Finish the movement slowly and eyeball the bay at the next low water.

Specifications

Gates 6 × rising-sector, 24.0 m span, folded in the sill
Gate travel sill to raised in 26 s at full flow; the automatic sequence works in overlapping pairs
Design differential ±8 m standing; 3 m limit while driving
Tide range MHWS +6.9 m CD, MLWS −1.9 m CD, mean level +2.5 m CD
Design surge residual +2.9 m on the predicted curve
Upstream basin 25 km² working surface; relief weir crest +6.0 m CD, 300 m³/s per metre
Levels HIGH WATER UPSTREAM +7.2 m CD; overtopping +7.8 m CD
Sluice bypass 460 m³/s at five turns; equalises a 3 m head in minutes
Hydraulics 2 × 315 kW HPUs, 180 bar nominal, 205 bar loaded maximum
Closure rule never drive against more than 3 m of differential
