ALBION PNEUMATIC DESPATCH CO. LTD · MANCHESTER

OPERATING INSTRUCTIONS

PNEUMATIC DESPATCH INSTALLATION · MARLOWE & DUNN LTD., KENSINGTON · CONTROL POSITION No. 1 BOOK MD/29 · SECOND EDITION, FEBRUARY 1929 · FOR THE USE OF THE CASH OFFICE

Overview

This installation carries charge-slips, requisitions and small parcels between the Cash Office and eight selling floors at up to twenty-five miles an hour, without a boy, a lift or a staircase. A felted carrier is seated in the breech at this position, the air is turned on, and the carrier rides the OUT main to the department chosen on the selector. The department keeps her as long as she is wanted, puts her on the RETURN, and the suction of the same exhauster brings her home to your breech with the change and the receipts.

The plant in the basement behind you is simple and it will serve you well if you serve it well: an exhauster-compressor set charges the RECEIVER; a REDUCING VALVE holds the MAIN at its working pressure; timing relays at the breech watch every flight and speak up through the annunciator when a carrier does not arrive when she should. There are eight carriers in circulation. None of them is lost — merely resting on a floor somewhere. Do not despatch against a falling main: a carrier starved of air stops in the tube, and a stopped carrier is a jammed carrier.

Controls

MOTOR STARTER
Three-position rotary: OFF, AUTO, RUN. At RUN the set charges without stopping; at AUTO the pressure switch cuts her in at 72 lb and out at 88 lb. Work her in AUTO. If the overload has tripped she will not turn until cooled and reset.
PRESSURE REGULATOR
Sets the working pressure of the MAIN, 12 to 34 pounds per square inch. Normal running is 26. The LO end vents the main — useful when a joint must be reseated, wasteful at any other time.
STATION SELECTOR
Eight-position drum: LACES, MILLINERY, FURS, BOOKS, SILKS, TOYS, CHEMIST, ENQUIRIES. Turn before you despatch; a carrier goes where the drum points, not where you meant it to point.
SLEEVE GATE
Guarded toggle at the breech. Lift the guard and flip to OPEN to seat or withdraw a carrier; close and seal her before every shot. The gate is interlocked in spirit if not in iron: despatching open blows the charge to the room.
DESPATCH LEVER
The long brass-handled lever in the quadrant: VENT, HOLD, DESPATCH. Pull to DESPATCH and let her spring back to HOLD. One stroke, one carrier.
RECEIVE DOOR
Releases carriers that have come IN from the floors from the receive box into the in-tray. The gong rings for every arrival.
REVERSE PURGE
Two-position rotary: SHUT, PURGE. Reverses the draught to draw a stalled carrier home. She vents the main while open — shut her as soon as she has done her work. Lever at HOLD and gate shut first.
ALARM ACCEPT
Stops the flashing and silences the buzzer once you have read the windows. It accepts nothing away: a window stays lit while its condition stands.
LAMPS TEST
Proves every jewel and window filament on the position. Use it at the start of each shift; a dark bulb you have not proved is not a good lamp, it is a bad surprise.
FAULT TEST SWITCHES
Guarded bank: JAM, GASKET, HEAT. Inserts each condition without waiting for heaven's own timing, for instruction and drill. They spring off at PLANT RESET. Never drill during the Saturday trade.
PLANT RESET
Clears latched trouble, frees a cooled overload and springs the test switches off. It starts no fire and closes no gate: the state of the iron is still the state of the iron.
SOUND CUT
Silences the gong and buzzer. The lamps keep their opinion. Cut the sound only when someone is listening with their eyes.

Normal operation

From cold and quiet to the first carrier away takes about two minutes. Follow the order; each step assumes the one before it.

  1. At the start of the shift, press LAMPS TEST. Every jewel and every annunciator window should light. Replace what does not.
  2. Turn the MOTOR STARTER to RUN. The receiver needle will climb; above 60 lb the set settles to a comfortable note.
  3. When the needle passes 80 lb, take the starter back to AUTO. The pressure switch now minds the receiver between 72 and 88 pounds.
  4. Set the PRESSURE REGULATOR to 26 — the mark amidships. The MAIN needle should settle within a pound or two of her in a minute.
  5. Confirm a carrier at the breech window. If the box holds arrivals, press the RECEIVE DOOR and seat her.
  6. Choose the department on the STATION SELECTOR — say, SILKS at 204 feet.
  7. Open the SLEEVE GATE, lay the carrier home in the sleeve, and shut and seal the gate.
  8. Pull the DESPATCH LEVER to DESPATCH and release it. You will hear her go, see her cross the diagram, and the department jewel lights when she arrives.
  9. When the department returns her, she rings in at the breech ready for the next floor. Carriers arriving FROM the floors drop into the receive box: press the RECEIVE DOOR to take them in.
  10. Going away: leave the starter in AUTO, the regulator at 26, the gate sealed, the purge SHUT, and the lever at HOLD.

Alarms

A new window flashes and the buzzer sounds; ALARM ACCEPT steadies the window and quiets the buzzer; the window goes out when its condition clears. Amber windows warn, red windows stop the show.

Window Colour Meaning Action
LINE PRESSURE LOW Amber The main stands below her working pressure while the plant should be holding her. Do not despatch. Check the starter is in AUTO, the regulator at 26, and listen for a hiss. See Faults.
CARRIER JAMMED IN LINE Red A carrier has stalled in the tube, or the test obstruction stands. Stop despatching. Recover by REVERSE PURGE — see Faults.
AIR MAIN LEAKING Red A joint gasket has blown and the main is falling faster than the reducing valve can feed her. Vent her cold and reseat — see Faults. Telephone for the fitter if she blows again.
EXHAUSTER OVERHEAT Red The head has passed 95°C and the overload has tripped the starter. Leave her to cool below 70°C, then PLANT RESET before restarting. See Faults.
RELIEF VALVE LIFTED Amber The receiver passed 92 lb — she is blowing to the room. Take the starter to AUTO or OFF. The valve seats herself below 86 lb. Persistent lifting means a lazy pressure switch: report it.

Faults and recovery

Carrier jammed in the line

You see: CARRIER JAMMED IN LINE, red and flashing; the diagram shows her stopped between stations; the gong never comes. Caused by despatching on a weak main, by a crushed tube at a floor penetration, or by the test switch.

To clear her:

  1. Leave the DESPATCH LEVER at HOLD and the SLEEVE GATE shut.
  2. Turn REVERSE PURGE to PURGE. The draught reverses and draws her home; a real carrier arrives at the breech in about twelve seconds, the test obstruction clears in six.
  3. Shut the PURGE the moment she is home — she vents the main while open.
  4. Restore the main to 26 lb, choose the station again, and despatch as usual.

If two purges fail to bring her home, stop, telephone the engineer, and put it in the book. Do not keep pulling the lever against a stuck carrier: you will flatten her felt and score the tube.

Air main gasket leak

You see: AIR MAIN LEAKING, red; a hiss at some joint on the run; the MAIN needle sagging though the set works; EXHAUSTER HEAD climbing as she labours. Despatches made meanwhile may stall — see the first fault.

To reseat the joint:

  1. Take the MOTOR STARTER to OFF.
  2. Take the PRESSURE REGULATOR to LO and open the REVERSE PURGE to help her down.
  3. Let the MAIN fall under 9 lb and hold her there five seconds: the gasket reseats cold, the hiss stops and the window goes out.
  4. Shut the PURGE, restore the REGULATOR to 26, restart in RUN and change to AUTO when charged.

If the window lights again within the hour, the joint wants copper and glue, not air: isolate at LO, tag the desk OUT OF SERVICE, telephone the fitter.

Exhauster overheat

You see: the EXHAUSTER HEAD thermometer climbing into the red, the TRIP flag showing, the set stopping dead with EXHAUSTER OVERHEAT lit, and the starter refusing AUTO and RUN. Usually worked into being: a leak left standing, purges forgotten open, or a slipped cooling fan belt.

To recover:

  1. Leave the starter alone; she is locked out for a reason.
  2. Attend to the cause: shut the purge, vent-and-reseat any leak as above.
  3. Watch the head fall below 70°C — ten to fifteen minutes, no less, whatever the floor wants.
  4. Press PLANT RESET to free the starter, return to RUN, then AUTO when charged.

Overheating twice in a morning is a machine complaint, not an operating one: report a slipped fan belt and give her an easy watch.

Specifications

Maker Albion Pneumatic Despatch Co. Ltd., Trafford Park, Manchester
Installation Marlowe & Dunn Ltd., Kensington High Street — Works No MD/36/0117
Main run Single OUT main and RETURN, 315 feet overall, 3-inch drawn brass
Stations Eight: LACES, MILLINERY, FURS, BOOKS, SILKS, TOYS, CHEMIST, ENQUIRIES
Carriers Eight, felted, 3¼ inches diameter; nominal load 2 pounds
Exhauster set 5 horsepower, 3-phase, 230/400 volts, 50 cycles per second, direct-on-line starter with overload release
Receiver 12 cubic feet; working 72–88 lb/in²; relief valve set 92 lb/in²
Main working pressure 26 lb/in² nominal; adjustable 12–34 lb/in² at the reducing valve
Carrier speed Up to 25 miles per hour on the level; terminal arrester at every station
Transit time About 9 seconds to ENQUIRIES (315 feet) at working pressure
Overload trip At 95°C head temperature; releases below 70°C and PLANT RESET
Gasket reseat Cold reseat below 9 lb/in² held five seconds
Tested Hydraulic test of main, January 1929; witnessed for the store by W. H. Cadwallader, engineer's office
